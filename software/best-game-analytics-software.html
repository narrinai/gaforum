What is game analytics software?

Game analytics software collects events from a game client or server, such as sessions, level progress and purchases, and turns them into retention curves, revenue per daily active user, funnels and cohort comparisons. Some products are built only for games, some are general product analytics with SDKs that studios adapt, and some cover neighboring jobs such as attribution or market data.

How we ranked these

A studio shipping its first live game usually asks the same thing: which tool will show us where players quit, what they pay for and whether last week's patch helped, without a data engineer on staff? This ranking compares twelve products against that question.

We read each vendor's own homepage and pricing pages, noted which engines and platforms the vendor names, checked whether a free allowance exists and whether prices are printed, and then weighed fit for small and mid-size studios. We did not run any of these tools or measure their numbers.

Where a page showed no prices or a figure could not be read, we say so instead of guessing. The order reflects how directly each product answers the retention, ARPDAU and funnel questions, and how little setup it asks for before the first useful chart.

Game-native metrics out of the box

Retention by install cohort, revenue per daily active user and level funnels are the reports a live game team opens every morning. A game-built product ships them as defaults, while a general product analytics tool expects you to model them from raw events. When reading each vendor page we looked for game-specific language, named game platforms and engine support, because that signals whether the dashboards arrive ready or need a week of event design first.

SDK coverage for your engine

An analytics tool is only as easy as its integration. Unity and Unreal teams want a drop-in SDK, Roblox creators need something that runs on that platform, and console or PC projects may need server-side event intake. We recorded the platforms each vendor names on its own pages.

Where a page names none, we say so, and you should ask the vendor before assuming support for your engine.

Free allowance and pricing visibility

Analytics bills grow with events, sessions or tracked conversions, so a game with a large casual audience can surprise its owner. We noted which vendors print a free monthly allowance, which publish a price point, and which sell through a demo request. A published allowance lets a small team estimate cost before integrating; a hidden price means a sales call comes first.

Experiments, configs and live operations

Seeing a problem is half the job; the other half is changing something safely. A/B testing, remote configuration and feature flags let a team adjust difficulty, offers or store layout without shipping a new build. We weighed whether each product pairs measurement with those controls, or stays a read-only reporting layer that needs a second tool beside it.

What this list shows

The list splits into three groups. Four products are analytics first (GameAnalytics, Amplitude, Mixpanel, PostHog), four are wider backend or live service suites that include analytics among other services (Unity Gaming Services, Firebase, PlayFab, Metaplay), and four solve adjacent problems: Game Refinery sells market intelligence, Solsten player psychology, and AppsFlyer and Tenjin attribution. Treating all twelve as interchangeable is the most common buying mistake, because an attribution platform will not show you where players leave level four.

Cost transparency is uneven. Amplitude, Mixpanel and PostHog print free monthly allowances (2 million events, 1 million events and 1 million source rows respectively), and AppsFlyer and Tenjin print price points ($0.07 per conversion after a free first-year package, and plans from $200 per month). GameAnalytics, Solsten, Metaplay's paid tier and Unity's live services page show no figures on the pages we opened.

None of the twelve vendor pages we read states a European headquarters, so studios with data residency requirements should ask each vendor directly.

How to choose

1. Decide whether you need game metrics or product metrics

Write down the five questions your producer asks every week. If they are retention by cohort, spend per player and level drop-off, start with a game-built product. If they are broader (sign-up flows, feature adoption, subscriptions across a launcher and a website), a product analytics tool may cover more of the business, at the cost of modeling game events yourself.

2. Check your engine and platform list against the vendor pages

List every platform you ship on, including Roblox, console or WebGL if relevant, and compare it with the platforms each vendor names. Where the page is silent, email the vendor and ask for an SDK link for your engine version. Do this before looking at price, because an unsupported platform makes the cheapest plan irrelevant.

3. Estimate your monthly event volume before you compare plans

Multiply daily active players by the events your design will send per session, then by thirty. Compare that figure with each free allowance. Studios often discover that a rich event schema costs more than the headline plan suggests.

If the price is quote-only, send the same estimate with your request so the answer is specific.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best game analytics software in 2026?

GameAnalytics is the first pick for most small and mid-size studios because it is built for games and names mobile, Roblox, VR/AR and PC/console support. Unity Gaming Services is better if your game already runs on Unity and you want analytics beside cloud code and A/B tests. Amplitude suits teams that want a documented 2 million event free plan and general funnel tooling.

Is there a free game analytics tool for a small studio?

Yes, several. Amplitude, Mixpanel and PostHog all print free monthly allowances, PlayFab offers a free-to-start tier with a limited Development Mode, Metaplay lets you build and run a game locally for free, and AppsFlyer has a free Zero plan. GameAnalytics runs a startup pricing program for VC-backed studios, but we could not read its prices.

Should a game studio use Mixpanel or Amplitude?

Both are product analytics platforms with free plans, session replay and experiments, so the choice is mostly about volume and fit. Amplitude states 2 million free events per month and Mixpanel 1 million. Run a trial with your own event schema, because neither vendor page we read describes a game-specific dashboard set.

Can I use Firebase for game analytics?

Firebase lists SDKs for Unity and C++ alongside Apple platforms, Android, Web and Flutter, and its product list includes Crashlytics and Remote Config. The homepage we read mentions a $300 billing credit but no analytics reports or plan figures, so confirm the exact analytics features on Google's documentation before committing.

What is the difference between game analytics and mobile attribution?

Game analytics measures what players do inside the game: sessions, progression, purchases. Attribution measures where installs came from and what each acquisition channel returned. AppsFlyer and Tenjin sit in the second group.

Most mobile studios eventually run one tool of each kind and connect the revenue data between them.

Do I need a backend platform to get game analytics?

No. GameAnalytics, Amplitude, Mixpanel and PostHog collect events without hosting your game servers. Backend suites such as PlayFab, Metaplay and Unity Gaming Services are worth considering when you also need player accounts, economy or live operations, because the analytics then sits beside the data it explains.

Which tools here are not really analytics products?

Game Refinery is a market intelligence dataset about other mobile games, not a tracker for your own players. Solsten is a consumer insights platform with a games user research solution. Both inform design decisions, but neither replaces an event pipeline that reports your own retention and revenue.
