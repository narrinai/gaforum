What are multistreaming platforms?

A multistreaming platform takes one live video signal, from a camera, an encoder or a browser studio, and relays it to several destinations at the same moment, such as YouTube, Facebook, Twitch, LinkedIn or a custom RTMP address. It saves running separate encoders and often adds chat merging, scheduling and recording.

How we ranked these

A product launch, a studio showcase or a tournament final rarely lives on one platform. The audience is split across YouTube, LinkedIn, Twitch and the company website, and sending a separate stream to each strains upload bandwidth and splits the crew's attention.

This ranking looks at twelve tools that solve that fan-out problem in different ways: cloud relays, browser studios, desktop mixers and a phone contribution app. We read each vendor's own pages: pricing, plan limits, supported ingest methods, platform lists and company details.

We did not run streams ourselves, and we mark every vendor claim as the vendor's. Order reflects how clearly a tool documents what it does and what it costs, how many ways it can take a signal in, and how well it serves a business that streams to several destinations rather than a hobbyist.

What can feed the relay

A relay is only useful if it accepts the signal you already produce. OneStream Live lists RTMP, RTMPS, SRT and WHIP, Livepush lists RTMP, SRT, pull URLs and a browser camera, and Lightstream works entirely in a browser. A crew with an encoder PC wants protocol support; a marketing team with a laptop wants a browser studio.

Check ingest first, because a mismatch forces a rebuild of the production chain on launch day.

How many destinations, at what quality

Plans differ on the number of simultaneous channels and the resolution allowed. Streamster ties channels to price (2 on free, 10 on the top plan), Lightstream states up to five, OneStream Live starts with two on the free tier and cites 45+ integrations, and Livepush cites 40+. Count the destinations you will actually use in the next year and compare against the limit on the plan you would buy, including the resolution cap.

Cost structure and commitment

Monthly subscriptions, annual plans and pay-per-hour billing suit different calendars. Castr starts at $16.67 per month, Livepush at $13 per month billed yearly, and Streamster offers hourly use from $0.48. A company streaming four times a year should compare hourly billing with a month-to-month plan it can cancel, and note which pages leave the currency unnamed.

Beyond the relay: recording, hosting and scheduling

Some tools stop at the fan-out; others add recording, a branded player, 24/7 playlists or VOD. Castr hosts and delivers, Livepush loops pre-recorded shows, and Riverside records separate guest tracks. Decide whether the stream's afterlife (replay page, clips, podcast feed) belongs in the same tool or in a separate video host that you already pay for.

What this list shows

The twelve tools split into groups: six cloud services (Castr, Restream, OneStream Live, Livepush, Streamster, Riverside), two browser studios (Lightstream, Be.Live), three desktop or mobile apps (Prism Live Studio, Streamlabs Desktop, OBS Studio) and one phone contribution app (Larix Broadcaster). Nine of the twelve can be used at no cost in some form: free plans at OneStream Live, Livepush, Streamster, Riverside and Lightstream, a free way to start at Restream, and free software in Prism, Larix and OBS. Six publish a dollar price on the pages we read: Castr, OneStream Live, Livepush, Streamster, Lightstream and Riverside.

The trade-off is control against effort. Free desktop software such as OBS Studio costs nothing but needs a person who understands encoders, while cloud relays cost $13 to $99 a month and take that work away. Several pages leave gaps that matter for budgeting: Be.Live shows only promotional banners, Restream's plan prices were not captured, and Streamster and OneStream Live do not name their currency.

Ask for the destination limit and the stream-length cap in writing before the event date.

How to choose

1. List every destination and the signal source

Write down each platform and website that must carry the launch, then note what produces the signal: an encoder, a browser, a phone. Match that list to the ingest methods and destination counts above. Many failures come from discovering on launch morning that the plan allows two destinations when the plan needs four.

2. Price the busiest month, not the average one

Estimate hours streamed in your heaviest month and compare monthly plans with hourly billing. Streamster's pay-per-use rate and Castr's entry plan suit lower volume, while a team streaming weekly should price the yearly options at Livepush or OneStream Live. Include the length caps: Lightstream's free tier stops at 10 hours per stream and OneStream's at one hour per live session.

3. Run one full rehearsal on the free tier or trial

Castr offers a 7-day Starter trial, Be.Live a 14-day trial and several others a free plan. Send a rehearsal stream to every real destination, check audio sync and recording, and confirm who receives chat. Only then pay, and cancel or downgrade after the event if the vendor allows monthly billing.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best multistreaming platform in 2026?

OneStream Live is our first pick for businesses, because its pricing is published from free to $99 a month and it takes RTMP, RTMPS, SRT and WHIP feeds. Castr is better when you also want hosting and VOD, and Livepush is better for always-on channels with a branded player.

Can I multistream for free?

Yes, within limits. OneStream Live's free plan allows two destinations at 720p, Livepush gives 20 streaming hours a month, Streamster's free plan has two channels, and OBS Studio is free software. Free tiers cap resolution, session length or destinations, so check the limit that matters for your event.

Do I need OBS if I use a cloud multistreaming service?

No. Browser studios such as Lightstream and Be.Live need no encoder, and OneStream Live offers a browser Live Studio. OBS is useful when you want your own scenes and capture cards, then it sends one feed to the relay, which handles the distribution.

How many platforms can one stream reach?

It depends on the tool and plan. OneStream Live cites 45+ integrations, Livepush 40+, Streamster up to 10 channels on its top plan, and Lightstream up to five destinations. A custom RTMP address lets most of them reach platforms that are not on the built-in list.

Is pay-per-hour streaming cheaper than a subscription?

For rare events it can be. Streamster lists pay-per-use hours from $0.48 to $1.92. A team streaming once a month at a few hours each may spend less than the $15 to $75 monthly plans, but weekly broadcasters usually do better on a fixed plan.

Can a phone supply a camera angle to a multistream setup?

Yes. Larix Broadcaster is a free iOS and Android app for remote contribution, Streamster has a smartphone remote mode, and OneStream Live has mobile apps. The phone sends its feed to a studio, relay or server that then fans out to the other destinations.

What should I check before a launch-day multistream?

Confirm the destination limit, the resolution cap, the maximum stream length and where chat appears. Rehearse with the real accounts, because platform keys and permissions fail more often than software does. Keep a backup encoder or a second relay account ready for the hour that matters.
