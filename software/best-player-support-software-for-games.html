What are player support software for games?

Player support software is a help desk or messaging tool adapted to games: players open a ticket from inside the game, a bot or an agent answers in their language, and the studio sees volume by topic. It differs from generic customer service tools by SDKs for mobile and PC, console handoff and community links.

How we ranked these

A launch weekend can turn a three-person support inbox into thousands of tickets about login errors, refunds and lost items. Studios then need a tool that meets players where they are, inside the game, in their own language, with automation for the repeat questions.

This ranking looks at fourteen options, two built around games and the rest general support platforms that studios borrow. We read the vendors' home pages and pricing links, compared the channels, SDKs and AI features each one states, and noted where prices stay hidden.

We did not run any of them. Vendor numbers such as client counts are marked as claims, and a missing detail is reported as missing.

Reach inside the game client

Players rarely leave a match to visit a help site. Tools with a mobile, PC or web SDK let a player file a ticket with context attached, while generic web widgets only suit launcher pages and websites. Console is harder still: some vendors hand off through QR codes, others do not mention consoles at all.

Check this first, since retrofitting an SDK into a shipped build is slow.

Automation of repeat questions

Password resets, purchase issues and bug reports make up most volume. Look at what each vendor says about AI agents, bots and copilots, and whether a human can take over cleanly. Vendor resolution rates are marketing numbers, so treat them as claims.

What matters is whether the bot can be trained on your own help center and whether it prices per resolved case or per seat.

Languages and time zones

A global player base writes in dozens of languages, at any hour. Compare multilingual support, translation features and routing rules across agents. Teams that outsource weekend coverage should also ask whether the vendor sells human services alongside software, which only a few in this list state.

Price visibility and seat model

Per-seat plans, per-outcome fees and quote-only contracts produce very different bills during a spike. Only some vendors here publish amounts. Where a price is missing, ask for a written estimate based on your peak ticket month, not your average one, and check whether an outcome-based fee applies on top of the seat price.

What this list shows

Of the fourteen tools, three state a price on the pages we opened: Intercom ($19 per seat plus $0.99 per outcome), LiveChat (from $19 per user) and Tidio (paid plans from $29 per month). Zendesk gives only a free trial and a startup offer. The others route buyers to demos, contact forms or unopened pricing pages.

Two vendors, Trengo and Crisp, call themselves European, and Crisp names a French company, which matters for teams with data residency concerns.

Only Helpshift and AIHelp are built around games, and both state mobile SDKs. The rest were designed for online shops and software companies, so a studio adopting them accepts extra setup: no console handoff, no store-purchase context, and chat widgets aimed at websites. The upside is more mature ticketing, reporting and app marketplaces, which is why large incumbents still rank well for mid-size studios.

How to choose

1. Count tickets per platform and per language

Pull a month of support volume and split it by device and language. If most contact comes from mobile, a mobile SDK tool rises on the list. If most arrives by email and Discord, a shared inbox may do.

This sorting stops you buying a feature set that your players do not use.

2. Model the bill at peak volume

Ask each shortlisted vendor to price a launch month, with both seats and AI resolutions included. Compare per-seat plans against outcome-based fees, since a quiet month and a patch-day month can differ by ten times. Keep the quotes next to the vendor's public prices so any gap is visible.

3. Test the handoff between bot and human

Run five real tickets through the bot and note where it escalates, what context the agent sees and whether the player must repeat themselves. A trial period is offered by several vendors here, including Zendesk, Intercom and AIHelp. Judge the handoff and the multilingual replies, not the demo script.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best player support software for games in 2026?

Helpshift ranks first for studios because it offers in-game SDKs for mobile, PC and web plus console handoff through QR codes. AIHelp is the better pick when you want a free trial and web chat alongside an SDK, and Zendesk fits teams that need a general help desk with a large app marketplace.

Do I need a game-specific support tool?

Not always. Generic platforms such as Zendesk, Intercom and Front handle email and chat well. A game-specific tool such as Helpshift or AIHelp adds in-game ticketing through an SDK, which matters when most players never open a browser.

Count where your tickets come from before deciding.

Which tools publish prices?

Intercom lists $19 per seat per month plus $0.99 per outcome, LiveChat says plans start from $19 per user per month, and Tidio says paid plans start from $29 per month. Helpshift, AIHelp, Kustomer, Ada and Sprinklr hide amounts behind demos or separate pricing pages.

Can these tools support console players?

Helpshift says it supports consoles through QR codes and links, handing the player to a phone or browser. Most other vendors in this list do not mention console support at all on their home pages, so ask directly if consoles are a large share of your audience.

How does AI change support volume for a studio?

Vendors claim large automated resolution shares, such as Intercom's 76% average for Fin and Ada's 84%, but these are self-reported and depend on content quality. A fair test uses your own help center and your own ticket history, with a human fallback for refunds and bans.

Is Discord useful as a support channel?

It can work as a community help channel, since it offers text and voice chat on PC, phone and console. It is not a ticketing system, so assignments, SLAs and history need another tool. Many studios pair it with a help desk and link both.

What does multilingual support involve?

It involves translating tickets, answering in the player's language and routing by language. Ada lists omnichannel multilingual support, and Helpshift sells human services as well as software. Check which languages are covered, since vendor pages seldom list them.
