What are game payment and web shop platforms?

A game payment and web shop platform lets a studio sell items, currency or games to players outside the app stores. It hosts the web store and checkout, accepts local payment methods, and in many cases acts as merchant of record, taking over sales tax, VAT, fraud handling and chargebacks so the studio does not register in each market itself.

How we ranked these

Studios that sell directly to players have two jobs that look alike but are not: building a storefront players want to open, and taking money in dozens of countries without becoming a tax department. The vendors below split along that line.

Some are built for games and ship web shops, offers and launchers. Others are general payment or merchant of record companies that list games as one use case.

We read each vendor's homepage, about page and pricing page, noted what each says about payment methods, currencies, merchant of record status and fees, and weighed fit for an indie or mid-size studio rather than a platform holder. We did not run transactions.

Where a figure comes from the vendor, the entry says so, and where no price is shown we say that the vendor sells on quote or through a contact form. Thirteen vendors passed the check; one planned vendor could not be opened and was dropped.

Who carries the tax and chargeback burden

A web shop is only cheaper than a store cut if you do not rebuild compliance yourself. Check whether the vendor names itself merchant of record, because that is the sentence that moves sales tax, VAT and most fraud exposure off your books. Platforms that only process payments leave registration, remittance and disputes with you.

Several vendors on this page offer both modes, so read which product you would actually be buying. Pay particular attention to chargeback handling and to whether tax is calculated per country or per region.

Reach in payment methods and currencies

Players in Southeast Asia, Brazil or Turkey rarely pay by card. A shop that lists many local methods and currencies converts better in those markets than one that stops at cards and wallets. Vendors quote counts such as 130+ currencies or 1000+ methods; treat those as vendor claims and compare the ones that matter for your audience, including carrier billing and prepaid vouchers.

Also ask whether prices can be set per region, since a single dollar price loses sales in cheaper markets.

Storefront and live-ops tools

Some platforms hand you a payment page; others give you a branded store, personalized offers, loyalty rewards, a launcher or deep links from inside the game. If your live-ops team already runs offers, a plain checkout may be enough. If not, built-in offer logic and player segmentation save engineering time.

Look at how the shop connects to the game: SDK, link-out, payment links or server calls, and who owns the player identity during purchase.

Fee transparency and contract terms

Most game-focused vendors do not print their rates. Expect a contact form, a demo request or a performance-based fee. That is workable, but it means your first real comparison happens in a sales call, so go in with your expected monthly volume and average purchase size.

A published rate card, volume discounts, minimums, payout schedules and exit terms all belong on your question list. Be wary of any claim about what you keep per sale until the fee basis is written down.

What this list shows

The list splits into two camps. Seven vendors are built around games or digital goods: Xsolla, Appcharge, Coda Payments, Stash, Aghanim, Tebex and Boku. The other six are general merchants or payment processors: FastSpring, Paddle, Paymentwall, Nuvei, Adyen and Stripe.

Among the pages we read, only Coda prints percentage rates, 1%, 5% and a flat 15% per transaction, and Adyen describes its structure without figures. At least eight of the thirteen route pricing questions to a contact form, a demo or a sales call.

The main trade-off is control against effort. A merchant of record that also builds the store, such as Appcharge or Stash, shortens launch time but ties your offer logic to one vendor. A processor such as Adyen or Stripe gives you the rails and leaves tax, shop design and fraud policy to you.

Two vendors, Appcharge and Aghanim, focus on mobile games specifically, which matters because link-out from an app has different rules from a PC storefront. Several of the figures above, such as volume processed or countries covered, are vendor-reported and not independently confirmed.

How to choose

1. Decide who stays merchant of record

Write down whether you want tax and compliance taken off your hands or whether you already have an entity set up for it. If the answer is take it off, shortlist only vendors that state merchant of record on their own pages: Xsolla, Appcharge, Stash, Aghanim, Coda Payments, FastSpring and Paddle are the ones whose opened pages say so or describe it. If you already handle tax through a finance team, a processor may cost less and give you more control.

2. Match the platform to where players are

List your top five player countries and the payment methods people use there. Then check each vendor's stated coverage for exactly those markets, not the global headline number. A shop that reaches 200+ countries on paper may still lack the voucher or wallet your biggest market prefers.

Ask for the method list by country in writing before you sign anything, and test a checkout from a device located in that market if a demo store is available.

3. Price it with your own numbers

Take your last full month of sales, split by country and average purchase value, and ask two or three vendors to quote against it. Compare total cost including tax handling, refunds, chargebacks and payout fees, not only the headline rate. For vendors that publish rates, like Coda, check which rate applies to your volume tier.

For quote-only vendors, push for a written rate card and a minimum term before you commit engineering time to an integration.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best game payment and web shop platform in 2026?

Xsolla is the broadest fit: a games-first company with web shop, payments and publishing tools, covering 200+ geographies and 1000+ payment methods according to its About page. Appcharge is the better pick if you ship a mobile game and want a branded web store with merchant of record. Coda Payments is the better pick if you want published per-transaction rates.

Do I need a merchant of record to sell in-game items on the web?

You do not strictly need one, but without it you register for sales tax and VAT where required, remit it, and handle disputes yourself. A merchant of record becomes the seller of record to the player and takes on that work for a fee. Small studios usually choose one to avoid building a compliance function.

How much does a game web shop platform cost?

It depends on the vendor, and most do not print rates. Coda lists standard rates from 1% to 15% per transaction on its pricing page, with custom pricing from $100K USD monthly volume. Xsolla, Appcharge, Stash and Tebex direct buyers to sales contacts.

Adyen charges a fixed fee plus a payment-method fee with no setup cost.

Can a web shop replace app store billing for a mobile game?

It can supplement it. Vendors such as Appcharge and Aghanim sell in-game items through web stores reached by links from the game. Whether you may steer players off the store depends on platform rules in each region, which change, so confirm the current terms for each store before moving volume.

Which platforms suit a PC or console studio rather than mobile?

Xsolla describes web shops for mobile, PC and web games, and Tebex serves server-based games and webstores. FastSpring lists a video games use case for selling games direct to consumers, and Paddle's use-case pages cover games alongside SaaS. Appcharge and Aghanim describe a mobile focus.

Is Stripe enough for a small game studio?

Stripe is enough if you only need to accept payments and are comfortable running tax and your own store pages. Its opened pages describe payments, billing, invoicing and tax tools, with 135+ currencies and payment methods. It does not provide a games-specific web shop, offers or launcher, so expect more build work than with a games-first platform.

What is carrier billing and does it matter for games?

Carrier billing charges a purchase to a phone bill, which helps in markets where players lack cards. Boku builds around local payment methods including wallets and direct carrier billing, and states 200+ local methods in 40+ markets on its homepage. It matters most if a large share of your audience is in prepaid mobile markets.
