What are DDoS protection for game servers?

DDoS protection for game servers is a service or appliance that absorbs and filters flood attacks aimed at game hosts, whose traffic is mostly UDP and custom protocols rather than web requests. It works by routing traffic through scrubbing capacity or by filtering at the network edge, and the key trade-off is how much latency and routing change it adds for players.

How we ranked these

A game server under attack is a business problem within minutes: players disconnect, streamers move on and refund requests start. Web-oriented protection is not always a fit, because game traffic is mostly UDP and an extra routing hop can add the milliseconds players notice.

This ranking covers ten providers, from a hosting company that markets a game-specific offer, to a Minecraft-focused proxy service, to carrier-grade vendors and large content delivery networks. We read each vendor homepage, noted what it says about games, capacity, deployment and price, and weighed fit for studios and community server operators.

We did not simulate attacks, and every capacity figure below is the vendor's claim. Where the opened page showed no game-specific wording, we say so.

Game and UDP awareness

Web DDoS tools inspect HTTP, while a game server needs filtering that understands UDP floods and custom protocols without breaking sessions. We looked for explicit game wording on each homepage, such as a game offer, a gaming customer list or protocol-level filtering. A vendor with only web terms (WAF, CDN, bot rules) on its page may still help, but you must ask how it handles non-HTTP traffic.

Added latency and routing model

Always-on proxying, on-demand diversion, hosting-level filtering and on-premises appliances each change the path packets take. The fewer hops between player and server, the lower the added delay. We noted the deployment options each vendor states, and flagged vendors that publish a latency-related claim, because a player in a ranked match feels ten extra milliseconds.

Capacity claims and where they come from

Terabit figures look impressive but are marketing until a contract states what you receive. We recorded published filtering capacity only where the homepage printed a number, and treated every figure as a vendor claim. More useful questions are where the scrubbing sites are, how fast mitigation starts and whether attacks beyond the stated capacity cause extra charges.

Cost model and free entry points

Protection can be bundled with hosting, billed by clean traffic, charged by subscription or sold by quote. A community server operator needs a free or low entry point, while a studio running its own data centers wants a contract. We noted which vendors show a free start, a price, a trial or only a sales form.

How to choose

1. Map your traffic before you shop

Write down your protocols, ports, regions and peak concurrent players. Separate game traffic (UDP) from your web store and API (HTTP), because you may want different products for each. Vendors that show only web terms on their pages need to be asked about UDP handling, and a clear answer here narrows the field quickly.

2. Ask each vendor for latency and failover numbers

Request the expected added round-trip time for your player regions, the list of scrubbing locations and what happens when the protection layer itself fails. A provider that cannot answer in writing is telling you something. Run a pilot server behind the service and measure ping from your top three player regions before signing.

3. Match the contract to your growth

A free tier or a low server price suits a community network, but a studio launching a hit may exceed it in a week. Ask how the bill changes under attack, whether overage fees apply and how quickly you can move to an enterprise plan. Keep a second provider in mind in case one is overwhelmed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best DDoS protection for game servers in 2026?

OVHcloud is the strongest fit for most studios and server operators because its homepage features a DDoS Protection Game offer and says Anti-DDoS Infrastructure is on by default across its products. TCPShield is better for Minecraft and community networks that want a free start. Gcore is the pick when you want a large published filtering capacity plus a cloud platform.

Does DDoS protection add lag for players?

It can. Any service that proxies traffic adds a hop, and the effect depends on how close the scrubbing site is to the player. TCPShield lists low latency among its features, and hosting-level filtering such as OVHcloud's avoids a separate proxy.

Test with your own player regions, because vendor pages do not publish latency numbers.

Is free DDoS protection good enough for a game server?

For a small community server, often yes. TCPShield offers protection at no cost for gaming networks, and StormWall offers a free test period. A commercial game with a large audience needs written capacity, support hours and a failover plan, which free tiers rarely guarantee.

Treat free tiers as an entry point, not a launch plan.

Can Cloudflare protect a game server?

Cloudflare's homepage lists WAF, CDN, DNS, rate limiting and bot mitigation on a global network, which fits a game's website, store and APIs. The page we opened did not describe protection for raw UDP game traffic, so ask Cloudflare which product covers that before relying on it.

Which DDoS protection providers are European?

Link11 GmbH presents itself as a European provider with its footer company in Germany, and OVHcloud sells in euros with pricing excluding VAT. If European hosting or jurisdiction is a requirement, ask each vendor where traffic is scrubbed and which legal entity signs the contract.

What is the difference between on-premises and cloud DDoS protection?

Cloud protection diverts traffic to a provider's scrubbing network and handles very large floods, while on-premises appliances filter at your own data center edge with no external hop. Corero lists on-premises and hybrid options, Nexusguard lists cloud, on-premises and hybrid, and many others are cloud-only. Hybrid models try to combine both.

Do I need DDoS protection for my game's website too?

Yes, because attackers often hit login pages, stores and APIs when the game servers are well defended. Web-focused products such as Imperva, Akamai and Cloudflare list WAF and bot tools for that layer, and a mixed setup is common: a game-aware layer for servers and a web layer for everything else.
