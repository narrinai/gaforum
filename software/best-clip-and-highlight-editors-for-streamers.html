What are clip and highlight editors for streamers?

A clip and highlight editor for streamers finds or records the best moments of a broadcast or play session and turns them into short, often vertical, videos for TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. Some tools capture clips while you play, others use AI on a finished stream, and others are general editors for polishing the result.

How we ranked these

Streaming produces hours of video and a few minutes worth sharing. The work is finding those minutes, cutting them, adding captions and re-framing for a phone screen.

This ranking looks at ten tools for that job, covering stream-based AI clippers, in-game capture apps and general editors. We read each vendor's homepage, pricing page and feature listing, compared what is published about sources, platforms and plans, and did not run the products ourselves.

Vendor claims are attributed to the vendor. Placement reflects fit for a working streamer who wants vertical clips with the least manual work.

Where a price was missing from the page we read, we state that instead of guessing, and we left out tools whose pages we could not verify.

Where the footage comes from

Some tools read a live or finished stream from Twitch, YouTube or Kick, while others record your game as you play. A streamer with a daily broadcast will care most about the first group, because the clips already exist in the VOD. A player who rarely streams will prefer capture apps.

We checked which sources each vendor actually names.

Automatic highlight finding

Finding the good moment is the slow part. We looked for stated AI or automatic detection, either during play or across a recording, and weighted it above manual trimming alone. Where the facts mention only a timeline editor or capture, the tool is ranked as a helper rather than a clipper.

Vertical output and posting

Short-video platforms want tall frames, captions and quick publishing. We noted which tools state export for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, and which add scheduling. A tool that only saves a landscape file leaves reformatting to another product, which adds a step for every clip.

Cost model and limits

Plans here run from free to credit packs to subscriptions. We recorded what each vendor publishes, including free tiers, trial terms and billing options, and we flagged where amounts were not on the page we read. For high-volume creators, per-credit pricing can cost more than a flat plan, so the model matters as much as the headline price.

What this list shows

Counting the ten tools, eight offer some free entry: Medal is free on PC, StreamLadder, Eklipse, Vizard and CapCut list free plans, Outplayed has a free tier with ads, OpusClip gives a free trial without a card, and OBS Studio is open source. Only StreamLadder ($15 to $39 a month) and Vizard (free with 60 credits a month) show actual amounts on the pages we read. The others list plan names but not figures, and the two graphics-card apps list no price at all.

The market splits into three groups. Stream-first AI clippers such as StreamLadder and Eklipse start from a broadcast and finish with vertical posts. Capture apps such as Medal, Outplayed, the NVIDIA App and AMD Software Adrenalin work during play.

General editors such as CapCut and OpusClip polish or cut any video. Many creators combine one from each group, using capture or OBS for source footage and an AI clipper for the shorts. The practical trade-off is automation versus control: AI picks fast but not always well.

How to choose

1. Decide where your clips start

If you stream regularly, begin with a tool that reads Twitch, YouTube or Kick streams, such as StreamLadder or Eklipse. If you mostly play and want to save a moment mid-match, begin with Medal or Outplayed. If you already have finished videos from any source, a general clipper like OpusClip or Vizard will do.

Picking the starting point first removes half the list.

2. Run a free pass on one real stream

Take a broadcast you already did and run it through a free plan or trial. Count how many suggested clips you would post, how much captioning you must fix, and how long export takes. Vizard's 60 free credits and OpusClip's no-card trial are quick to test.

Judge by clips kept, not clips generated, since over-detection wastes review time.

3. Check hardware and platform limits

The NVIDIA App and AMD Software Adrenalin depend on a matching graphics card, Medal is listed for PC, and Outplayed lists PC and Android. Confirm your device is supported before building a workflow around any of them. Also check whether your posting targets are covered: tools that name TikTok, Reels and Shorts export save a manual reformatting step.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best clip and highlight editor for streamers in 2026?

StreamLadder ranks first because it turns Twitch, YouTube and Kick streams into vertical clips with AI and publishes its plan prices. Eklipse is better if you want scheduling built in, and Medal is better if you mainly clip during play on PC and want a free recorder.

Is there a free tool that clips streams automatically?

Yes. StreamLadder and Eklipse both list free plans, and Eklipse names an auto clipper and gaming stream highlights among its free features. Vizard gives 60 credits a month at no cost.

Limits differ, so read each pricing page before committing.

Do I need a separate clipper if I use OBS Studio?

Usually yes. OBS Studio records and streams, but the facts we read do not list automatic highlights or vertical export. Pairing OBS recordings with StreamLadder, Eklipse or OpusClip covers the missing steps.

Can I clip on console?

The capture apps here are mostly PC tools: Medal is listed for PC, the NVIDIA App and AMD Software Adrenalin depend on PC graphics cards, and Outplayed lists PC and Android. For console footage, use a clipper that accepts uploaded or streamed video, such as StreamLadder from a Twitch stream.

Are GPU driver apps good enough for clips?

They are good for capture without extra software, because the NVIDIA App lists ShadowPlay and AMD Software Adrenalin lists in-game capture. They do not, in the facts we read, find highlights or reformat for vertical video, so most creators add an editor afterward.

What does AI clipping cost?

StreamLadder lists $15, $27 and $39 a month for Starter, Creator and Pro, with a 7-day trial. Vizard has a free plan with 60 credits a month, and the other clippers publish plan names but not amounts on the pages we read. Confirm the current price on each vendor site.

Is CapCut a clip tool or an editor?

It is a general editor with AI features, templates and effects, not a stream clipper. It fits as the polishing step after an auto-clipper picks the moment, and its pricing page compares Free, Pro, Ultra and Teams plans.
