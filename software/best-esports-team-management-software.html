What is esports team management software?

Esports team management software coordinates the people behind a competitive roster: who plays, when practice and scrims happen, who is available, how announcements reach everyone and how dues or prizes are tracked. Most teams assemble it from a scheduling app, a chat platform and a database or board rather than one product.

How we ranked these

No dominant product exists for managing an esports roster, so this ranking compares what teams actually use. Dedicated sports apps such as Spond, Heja and TeamSnap handle availability and payments but come from physical sports.

Calendars like Teamup add access control, chat tools such as Discord and Slack carry daily talk, and workspaces like Notion, Airtable and Trello hold rosters and playbooks. Challengermode is the one esports-native option, a competition platform with matchmaking.

We read vendor pages and pricing tables, noted free tiers and per-seat costs, and ranked by how well each handles the weekly rhythm of availability, scrims and money for an amateur or semi-pro squad.

Availability and scrim scheduling

A scrim only happens when enough players confirm. Look for invites with yes and no replies, reminders, calendar sync and views for different roles. Apps built for sports teams do this by default; a generic board or database needs setup.

Check whether coaches, analysts and substitutes can see different calendars.

Roster and role records

Teams need a place for player handles, roles, contact details and contract dates. Databases such as Airtable and Notion model this flexibly, while sports apps keep a simple member list. Decide how much structure you want before choosing, since a flexible tool takes an evening to build and a simple one takes minutes.

Money and dues

Collecting dues, fees or buy-ins is where free chat tools stop. Spond lists payments, TeamSnap lists registration and payments, and Heja lists fundraising. Esports-native prize handling is a different job covered by tournament software.

Check the fee model and the countries a payment feature supports.

Cost per seat and free tiers

A ten-person roster on a per-seat plan pays ten times the sticker price. Compare free tiers first: several tools here are free for the core job. Then read what the paid plan unlocks, such as ad-free use, more users or access control, and whether prices are monthly or annual.

What this list shows

Eleven products appear, and nine of them have a free route: Challengermode (free to start), Spond, TeamSnap Classic, Heja, Notion, Airtable, Trello, Slack and Teamup's free plan. Teamup, Notion and Slack print prices, from $12 per master calendar, 9.50 euros per member per month and 6.75 euros per user per month on annual billing, while SportsEngine sells by quote. Only Teamup, headquartered in Zurich, and Notion, in San Francisco, name a home in the opened pages.

The split to understand is sports apps versus general work tools. Sports apps are fast to set up, handle RSVPs and money, and speak the language of coaches, but they assume a physical club. General tools like Notion, Airtable and Trello fit an esports org that needs analysts, content and sponsor tracking, yet require someone to build the system.

Discord and Slack fill the chat gap. Most teams end up with a pair: a scheduling app and a chat server.

How to choose

1. Count your roles before your players

A five-player roster with a coach needs RSVPs and a calendar. An organization with academy squads, content staff and sponsors needs a database and permissions. Write down the roles and what each must see, then choose between a sports app and a workspace.

2. Pick the chat home first

Most esports teams already live in Discord. If that is true for you, add a scheduling app that can post reminders there or sync to calendars, rather than replacing it. If your staff already pays for Slack, test whether its channels and workflows cover scrim announcements.

3. Trial the free tier with a real scrim week

Run one full week of practice through the tool: post times, collect replies, track attendance and chase dues. If you end up copying data elsewhere, the fit is wrong. Only then compare paid plans on seat price and access control.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best esports team management software in 2026?

Spond is first for most amateur and semi-pro squads: the team app is free, and it handles events, invites, reminders, availability requests and payments. Teamup is better when you need shared calendars with access control, and Challengermode is better when competition and matchmaking matter more than internal admin.

Do esports teams need special software?

Not always. Most teams combine a scheduling app, a chat server and a shared database. Sports tools such as Spond and Heja cover RSVPs and reminders, and Discord covers talk.

Dedicated esports products mostly target tournaments, which is a different job from running your own roster.

Which tool is cheapest for a small roster?

Spond's team app is free, Heja is free with paid plans from $8.33 for ad-free use, and TeamSnap Classic is free. Notion, Airtable, Trello and Slack also have free plans. Teamup is free but has a 3-day trial on paid tiers that start at $12 per master calendar.

Can I track scrim schedules in Notion or Airtable?

Yes, but you build it yourself. Notion offers databases and teamspaces at 9.50 euros per member per month on its first paid tier, and Airtable is a no-code database with a public API and per-seat plans. Neither provides RSVP reminders the way Spond or Heja do.

Is Discord enough for team management?

For communication, usually. Discord offers voice, text and screen streaming on Windows, Mac, browser, phone and console. The homepage does not list scheduling, attendance or payment features, so pair it with a calendar or sports app.

How should we collect dues?

Use a tool with payments built in. Spond and TeamSnap list payments, and TeamSnap also lists registration. Heja lists fundraising.

Check fees and countries with each vendor, since the pages we read do not specify rates.

What about SportsEngine?

SportsEngine targets youth clubs, teams, leagues and facilities, with registration, team stores and custom websites. It says it is now part of PlayMetrics and sells by quote, so it fits an academy program or league organizer more than a single roster.
