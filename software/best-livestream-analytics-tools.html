What are livestream analytics tools?

Livestream analytics tools collect and display statistics about channels, games and audiences on platforms such as Twitch, YouTube and Kick. They report concurrent viewers, hours watched, follower growth and rankings, and enterprise versions add creator discovery, fake audience scoring and campaign reporting for publishers and agencies.

How we ranked these

Streamers ask three different questions of analytics: which game should I play, how is my channel growing, and who is worth partnering with. Public stat sites answer the first two for free.

Business platforms answer the third for brands and studios on a quote. A few overlay suites are listed here too, because streamers often assume they include reporting.

This ranking read vendor homepages, about pages, plans and pricing pages for eleven products and judged them on platform coverage, what the pages say is measured, whether a price is published and who the tool is built for. We did not run the tools.

Three planned entries could not be opened and were left out, and the facts for two suites show no analytics features, which the entries say plainly.

Platforms covered

Coverage splits by network. SullyGnome and TwitchTracker are Twitch sites, Twitchmetrics adds YouTube and Kick, Social Blade lists YouTube, TikTok, Twitch, Facebook and Instagram, and vidIQ and TubeBuddy focus on YouTube. Hatchet cites 32 platforms.

Pick the tool that matches where your audience actually watches, or you will read numbers for the wrong channel.

What is measured

Look for the metric that answers your question: concurrent viewers, hours watched, game rankings, follower growth, keyword research or campaign results. TwitchTracker lists viewers, watch time, stream time and languages, Social Blade lists growth and report cards, and Hatchet lists mention tracking and fake audience scoring. A tool with many charts but not your metric wastes time.

Public data versus your own data

Stat sites show public numbers that anyone can look up, which suits research and competitor checks. Your own retention, revenue and chat data live in the platform dashboards. Twitchmetrics offers advanced analytics on a streamer's own channel in its streamer plan.

Decide whether you need to study the market or your own channel before paying.

Price transparency and audience size

Prices vary from free browsing to quotes. Social Blade lists $59.99 per year, vidIQ lists Boost at $19 per month, and Hatchet and Gamesight send buyers to a demo. Twitchmetrics records only streams with 10 or more concurrent viewers, so tiny channels may not appear.

Check minimum thresholds before relying on a ranking.

Counting the eleven entries, only two publish clear prices, Social Blade and vidIQ, while Hatchet and Gamesight sell by demo, and SullyGnome, TwitchTracker, Twitchmetrics, Twitch Insights, StreamElements and Streamlabs show no plan amounts in the pages we opened. TubeBuddy lists plans without readable prices. Three public stat sites, SullyGnome, TwitchTracker and Twitchmetrics, show no price in the pages we read and, unlike Hatchet and Gamesight, do not send buyers to a demo call.

The gap between hobby and business tools is wide. Hatchet, based in Barcelona and part of GameSquare, and Gamesight target studios and agencies with creator discovery and campaign measurement. The rest serve individual creators.

Three tools planned for this page, Streams Charts, Esports Charts and a separate Stream Hatchet listing, could not be verified from their vendor pages, which is why Hatchet appears under its new address and the esports charts sit outside this ranking.

How to choose

1. Write the question before opening a tool

Choose between three tasks: research games and peers, track your own growth, or plan a sponsored campaign. Game and peer research points to SullyGnome, TwitchTracker or Twitchmetrics. Cross-network growth points to Social Blade.

YouTube optimization points to vidIQ or TubeBuddy. Campaign planning points to Hatchet or Gamesight.

One clear question keeps you from drowning in charts.

2. Compare one channel in two tools

Look up your own channel, or one you know well, in two stat sites and compare peak viewers and hours watched for the same week. Differences show you how each tool samples data. If both agree within a small margin, trust either.

If they differ widely, read each tool's about page and use the one with the clearer method.

3. Keep the platform dashboard as the source of truth

Public stat sites estimate from visible data, while the Twitch or YouTube dashboard holds your actual retention and revenue. Use outside tools for context and for pitching sponsors, and use the official dashboard for decisions about your own schedule. Save screenshots monthly so you can show a trend, not a single peak.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best livestream analytics tool in 2026?

Social Blade is the best all-round pick here: it covers five platforms, publishes a $59.99 yearly membership and has free browsing. SullyGnome is better for Twitch game research, and Hatchet is better for a studio or brand that needs creator discovery and campaign reporting on a quote.

Which analytics site is best for Twitch?

SullyGnome and TwitchTracker both focus on Twitch statistics for channels and games. SullyGnome emphasizes channels, games, teams and milestones, while TwitchTracker lists rankings, watch time, stream time and clips. Twitchmetrics adds YouTube and Kick.

Try two and compare, since the vendor pages do not show how the data is sampled.

Are livestream analytics tools free?

Many are. The public stat sites here, including SullyGnome, TwitchTracker and Twitchmetrics, show data without a stated charge, though their pages do not list premium options clearly. Social Blade is free with ads and lists a $59.99 yearly membership, and vidIQ has a free plan.

Can analytics tools tell me which game to stream?

They can help. SullyGnome and TwitchTracker publish game statistics, so you can compare viewers against the number of channels. A game with decent viewers and few streamers gives a small channel more room to be seen.

Treat it as a guide, because your own interest and skill matter for retention.

What do studios use to measure streamer campaigns?

Studios use business platforms. Hatchet describes creator discovery, fake audience scoring, mention tracking and campaign reporting, and Gamesight lists measurement, a creator CRM and key distribution. Both route buyers to a demo and show no prices on the pages we opened.

Does vidIQ work for Twitch?

No, the vendor describes vidIQ as a YouTube growth tool with keyword research, competitor tracking and channel audits. It helps a streamer whose VODs and clips live on YouTube. For Twitch viewership, use a site such as SullyGnome or TwitchTracker.

Do StreamElements and Streamlabs include analytics?

The pages we read describe overlays, alerts, tipping, chatbot and merch, and do not describe analytics dashboards. They may include some reporting inside the product, but we could not confirm it. For hours watched and rankings, use a dedicated stat site.
