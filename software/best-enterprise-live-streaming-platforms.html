What are enterprise live streaming platforms?

Enterprise live streaming platforms are business video systems that broadcast one presenter or studio to many viewers at once, such as employees at a town hall or customers at a launch. They add what consumer tools lack: access control, single sign-on, delivery that spares office networks, audience analytics and a managed library of recordings.

How we ranked these

A chief executive speaking to twenty thousand employees is a different problem from a gamer streaming to a few hundred followers. The video must start for everyone, stay behind a login, and not saturate the office network.

This ranking is written for communications, IT and marketing teams who run that kind of event. We read each vendor's homepage, product and pricing pages and compared what they publish about live features, security, delivery and cost.

We did not run broadcasts ourselves, and where a vendor makes a claim, such as a certification, we say it is the vendor's claim. Twelve platforms are ranked.

Order reflects fit for corporate broadcasts, so developer APIs and classroom suites sit lower even when they are good products.

Delivery to crowded office networks

A live event fails when thousands of staff pull the same stream across one office link. Enterprise eCDN tools cache and share video inside the network to take that load off. We checked whether a vendor describes network offload, a rehearsal or silent test, and real-time monitoring.

Vbrick and Hive Streaming list these directly. Platforms that describe only public delivery rank lower for internal broadcasts, however polished their player is.

Security and compliance claims

Corporate events often carry earnings news or strategy. Look for stated certifications, access controls and the kind of buyers a vendor names. We recorded only what the vendor's pages say, such as Vbrick's FedRAMP statement, and treated them as claims to verify in procurement.

A vendor that is silent about security on its public pages is not necessarily weak, but you will need documents before a security review.

Pricing you can plan around

Most platforms here sell by quote. A few publish rates: Dacast lists three monthly plans and Mux lists per-minute usage rates. Others publish tiers without dollar amounts.

Published prices help a department bid for budget before a sales cycle starts. We weighed this lightly against capability, because an unpublished price often reflects negotiated enterprise contracts, but it counts when a team needs a fast answer.

Event workflow versus infrastructure

Some products give a producer a finished event: registration, polls, captions, a studio and reporting. Others give engineers building blocks such as servers or APIs. We favored products a communications team can operate without developers, and we ranked infrastructure tools lower on this page for that reason.

If you do have engineers, the infrastructure tools may save money and offer more control.

What this list shows

The list splits into three groups. Six platforms are enterprise video suites sold on contact: Vbrick, Hive Streaming, Qumu, Panopto, YuJa and Brightcove all show no price on the pages we read, and Kaltura offers only trial tiers before quotes. Three publish some pricing: Dacast, Mux and Zoom, though Zoom's dollar amounts sit on another page.

Free entry points exist at Vimeo (a free plan), Kaltura (a trial), Dacast (14 days) and Wowza (a trial). Anyone comparing budgets will mostly be waiting for quotes.

The trade-off is breadth versus focus. Kaltura, Brightcove and Vimeo cover hosting, OTT and events under one roof, which simplifies vendor management but spreads attention. Hive Streaming is narrow on purpose, an add-on that improves delivery for a platform you already own.

Wowza and Mux sit furthest toward infrastructure. One vendor change is worth noting: Qumu's website now redirects to a page on the Enghouse Video site, so check who supports the product before signing.

How to choose

1. Count the viewers and map their networks

Write down the largest audience you expect and where they sit: one headquarters, many branches, home offices. If thousands will join from a few buildings, delivery inside the network decides the outcome, and Vbrick or Hive Streaming should be on the shortlist. If viewers are customers on the public internet, hosting platforms like Brightcove or Vimeo matter more than eCDN.

2. List what the event needs besides video

Registration, polls, captions, a recording library, single sign-on and reports each narrow the field. Zoom Events and Webinars is strong on registration and audience interaction. Panopto and Kaltura are strong when the recording library matters.

Decide which of these are mandatory before you request demos, so you can disqualify products quickly.

3. Ask for a rehearsal and written security documents

Request a test event on your real network, and ask for certification documents rather than relying on website statements. Check who supports the product and for how long, especially where a brand has been moved to a parent company's site. Ask what the price includes: viewer hours, storage, support and add-ons such as China delivery.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best enterprise live streaming platform in 2026?

Vbrick is our top pick for corporate town halls because it combines live webcasting, an eCDN, analytics and a vendor-stated FedRAMP certification. Hive Streaming is better when you already own a video platform and need delivery help for a large event. Kaltura is the better third pick when you want a video portal and webinars in the same contract.

What is an eCDN and do I need one?

An eCDN is a delivery layer inside a company network that lets viewers share a stream rather than each pulling it from the internet. You need one when thousands of employees watch from the same offices. Vbrick and Hive Streaming both list eCDN products.

If your audience is external customers, you probably do not.

Which platforms publish their prices?

Few do. Dacast lists plans at $39, $63 and $165 per month, and Mux lists per-minute usage rates. Zoom shows three plan names but sends you elsewhere for amounts.

Vbrick, Brightcove, Qumu, YuJa, Hive Streaming and Panopto expect you to request a quote.

Can a free plan handle an internal company broadcast?

A free plan can host a small test, but company-wide events need access control, support and reporting that free tiers rarely include. Vimeo offers a free plan and Kaltura a free trial. Treat those as ways to evaluate the interface, then price a paid tier against your audience size and security needs.

Is Zoom good enough for town halls?

For registration-based webinars and smaller company meetings, yes. The Webinars plan covers up to 500 attendees, and higher plans add production tools. For tens of thousands of viewers on office networks, the pages we read describe no eCDN, so dedicated platforms such as Vbrick are the safer path.

Should engineers build on a video API instead?

Only if you have developers and want a custom experience. Mux sells live encoding and delivery per minute, and Wowza offers server software you run yourself. Both give control, but you must build registration, reporting and moderation.

A communications team is faster with a finished event product.

What should I ask a vendor before signing?

Ask for a rehearsal on your own network, current security certificates, a written price covering viewers, storage and support, and the support commitment for the product's future. Qumu's move to a parent company site is a reminder to confirm who stands behind a product.
