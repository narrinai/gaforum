Best 2D Game Engines in 2026

What are 2D game engines?

A 2D game engine is software for making games drawn in flat planes: sprite animation, tilemaps, collision, scene editing and export to devices. Some are full editors with visual tools, others are code libraries for the browser. This ranking covers both and compares editor tools, export targets and license terms published by each vendor.

How we ranked these

Picking a 2D engine usually fails on one of three points: the tool cannot reach the platform you promised a publisher, the license adds a fee after launch, or the workflow clashes with how your team builds levels. A pixel platformer and a browser puzzle game need different things, even though both are called 2D.

We read vendor homepages, documentation pages and license statements for twelve tools and compared what each one publishes about editors, tilemaps, sprite tooling, export targets and cost. We did not run benchmarks, and where a vendor stays silent, such as on price, we say so rather than guess.

Ranking weighs fit for small teams first, then breadth of targets, then clarity of terms. Tools that cannot show a price, platform list or license on their own pages rank lower, however well known they are.

Tilemap and sprite tooling

A flat-game engine lives or dies on how fast you can lay out tiles and animate sprites. Some tools ship dedicated editors: Defold lists tilemap and particle editors, GameMaker has a room workflow, and Stencyl offers a Scene Designer and Actor Editor. Library-style choices such as LOVE and KAPLAY give you drawing calls and expect you to bring your own level tools.

Phaser adds GPU-driven tilemap and sprite layers for dense scenes. We checked which tool describes these features on its own pages and gave credit only for what is stated.

Where the build can ship

Export targets decide whether a game reaches phones, browsers, Steam or consoles. GameMaker lists Xbox Series X|S, PlayStation 5 and Switch; Defold lists Xbox, PlayStation and Switch too; Godot sends consoles through third-party publishers; browser frameworks such as Phaser, KAPLAY and Excalibur.js focus on the web. We recorded the platforms each vendor names and treated unlisted platforms as unconfirmed rather than absent.

License and cost after launch

Free to start does not always mean free to ship. Defold states no royalties and no runtime fee, Solar2D promises no hidden fees under MIT, and LÖVE allows commercial use under the zlib/libpng license. GameMaker and GDevelop have paid tiers where console release or iOS publishing sits behind a plan, and Stencyl and Cocos Creator show no prices on their homepages.

Read the plan page before you promise a platform to a publisher.

Scripting model and team skills

The language your team already writes is a real cost. GameMaker uses its own GML, Godot offers GDScript, C#, C++ or GDExtension, Phaser, KAPLAY and Excalibur.js use JavaScript or TypeScript, and LÖVE and Solar2D use Lua. GDevelop and Stencyl avoid code with events and blocks.

A mismatch means retraining, so we weighed how well each scripting option suits a small group of two to ten people.

  1. 1

    GameMaker

    Dedicated 2D engine with GML and console targets

    Why #1: Built only for 2D, with Xbox, PlayStation and Switch among its homepage targets and a free tier for prototypes, which settles the top spot for sprite-driven work.

    Best for
    Solo 2D developers shipping to consoles
    Pricing
    A free tier with no credit card; Professional adds commercial release and Enterprise adds console release, neither with a price on the homepage.
    Free option
    Free plan
    Company
    GameMaker
    Platforms
    Windows, Mac, Linux, Android, iOS, HTML5, Xbox Series X|S, PlayStation 5, Switch targets
    Key features
    GML language, Asset Bundles, Marketplace, Education licenses, GX.games upload

    GameMaker takes first place because it makes no attempt to be a 3D tool. The homepage presents GML scripting, a marketplace of assets and tutorials, and a target list that runs from HTML5 and mobile to Xbox Series X|S, PlayStation 5 and Nintendo Switch.

    The free tier needs no card, so a prototype costs nothing, and the vendor cites UNDERTALE, Hyper Light Drifter and Chicory: A Colorful Tale as titles built with it. For a small team that wants a sprite and room workflow without learning a general engine, the path from first test to console release is the shortest on this list.

    Where it falls short

    Console release sits behind the Enterprise tier, and neither paid tier shows a price on the homepage, so budgeting needs a sales or store visit. The page does not state which operating systems run the editor.

    GML is a proprietary language, which ties project code to this one tool.

    Skip it if

    Teams that insist on open-source code or a 3D roadmap should read the Godot entry; browser-first developers will prefer Phaser.

    Godot

    Open-source engine with a separate 2D renderer

    Why #2: Free, open source and equipped with a dedicated 2D engine, so a studio pays nothing and keeps full control of the editor and its source.

    Best for
    Teams wanting a free engine with no strings
    Pricing
    No charge. The homepage calls it free and open source and makes no further pricing claim; it does not name the license in the page text.
    Free option
    Open source
    Company
    Godot
    Platforms
    Exports to desktop, mobile and web; consoles through third-party publishers
    Key features
    Node and scene system, GDScript, C#, C++ or GDExtension, Dedicated 2D engine, Export to desktop, mobile and web

    Where GameMaker locks the toolchain, Godot hands over everything. Its node and scene model lets you compose a level from small reusable pieces, and the vendor describes a dedicated 2D engine alongside its 3D one rather than a flattened 3D view.

    Scripting comes in GDScript, C#, C++ or GDExtension, and exports reach desktop, mobile and web. Sponsors named on the homepage include Scorewarrior, W4 Games, JetBrains and Mega Crit, and the Godot Foundation hosts the project, which gives a small team some comfort about continuity.

    The latest download listed is Godot 4.7.2.

    Where it falls short

    Console export is not built in: the homepage says consoles run through third-party publishers. The only editor download link shown is for Windows.

    C# on the web needs Godot 3, according to the homepage, which matters if your team writes C# and ships browser builds.

    Skip it if

    If you want the shortest route to a console port from one vendor, GameMaker is closer; if you write mostly TypeScript, look at Phaser.

    Phaser

    MIT-licensed HTML5 framework for browser 2D games

    Why #3: A code-first framework tuned for the browser, with GPU tilemap and sprite layers that suit large 2D scenes.

    Best for
    Web studios shipping playable ads and portals
    Pricing
    Free under the MIT license, per the official repository page.
    Free option
    Open source
    Company
    Phaser
    Platforms
    Desktop and mobile browsers, YouTube Playables, Discord Activities, Reddit games, Twitch overlays
    Key features
    Canvas and WebGL rendering, TilemapGPULayer, SpriteGPULayer, Unified Filter system

    Phaser belongs near the top for anyone whose game will live in a browser tab. The repository describes a free, fast 2D framework for HTML5 games, written for JavaScript or TypeScript, and the fourth major version is built on a new WebGL renderer with a TilemapGPULayer and a SpriteGPULayer for dense scenes.

    The reach is unusually wide: the page lists YouTube Playables, Discord Activities, Reddit games and Twitch overlays alongside ordinary browsers. The repository reports 40.7k GitHub stars and more than 13 years of active development, and Phaser Studio Inc develops it commercially with the community.

    Where it falls short

    There is no visual editor in the facts we found, so level design relies on external tools. iOS, Android and Steam builds depend on third-party wrappers rather than a first-party exporter. Programming skill is a prerequisite, which rules out a no-code workflow.

    Skip it if

    Designers who want drag-and-drop logic should look at GDevelop; teams targeting consoles should choose GameMaker or Defold.

    Best for
    Small teams wanting royalty-free console targets

    Defold earns its place from the editor itself: the homepage lists a visual editor, a scene editor, a code editor and dedicated ParticleFX and Tilemap editors, plus hot reload, a profiler and a debugger. The target list is long, covering Xbox, PlayStation 4 and 5, Switch, Steam, mobile, HTML5, Poki and Facebook.

What this list shows

Counting the twelve tools, seven are described by their vendors as free and open source or published under a named open license (Godot, Phaser, Solar2D, LOVE, KAPLAY, Excalibur.js and GDevelop), and Defold is free but source available. None of the twelve homepages prints a paid price, so the open tools are the only ones with a clear cost. Three of the twelve (Phaser, KAPLAY and Excalibur.js) are browser-first frameworks, and two more (LÖVE and Solar2D) are Lua code libraries.

The trade-off in this niche is control against convenience. Frameworks like LÖVE, Phaser and Excalibur.js are light and free of lock-in but leave level editors, build packaging and console porting to you. Full editors such as GameMaker and Defold shorten that work, yet GameMaker ties console release to a higher tier and Defold's license is source available rather than open source.

No-code tools lower the barrier but cap how far custom mechanics can go.

How to choose

1. Write down the platforms you promised

List every store and device a publisher, a portal or your own plan requires, then cross it with each vendor's published target list. Consoles narrow the field fast: GameMaker and Defold name them, Godot routes them through third parties, and most web frameworks do not list them. If a platform is missing from the page, email the vendor before you commit months of work.

2. Match the tool to how your team builds levels

If your designers place tiles and trigger events, favor an editor with scene and tilemap tools such as Defold, GameMaker or GDevelop. If your developers prefer code and version control, a framework like LÖVE or Phaser removes friction. Try a small vertical slice with two finalists before deciding; a single weekend shows more than a feature table.

3. Cost the license for year two

Calculate what you pay at launch, not only on day one. Check which tier unlocks your target platforms, whether support is paid, and whether the source license lets you patch the engine. Open tools with MIT, BSD or zlib terms have no such surprise; commercial tools need their pricing page opened and the figures saved with the date.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 2D game engine in 2026?

GameMaker is our first choice for most small teams: it is built only for 2D, has a free tier and lists console targets. Godot is better when open-source code and a future 3D option matter, and Phaser is better when the game runs in a browser. Defold ranks fourth and fits teams that want royalty-free consoles.

Which 2D engine is completely free with no royalties?

Defold states that it is free forever with no royalties and no runtime fee, Solar2D says no hidden fees or royalties under MIT, and LÖVE allows commercial use without limitation under zlib/libpng. Godot, Phaser, KAPLAY and Excalibur.js are also free and open source.

Can I make a 2D game without coding?

Yes. GDevelop uses an event system with ready-made behaviors, Stencyl uses a Scratch-inspired block builder, and Buildbox markets itself as needing no scripting. These tools trade depth for speed, so complex custom mechanics may require extensions, and Buildbox publishes no price or platform list.

Which engine is best for browser 2D games?

Phaser is the leading choice here, with MIT licensing, Canvas and WebGL rendering and listings for YouTube Playables and Discord Activities. KAPLAY suits quick TypeScript prototypes, and Excalibur.js suits typed projects with a documented API.

Does GameMaker support consoles?

The homepage lists Xbox Series X|S, PlayStation 5 and Nintendo Switch as targets, and states that the Enterprise tier adds release on major consoles. Neither paid tier shows a price on the homepage, so ask the vendor or check the store before budgeting.

Is Godot good for 2D or only for 3D?

Godot has a dedicated 2D engine, according to its homepage, rather than treating 2D as a flat 3D scene. It takes second place here for that reason. The caveats are that consoles go through third-party publishers and that C# on the web needs Godot 3.

How do I choose between Lua and TypeScript engines?

Choose by team skill and target. Lua suits LÖVE and Solar2D, which both list mobile builds, with Solar2D also naming tvOS and Android TV. TypeScript suits Excalibur.js and KAPLAY for the browser.

If neither language is in your team already, an editor with its own scripting, like GameMaker, is quicker.
