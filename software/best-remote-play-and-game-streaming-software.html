What are remote play and game streaming software?

Remote play software captures the screen, audio and controller input of a PC you own or rent and sends them to another device over your network or the internet. It covers couch play from a bedroom PC, travel play on a laptop, and general remote desktop tools that can also carry a game.

How we ranked these

This category is about one idea: the PC stays where it is, and the game shows up somewhere else. That can mean a living room TV, a handheld, a phone or a hotel laptop.

Tools in this space split into three families. Game-first streamers such as Moonlight, Sunshine, Steam Remote Play and Parsec are tuned for frame rate and controllers.

Cloud services such as GeForce NOW move the PC into a data center. General remote desktop products such as Splashtop, AnyDesk, TeamViewer, Jump Desktop and Chrome Remote Desktop are designed for support and office work, and gaming is a side use.

We read vendor pages, repositories and pricing pages, and we did not benchmark latency. Position comes from gaming focus, device reach, cost and how clearly the vendor documents what it does.

Built for games or for support

Ask what the tool was designed to carry. Moonlight advertises up to 4K with HDR and up to 120 FPS, while Parsec describes itself as a remote desktop for work or games with a 4:4:4 color mode. Splashtop, AnyDesk and TeamViewer lean on remote support and device management in their own descriptions.

Chrome Remote Desktop mentions screen sharing and support. A support tool can show a game, but nothing on the vendor pages promises controller handling or frame pacing.

Where the client runs

Match the client list to the screens you own. Moonlight lists Windows, macOS, Linux, Steam Link, iOS, Android, Amazon FireOS, ChromeOS, Xbox and more. Chrome Remote Desktop works from a phone, tablet or another computer.

Steam Remote Play ties into the Steam Mobile App. Check for a TV or handheld client before choosing, since that is where most players want the stream.

The host side is a separate question.

Host setup and ownership

Some tools require you to run your own host. Sunshine is a self-hosted game stream host for Moonlight and is open source under GPL-3.0. Parsec connects to a computer you own or rent.

GeForce NOW removes the host by renting NVIDIA's servers and streaming stores you own. Self-hosting costs nothing in fees but you handle ports, drivers and updates, whereas hosted services cost money and take that work away.

Price and licensing

Open source tools cost nothing: Moonlight is an open source client and Sunshine is GPL-3.0. Parsec shows Pro at $9.99 or $8.33 depending on billing. Splashtop lists several tiers from $6 per month billed annually, with a free trial.

TeamViewer sells annual subscriptions with a 30-day trial, and AnyDesk uses licenses from Solo to Ultimate. Check whether a free tier covers personal and non-commercial use before assuming a tool is free for your situation.

    Open source client for streaming your PC games

    Why #1: It lists the broadest client set, advertises 4K HDR at up to 120 FPS and costs nothing.

    Best for

What this list shows

Across the ten entries, two are open source (Moonlight and Sunshine), four publish prices or tiers in readable text (Parsec, Splashtop and, in part, TeamViewer and AnyDesk by license or trial), and four show no price at all (Steam Remote Play, Chrome Remote Desktop, GeForce NOW and Jump Desktop, whose pricing link returned a 404 when we opened it). Only five entries are built around games first. The rest were designed for remote support and carry games as a secondary use.

The main trade-off is polish against cost. Open source streaming from Moonlight and Sunshine reaches the most devices at no cost but expects you to configure a host. Steam Remote Play is simple if your library lives on Steam.

Parsec trades a subscription for easier setup and a trial. Support-focused tools make sense only when you also need to administer machines, since their pricing is built around technicians and teams, not players.

How to choose

1. Count your screens and hosts

Write down the PC that will host the game and every device that will receive it: TV, phone, handheld, laptop. Compare that list with each client list, starting with Moonlight, which names the widest set of platforms. If the only receiver is another Windows laptop, nearly any tool works; if it is an Apple TV or Fire stick, the list narrows quickly.

2. Test on your home network first

Run a stream over your own Wi-Fi or a cable to the same router before trying a remote link. Sunshine with Moonlight costs nothing to try, Steam Remote Play is built into Steam, and Parsec offers a trial. If a local stream stutters, a long-distance one will not improve, and you will have learned that without paying for anything.

3. Add paid tiers only for a reason

Move to a paid product when you need something a free tool lacks: a managed connection, team seats, or a host-free option such as GeForce NOW. Parsec Teams, Splashtop and TeamViewer make sense when several people connect to shared machines. For a single player streaming at home, the free route is usually enough, and the savings pay for a better router.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best remote play and game streaming software in 2026?

Moonlight with Sunshine is the best choice for most PC owners because both are open source, Moonlight lists clients for Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android and Xbox, and it advertises up to 4K HDR at 120 FPS. Parsec is better if you want a polished app with a trial, and Steam Remote Play is better for a Steam-only library.

Is Moonlight free?

Yes. Moonlight is described as an open source game streaming client, and its site lists no price. It needs a compatible host, and Sunshine, which is licensed under GPL-3.0, is the open source host most people pair with it.

Costs come only from your own network and hardware.

Do I need a GPU on the host for game streaming?

The vendor pages we read do not list GPU requirements for the host, so check the Sunshine and Parsec documentation before buying hardware. In general, the host PC runs the game and encodes the video, which is why GeForce NOW can replace it with NVIDIA's servers.

Can I use AnyDesk or TeamViewer for gaming?

You can connect with them, but their pages describe remote access, support and device management, not controller input or frame rate targets. For play, Moonlight, Parsec or Steam Remote Play are described more closely for games. Use AnyDesk or TeamViewer when a technician needs access to a machine.

How does Steam Remote Play compare with Moonlight?

Steam Remote Play is part of the Steam store and links to the Steam Mobile App, so setup is simple if you buy games on Steam. Moonlight is open source, lists more client platforms and can stream any game on the host, not only Steam titles.

Which tools show prices?

Parsec lists Pro at $9.99 or $8.33 and Teams at $35 or $30. Splashtop lists plans from $6 per month billed annually and a free trial. TeamViewer shows annual subscriptions with a 30-day trial.

Moonlight and Sunshine are free; Steam Remote Play, Chrome Remote Desktop and Jump Desktop showed no price in the text we read.

When is GeForce NOW better than streaming my own PC?

When your PC is weak or you travel with a thin laptop. GeForce NOW streams games from stores such as Steam, Epic and GOG on NVIDIA servers, with a top tier described as reaching 5K at 120 FPS. If your own PC is strong and you are mostly at home, local streaming costs less.
