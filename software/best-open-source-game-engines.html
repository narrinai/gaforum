What are open-source game engines?

An open-source game engine is one whose source code is published under a license that lets you use, study and often modify it, usually without fees or royalties. The terms differ: MIT, BSD, Apache 2.0 and zlib are permissive, while source-available licenses restrict some uses. This list covers engines and libraries for 2D, 3D and the web.

How we ranked these

Open source answers the question of cost but not the question of risk. A permissive license can sit beside a project with one maintainer, a donation-funded team or a foundation with corporate members, and each model fails differently.

For this ranking we read twelve vendor homepages and repository pages and looked for three things: the named license, the governance and funding model, and the signs of activity that the pages themselves show, such as version numbers, sponsors and grants. We did not read source code or count commits.

Tools whose homepages do not name a license are ranked lower, with a note that the repository must be checked, because a license you cannot find in two minutes is a license you cannot yet rely on.

Which license, in plain words

Permissive licenses (MIT, BSD, Apache 2.0, zlib/libpng) allow commercial use with few conditions. Bevy states MIT or Apache 2.0, Stride and Phaser state MIT, Solar2D states MIT, Excalibur.js states BSD 2 Clause, and LÖVE states zlib/libpng. Godot, raylib, KAPLAY and O3DE say open source without naming the license in page text.

Babylon.js and Three.js state nothing on their homepages. We credit only what is printed.

Who governs the project

Governance tells you what happens if the founder leaves. Godot is hosted by the Godot Foundation, O3DE by the Open 3D Foundation with the Linux Foundation running site policies, and Stride has .NET Foundation support. Phaser has a company, Phaser Studio Inc, working alongside a community.

Solar2D relies on a small company and sponsors, and Bevy on donors. Foundation hosting lowers key-person risk.

Signs of life on the page

We looked for evidence a project is moving: Godot 4.7.2, Stride 4.4 announced October 9, 2026, O3DE 26.05, Babylon.js 9.0, LÖVE 11.5 and Phaser 4.2.1 all appear on their own pages. raylib lists grants, and Excalibur.js shows a 2026 copyright. These are vendor-displayed signals, not audits, and a version number alone does not prove health.

How the project pays its bills

Funding shapes priorities. Bevy publishes donor tiers from $25 to $6,000 monthly, Solar2D takes sponsorship through GitHub Sponsors and Patreon, KAPLAY and Stride take donations through Open Collective, raylib lists GitHub Sponsors, Patreon and Ko-fi plus grants, and Excalibur.js offers a Premium Support page. Projects with several funding channels tend to be steadier than single-channel ones.

  1. 1

    Godot

    Foundation-hosted engine for 2D and 3D, free and open

    Why #1: Community governance through the Godot Foundation and a long sponsor list make it the safest open-source default.

    Best for
    Studios wanting governance they can point to
    Pricing
    Nothing to pay. Described as a free, open-source engine; the homepage does not state which license applies.
    Free option
    Open source
    Company
    Godot
    Platforms
    Desktop, mobile and web; console work through partners
    Key features
    Scene tree, GDScript, C# and C++, 2D engine, 3D engine, XR support

    Open-source projects live or die by who holds the keys, and Godot answers with a named structure: the homepage says the Godot Foundation hosts the project and credits Juan Linietsky and Ariel Manzur as creators. Corporate and patron sponsors listed include Scorewarrior, W4 Games, JetBrains and Mega Crit, so funding is not left to one company.

    It covers both 2D and 3D, supports XR through OpenXR and WebXR, and offers several scripting routes. The latest download shown on the page is version 4.7.2.

    For readers who rank governance and breadth above all, this entry leads the list.

    Where it falls short

    The license name does not appear in the homepage text, so you must check the repository for exact terms. Console support is delegated to third-party publishers.

    C# web exports are tied to the older Godot 3 line.

    Skip it if

    Rust shops should look at Bevy, and .NET teams at Stride, both of which state MIT terms on their own pages.

    Bevy

    Rust engine under a dual MIT or Apache 2.0 license

    Why #2: A permissive dual license, a published donor ladder and a clear no-sales-cut statement give it the cleanest terms here.

    Best for
    Rust developers who value patent-clear permissive terms
    Pricing
    Free of contracts, license fees and sales cuts. Monthly donor tiers exist from $25 up to $6,000.
    Free option
    Open source
    Company
    Bevy
    Platforms
    Windows, macOS, Linux, web, iOS, Android
    Key features
    Entity component system, Hot reloading, Scenes, Animation, Bevy UI

    Bevy's homepage states its legal position in one breath: open source under MIT or Apache 2.0, with no contracts, license fees or sales cuts. Funding is also transparent, with optional monthly donor tiers published from $25 to $6,000.

    The engine is described as data-driven and built in Rust, with an ECS core, 2D and 3D renderers, a render graph, UI and hot reloading. Targets include desktop, web and both mobile systems.

    The page quotes compile times of 0.8 to 3.0 seconds under a fast compiles setup. Governance beyond the donor list is not described, which keeps it one rung under Godot.

    Where it falls short

    There is no foundation named on the homepage, so long-term stewardship rests on donors and contributors. A Rust-only toolchain narrows who can contribute.

    No visual editor appears in the listed features.

    Skip it if

    Readers who need a foundation-backed project should pick Godot; those writing C# should prefer Stride.

    Stride

    MIT C# engine supported by the .NET Foundation

    Why #3: MIT terms that explicitly cover both editor and runtime, plus a named foundation sponsor, for the C# community.

    Best for
    C# teams wanting MIT source for editor and runtime
    Pricing
    Free. Editor and runtime sources carry the MIT license, per the homepage, and a donation page on Open Collective is linked.
    Free option
    Open source
    Company
    Stride
    Platforms
    Desktop and VR
    Key features

    The detail worth noting on the Stride page is scope of license: it says both the editor and the runtime source code are MIT licensed, which removes a common doubt about open-source engines whose tools are closed. It targets C# developers on .NET 10, lists Vulkan and Direct3D 12 support, and ships templates and an asset creation toolchain.

    The .NET Foundation supports the project. Version 4.4, announced October 9, 2026, brings a graphics rewrite and Native AOT support.

    Open Collective donations fund continued work, so a studio can contribute without a contract.

    Where it falls short

    Named targets are desktop and VR only, so mobile and console plans lack confirmation. A major graphics rewrite landing in the latest release raises short-term upgrade risk.

    Skip it if

    Teams needing mobile builds should look at Godot or Bevy, which list them.

    Best for

    Governance is O3DE's strongest card. The homepage credits the Open 3D Foundation, and notes that the Linux Foundation runs the site's policies, while presenting the engine as open source, community-driven and modular.

    Membership and donations are open to individuals and companies through Become a Member. The page also cites integration with ROS for robotics work, which is unusual for a game engine and attracts simulation users.

    Release 26.05 is named as the newest. A team that values a multi-company foundation over a single maintainer will find the structure reassuring, even though the license text is not visible on the front page.

    Where it falls short

    Phaser

    MIT browser framework developed with a commercial company

    Why #5: MIT licensing plus a company, Phaser Studio Inc, that develops it with the community for more than 13 years.

    Best for
    Web developers shipping HTML5 titles
    Pricing
    Free under MIT, as stated on the official repository page.
    Free option
    Open source
    Company
    Phaser
    Platforms
    Browsers, YouTube Playables, Discord Activities, Reddit games, Twitch overlays
    Key features

    Phaser mixes the two models that most open-source engines choose between. The repository page states an MIT license, reports 40.7k GitHub stars, and says the project has been actively developed for over 13 years, with Phaser Studio Inc building it commercially together with the community.

    That gives users a license they can read and a company accountable for releases. Version 4.2.1 is the current release, built on a new WebGL renderer with a unified filter system.

    The framework targets JavaScript or TypeScript and reaches YouTube Playables, Discord Activities, Reddit games and Twitch overlays.

    Where it falls short

    Company involvement means roadmap decisions rest partly with Phaser Studio, which some purists treat as a dependency. Native iOS, Android and Steam builds rely on third-party tools.

    Only 2D games are in scope.

    Skip it if

    Teams that need an editor-based 3D workflow should read the Godot entry instead.

    Solar2D

    MIT-licensed Lua engine kept alive by sponsors

    Why #6: A transparent MIT license and a story of surviving a vendor's exit, funded by user sponsors.

    Best for
    Lua developers who accept a community-funded project
    Pricing
    Free under MIT with no hidden fees, charges or royalties, per the homepage.
    Free option
    Open source
    Company
    Solar2D LLC, United States
    Platforms
    iOS, tvOS, Android, Android TV, macOS, Windows, Linux, HTML5
    Key features
    Lua, Plugins, Native libraries, Live builds

    Solar2D has the clearest rescue story on the list. It is a fork of the Corona SDK, which the homepage says is no longer commercially supported, and it continues under the MIT license with no hidden fees, charges or royalties.

    Solar2D LLC lists an address in Sheridan, Wyoming, and development is sponsored by users through GitHub Sponsors and Patreon. Governance is therefore a small company plus patrons, a lean model with little corporate backing.

    The engine is Lua-based, supports plugins and calls into any native library, and the build list covers phones, TV platforms, desktops and HTML5.

    Where it falls short

    Funding depends on user sponsorship, so continuity risk is higher than for foundation-hosted projects. The page does not describe contributor counts or release cadence.

    Lua limits the available talent pool.

    Skip it if

    Organizations needing foundation governance should look at O3DE or Godot.

What this list shows

Of the twelve entries, six state a specific permissive license in their own page text (Bevy, Stride, Phaser, Solar2D, Excalibur.js and LÖVE). Four say open source or free without a license name (Godot, O3DE, raylib and KAPLAY), and two (Babylon.js and Three.js) state nothing on their homepages. Three are hosted or supported by a named foundation (Godot, O3DE and Stride).

Eight of them target the browser or list web or HTML5 among their outputs.

The trade-off is between convenience and independence. Editors such as Godot and Stride shorten production, while libraries such as raylib, LÖVE and KAPLAY leave the structure to you. Small, donation-funded projects can move fast but carry key-person risk, and foundation-hosted ones usually move more deliberately.

Check what the license says about patents and trademarks if you plan to fork or redistribute an engine.

How to choose

1. Open the license text, not the badge

Find the LICENSE file for each shortlisted engine and read it, including any exceptions for assets, logos or console SDKs. MIT, BSD and zlib are short and permissive; Apache 2.0 adds a patent grant. Where the homepage does not name the license, write the repository's answer in your notes and save the date.

2. Score the governance risk

Ask who can merge changes, who pays the maintainers and what happens if they stop. A foundation, a company with a product or several funding routes lowers the risk. A single donor-funded maintainer is acceptable for a prototype, but a multi-year commercial game deserves a fallback plan, such as pinning a version and keeping a copy of the source.

3. Test the target platform early

Open-source status does not guarantee a console path. Godot routes consoles through third parties, Stride names desktop and VR, and web libraries reach browsers first. Build a minimal project for your hardest target in the first week, and count the hours the export takes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best open-source game engine in 2026?

Godot is our first pick for most teams: it covers 2D and 3D, is hosted by the Godot Foundation and lists many sponsors. Bevy is better for Rust teams who want a stated MIT or Apache 2.0 license, and Stride is better for C# teams who want MIT-licensed editor and runtime.

Which open-source engine has the most permissive license?

LÖVE uses zlib/libpng, which allows commercial use without limitations, and Bevy offers a choice of MIT or Apache 2.0. Phaser, Stride and Solar2D use MIT, and Excalibur.js uses BSD 2 Clause. All of these are permissive, but Apache 2.0 adds a patent grant.

Are open-source engines safe for commercial games?

Generally yes when the license is permissive and you keep a copy of the version you shipped. The risks are in support and continuity, not legal terms. Read the license for each engine, and note that Babylon.js and Three.js state none on their homepages, so confirm in the repository.

Is Defold open source?

Defold describes itself as source available under a developer-friendly license, which differs from an OSI-style open-source license. It is free with no royalties, but it is not on this list for that reason. Check its license if you plan to fork the engine.

Which open-source engine is best for mobile?

Godot exports to mobile, Bevy lists iOS and Android, Solar2D lists iOS, tvOS, Android and Android TV, and LÖVE offers an Android APK with iOS as source. Solar2D has the broadest mobile list among the Lua tools, while Godot has the largest editor.

Which engines are governed by a foundation?

Godot is hosted by the Godot Foundation, O3DE by the Open 3D Foundation with the Linux Foundation running site policies, and Stride has .NET Foundation support. Phaser is developed by Phaser Studio Inc with a community. Foundation hosting lowers the risk that a single person's departure ends the project.

Can I make 3D games with an open-source engine?

Yes. Godot, Bevy, Stride and O3DE are engines with 3D support, and raylib has 3D with skeletal animation. Babylon.js and Three.js render 3D in the browser.

Godot describes its 3D as simple, while O3DE emphasizes high fidelity and a modular design.
