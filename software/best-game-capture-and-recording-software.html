What is game capture and recording software?

Game capture and recording software saves gameplay video from a PC while you play, either as continuous recordings or as short clips of recent moments. Most tools add editing, a clip library or sharing links, and many use the graphics card to keep frame-rate loss low.

How we ranked these

Recording a game sounds simple, yet the tools differ sharply in how they work: some run in the background and clip a great play for you, some record only when you press a key, some belong to a graphics driver, and one dates from 2013. This page ranks ten options for a player who wants local recordings, quick clips and an easy way to post them.

The reading covered each vendor's homepage, help center and download page, with attention to price, platform and what the vendor says about editing and sharing. We did not measure performance, so none of these entries claims lower frame-rate loss than another.

Where a price is missing from the pages we opened, the entry says so.

Automatic clips versus manual recording

A few apps watch the game and save highlights without a hotkey, while others record only when you start them. Automatic capture suits players who forget to hit record after a clutch moment; manual recording suits people producing longer videos. Look at what the vendor says about detecting plays and which games that applies to, because coverage is often a short list.

A tool that clips only four games helps little if you play something else.

Where the footage ends up

Some recorders keep files on your own drive and stop there, which is simple and private. Others host clips, give you a sharing link, or send them to a community feed. Hosting is convenient, yet it ties your library to the vendor's service and terms.

Decide whether you want raw files for your own editor or a one-click post, and pick the tool that matches, since switching libraries later means moving every clip by hand.

Dependence on a particular graphics card

Driver-bundled recorders only work with that maker's hardware. That is fine if you own the matching card and wasteful if you plan to switch brands. General tools run on any card but may use more of your system.

Read each platform line closely, since the cleanest-looking option may quietly require specific hardware, and confirm the card before you rely on it.

Age and upkeep of the software

Recording tools sit close to the operating system and game engines, so stale releases can break on new games or Windows builds. We checked for release dates and vendor activity where the pages showed them. A free price tag does not help if the last update predates the games you play, so the freshness note in each entry matters.

Where we found no date, we say that too.

What this list shows

Of the ten tools ranked, two are open source or free for good (OBS Studio and ShareX), three offer a free tier with paid extras (Medal, Outplayed and Allstar), three sell a free version or a trial beside a paid one (Bandicam, Mirillis Action and Fraps), and two ship as part of a graphics-card driver (NVIDIA App and AMD Software Adrenalin). None of the paid options states a price on the pages we opened, and Fraps has a newest release dated February 2013.

The trade-off in this niche is convenience against control. Automatic clippers such as Outplayed and Allstar save moments for you but limit the games they cover or host the files; open tools like OBS Studio and ShareX hand you raw files and a lot of settings. Windows dominates the platform lists: Bandicam, Mirillis Action, Fraps, ShareX and both driver apps are Windows, while OBS Studio is the one option that also covers macOS and Linux.

How to choose

1. Decide between clips and long recordings

If you want short moments to post, favor tools that capture automatically or hold a clip library, such as Outplayed, Medal or Allstar. If you record full sessions for editing, favor OBS Studio, Bandicam or Mirillis Action. Mixing the two is common: a clipper for highlights and a general recorder for tutorials or commentary videos, each doing the job it is built for.

2. Check your graphics card first

Open the driver app you already have installed. If it is NVIDIA or AMD, it likely includes a capture feature at no cost, so test that before downloading anything. Only add a third-party recorder if the driver feature lacks editing, hosting or the game you play.

This saves an install and keeps your background processes low and your startup list short.

3. Test one clip from your main game

Record a few minutes in the game you play most, then save a clip, trim it and share it. Look at file size, how long the export takes and whether the tool kept its overlay out of the footage. Do this with two candidates, because defaults differ and a tool that works for one game can disappoint in another, so repeat the check per title.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best game capture and recording software in 2026?

Outplayed is our first pick for players who want automatic highlight capture with a built-in editor and a free tier. Medal is better if you mainly want fast clip sharing, and the NVIDIA App is better for GeForce owners who prefer a driver-level recorder with no extra install.

Is there free software to record PC gameplay?

Yes. OBS Studio is free and open source on Windows, macOS and Linux, and ShareX is free with no ads on Windows. Medal says it is free to record, edit and share clips on PC, Allstar describes its clip capture as free, and Outplayed has a Free tier with ads.

Which recorders save clips automatically?

Outplayed says it automatically captures a player's best moments and biggest plays. Allstar describes clip capture for Counter-Strike 2, League of Legends, Fortnite and Dota 2. Medal records, edits and shares clips, but the page we read does not say how it triggers them, so check its settings.

Do I need a special graphics card to record gameplay?

Not for general tools like OBS Studio, Bandicam or Mirillis Action. Driver recorders do tie you to hardware: the NVIDIA App lists ShadowPlay for NVIDIA GPUs, and AMD Software Adrenalin supports AMD products. If you own neither, pick a general recorder instead.

Is Fraps still worth using?

Only for specific cases. The Fraps download page lists release 3.5.99 dated February 26, 2013, and the vendor page does not say the product is discontinued. It records games using DirectX or OpenGL and shows frame rate, but newer tools get more recent updates.

Can I record on a Mac or Linux?

OBS Studio is the option here that lists macOS 13 or newer and Linux. ShareX is Windows only on the page we read, and Bandicam, Mirillis Action, Fraps, Medal and the two driver apps are PC or Windows products. Mac and Linux players should start with OBS Studio.

What does Mirillis Action cost?

The Mirillis Action homepage offers a free trial download and Buy Mirillis Action options, but the page we opened did not show a price. It lists recording at 120 FPS with low CPU usage and runs on Windows Vista through Windows 11. Check the checkout page for the current figure.
