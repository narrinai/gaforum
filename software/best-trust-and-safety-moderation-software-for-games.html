What are trust and safety moderation software for games?

Trust and safety moderation software for games scans what players say and share, including voice chat, text, usernames, images and reports, and flags harassment, hate and other harmful content. Studios use it to automate first-pass review, enforce a code of conduct and document decisions for regulators.

How we ranked these

Toxic voice chat drives players away faster than most bugs, and regulation such as the EU Digital Services Act and the UK Online Safety Act now asks platforms to show their work. A studio of any size has to choose between building filters, buying an API and hiring a managed review team.

This ranking covers eleven vendors. We read each vendor's home page, pricing page and About page, compared the content types and languages they say they cover, and noted what they leave unstated.

We did not run any detection ourselves, so accuracy numbers appear only as vendor claims. A tool that cannot handle live voice is marked as such, and a vendor that shows no price is reported that way.

Voice, text and image coverage

Toxicity in games lives in live voice, quick text chat, usernames and uploaded images. Few vendors cover all of them. Look at what each one states: some are voice specialists, some process only text, some handle images and video for general platforms.

Match the coverage to where your players actually misbehave, and be wary of assuming a text API can listen to voice.

Pricing you can forecast

Moderation costs scale with usage: hours of audio, requests or monthly seats. A few vendors publish tiers, while most ask for a demo. Compare free allowances, overage rates and minimum monthly commitments, and run the numbers against your peak concurrency rather than your average day.

A fixed license plus usage fees behaves differently from pure pay-as-you-go.

Human review and enforcement workflow

Automation flags content, but someone must decide on bans and appeals. Some vendors sell managed human moderation, others only software with a review dashboard. Ask who reviews edge cases, how decisions are logged and whether enforcement actions can be automated back into your game server.

A review trail matters when a player disputes a penalty.

Regulation and language support

Rules such as the Digital Services Act and the Online Safety Act are on the minds of many studios selling in Europe and the UK. A few vendors state compliance tooling, and others say nothing. Also compare language coverage and whether results are explainable, since a moderator has to understand why a line was flagged before acting on it.

What this list shows

Four of the eleven vendors publish amounts we could read: Modulate (ToxMod tiers from $0 to $20,000 a month), Sightengine (free tier up to $99 a month), Tisane ($0 to $899 a month) and Bodyguard (a license from EUR 3,000 per month). The other seven sell on quote or through demos. Two vendors state a free plan or trial in addition, Modulate's Starter tier and CleanSpeak's 30-day trial.

The list splits into game-specific voice and chat specialists, such as Modulate and GGWP, and general content moderation vendors that serve marketplaces and social apps, such as Hive, Checkstep and Besedo. The general vendors usually offer broader coverage of images and video and more regulatory tooling, while the game specialists understand in-game behavior and enforcement. Because ActiveFence now appears under the Alice name and Hive under Deploy, expect brand changes and check contracts for the entity you are buying from.

How to choose

1. Map where harm happens in your game

Pull your last month of reports and sort by channel: voice lobbies, text chat, usernames, user-made images or streams. A shooter with open voice leans on voice detection; a social sandbox with avatars leans on images and names. Choosing a vendor before this sort tends to leave the biggest channel uncovered.

2. Price a bad month, not a normal one

Ask for quotes at your peak concurrent players and at a launch weekend, including audio hours or request counts. Compare published tiers with quote-only offers on the same volumes. Watch for monthly minimums and overage rates, which turn a cheap pilot into a large bill when a game succeeds.

3. Test enforcement, not only detection

A detector that flags content but cannot feed your ban system adds work. Ask for a pilot on recorded samples with your own policy, and review how flagged items reach moderators, how appeals work and what audit logs exist. Keep a human decision step for bans, since vendor accuracy numbers are claims.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best trust and safety moderation software for games in 2026?

Modulate ranks first for studios with voice chat, since ToxMod is built for game voice and its tiers are published from $0 with 1,500 included hours. GGWP is the better pick when you want text, voice, usernames and reports in one platform plus managed human review, and Bodyguard suits studios that also moderate social channels.

Which tools detect toxic voice chat?

Modulate's ToxMod is the dedicated voice product on this list, and GGWP states voice among its covered channels. Checkstep and Hive list audio moderation, though their pages do not focus on live game voice. Ask for a trial on recorded game audio before committing.

Is there a free moderation tool for a small studio?

Modulate's ToxMod Starter tier is $0 monthly with 1,500 included hours one time, Sightengine has a free tier of 2,000 operations per month, and Tisane has a $0 plan with 50,000 requests. CleanSpeak offers a 30-day trial. These cover tests and small games, not a busy launch.

How do these tools help with the EU Digital Services Act?

Checkstep states compliance tooling for DSA and the UK Online Safety Act, and Tremau offers advisory services on both rules alongside its Nima platform. Tisane says its results are explainable and DSA compliant. None of this replaces legal advice, and each claim comes from the vendor.

Do I still need human moderators?

Yes for bans and appeals. GGWP and Besedo both sell human moderation alongside software, and Checkstep claims a 90% reduction in human moderation costs, which is a vendor figure. Automation handles the first pass; people should make final calls on penalties.

What happened to Perspective API?

Tisane's documentation includes a migration guide for Perspective API, which it describes as sunsetting. We could not verify Perspective API's own page, so it is not ranked. If you rely on it for text scoring, plan a move before it ends.

Can I moderate social channels as well as in-game chat?

Bodyguard lists Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, Twitch and Discord in its dashboard, and GGWP covers Discord. That helps studios whose communities spill out of the game. Check that the connectors support your server's size and language.
