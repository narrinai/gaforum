What is game anti-cheat software?

Game anti-cheat software is a licensed security layer that detects cheats, tampering and exploit tools in an online game, then blocks or bans the offender. Approaches differ: some run protection on the player's machine, some analyze behavior on the server, and some harden the game binary itself. Studios usually integrate a client module, a server component and a ban and appeal workflow.

How we ranked these

Cheating drives honest players away faster than almost any bug, and a small studio rarely has a security team to answer it. This page compares twelve products a studio can license or consult: dedicated game anti-cheat services, publisher-owned systems from Asia, a game security division inside a larger company, and general mobile app protection vendors that games sometimes borrow.

We read each vendor's public homepage and licensing or contact pages, recorded the platforms it names and whether prices exist, and judged fit for independent and mid-size studios. We did not install or attack any of them, and no detection claim on this page is our own measurement.

Several vendors publish very little, so the page says plainly what we could and could not confirm.

Detection approach and what the vendor says about it

Cheat prevention can lean on heuristics and gameplay analysis, on machine learning over player behavior, or on binary hardening that makes tampering harder. Each has different costs for players and for your engineers. We noted how each vendor describes its own method, and flagged cases where the opened pages stay general, so you can ask pointed questions in a vendor call.

Platforms covered, from PC to console and mobile

A cheat protection module written for desktop does not automatically cover a phone or console build. We recorded the platforms each vendor names on its own site. Cross-platform games need a service that names every target, and a vendor whose pages are silent about consoles should be asked for written confirmation before you plan a launch around it.

Licensing model and visible pricing

Anti-cheat is almost always sold by contract. A free service tier, such as one Epic offers, changes the math for an indie studio, while quote-only vendors require a sales conversation before you can budget. We checked each homepage and licensing page for tiers, prices or a contact form, and marked every case where nothing was shown.

Player fairness: appeals and false positives

Banning an innocent customer costs more than missing a cheater, so the workflow around bans matters. We looked for an appeals path, independence from the game publisher and documentation or support listed on the vendor site. A system with no visible way to review a wrong ban pushes that burden onto your community team.

What this list shows

Only one vendor on this list shows a free tier: Easy Anti-Cheat lists a free service level plus paid Core, Fortified and Premier tiers, with prices behind a licensing form. The other eleven show no prices on the pages we opened. Seven of the twelve sell dedicated game protection (Easy Anti-Cheat, BattlEye, Denuvo, Anybrain, XIGNCODE3, nProtect GameGuard and Tencent ACE), while Irdeto is the parent organization behind Denuvo, and four, Appdome, Promon, Guardsquare and Digital.ai, are app security vendors whose pages describe mobile or application hardening more than gameplay cheating.

Geography shapes the choice. BattlEye is registered in Reutlingen, Germany, according to its imprint, which may matter to studios that prefer a European vendor. XIGNCODE3 (WELLBIA), nProtect GameGuard (INCA Internet) and Tencent's ACE come from Korean and Chinese companies, and their sites are in Korean, English or Chinese.

Language, support hours and the regions where each product is used are practical questions, since an anti-cheat vendor becomes a long-term dependency in your launch pipeline.

How to choose

1. Define the cheats you actually face

Aim bots, wallhacks, memory editing, modified clients and mobile emulators call for different defenses. Review your own telemetry and community reports, rank the three costliest abuses, and bring that list to every vendor call. A vendor that answers in general terms about 'advanced protection' has not yet matched its product to your game.

2. Confirm platforms in writing

Take your release platform list, including console and mobile builds, and ask each shortlisted vendor to confirm support for each one by email. Public pages for several tools here mention PC only or give a short product list. A written answer protects your schedule, and it surfaces whether console certification needs additional work.

3. Plan the ban and appeal workflow before integration

Decide who reviews flagged accounts, how long evidence is kept and how a player can appeal. Ask whether the vendor supplies tooling for this or whether your support team builds it. Wrongful bans spread quickly on social media, so design the human process alongside the technical module, not after launch.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best game anti-cheat software in 2026?

Easy Anti-Cheat is the best starting point for most studios because it lists a free service tier, cross-platform coverage and a player appeals path. BattlEye is better if you want an independent, PC-focused service with a German registered company. Anybrain is worth a call if you want AI-based detection across PC, console and mobile.

Is there free anti-cheat for indie games?

Easy Anti-Cheat's licensing page lists a free service tier alongside paid Core, Fortified and Premier tiers. The paid prices are not shown and are arranged through a form. No other vendor on this page shows a free tier in the pages we opened, so most alternatives need a quote.

What is the difference between client-side and server-side anti-cheat?

Client-side protection runs on the player's device to detect tampering, injected code or known cheat tools. Server-side analysis looks at behavior and game state on your servers to flag impossible actions. Many services combine both.

Ask each vendor which parts it supplies and which you must build yourself.

Can mobile app security tools replace game anti-cheat?

Not on their own. Appdome, Promon and Guardsquare describe app hardening for Android and iOS, which makes tampering harder, but their opened pages do not describe gameplay cheat detection. They can complement a game anti-cheat service for mobile builds.

Digital.ai's pages describe application hardening, not games.

Which anti-cheat vendors are European?

BattlEye Innovations e.K. is registered in Reutlingen, Germany. Promon and Guardsquare are app security vendors, but the pages we opened do not state their country, so we cannot confirm either. If European hosting or a European company is a requirement, ask every vendor for its registered entity and data location.

Are Denuvo and Irdeto the same company?

Denuvo by Irdeto is presented under the Irdeto brand, and the denuvo.com domain redirects to Irdeto's video games page. The page lists pre-release protection, piracy protection and cheat prevention. We list both entries because buyers search for each name, but they describe one commercial offer.

How much does anti-cheat software cost?

Most vendors here publish no prices. Easy Anti-Cheat names its tiers without figures, Appdome asks buyers to request a quote by choosing features, and the rest offer contact forms or consultations. Budget by asking for a written quote that states the per-title fee, the integration support included and the cost of console builds.
