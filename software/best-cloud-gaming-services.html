Best Cloud Gaming Services in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

GeForce NOW comes first in our ranking of cloud gaming services, ahead of Xbox Cloud Gaming and Boosteroid. Cloud gaming services run games on remote hardware and stream them to your screen; this ranking compares libraries, billing models and device support.

What are cloud gaming services?

Cloud gaming services run a game on a remote server and send the video to your phone, TV, browser or laptop while your controller input travels back. They differ in what you play: a catalog the service licenses, games you already own in other stores, or a full rented Windows PC.

How we ranked these

Picking a cloud gaming service is mostly a question of where your games come from. Some services stream titles you already own on Steam or Epic, some bundle a rotating catalog into a subscription, and some rent you an entire Windows PC.

Price pages tend to be vague, and several vendors show no plan amounts in text we could read. This ranking is built from vendor home pages, membership and pricing pages and terms of use, opened on the day of review.

We did not run latency tests, so speed claims are the vendors' own and are marked that way. Each service is placed by how well it serves a player who wants a large library, flexible devices and a billing model that is easy to understand.

Services that hide plan prices or limit you to one device type lose ground. Where a number was not published, we say so instead of guessing.

Where the games come from

Check whether the service streams games you already own, ships a licensed catalog, or hands you a blank Windows PC. Ownership-based services such as GeForce NOW and Loudplay let you reuse Steam and Epic purchases. Catalog services such as Blacknut, Antstream and Amazon Luna decide what you can play.

A rented PC like Shadow lets you install anything but you also manage the install yourself. This one choice decides how much you spend on top of the subscription and how often a favorite title disappears.

Devices you actually own

List the screens you want to play on before you compare libraries. Boosteroid runs in a browser or apps on Windows, Android, macOS, iOS and Linux. Blacknut names PC, Mac, phones, tablets and Smart TV.

Antstream names PC, console, mobile and TV. Others, such as Xbox Cloud Gaming and PlayStation Plus, tie the experience to a console ecosystem.

A service that misses your living room TV or your iPhone is a poor fit however large its library.

How the bill is built

Subscriptions, prepaid hours and bundles all appear in this list. Loudplay sells prepaid hour packages on desktop, Parsec lists a monthly or annual Pro plan, Amazon Luna is included with Prime on the opened page, and Xbox and PlayStation sit inside their own memberships. Most vendors showed no plan amounts in readable text, so treat each price as something to confirm on the checkout page.

Look for auto-renewal terms and for whether hours expire.

Speed claims versus proof

Frame rate and resolution caps come from the vendor, not from us. GeForce NOW states 50-Series servers with up to 5K 120 and 360 FPS for Ultimate members. Shadow and Boosteroid say little about specific figures in the text we read.

Latency depends on your distance to a data center and your connection; Loudplay asks for at least 10 Mbps and suggests 30 Mbps or more. Use free trials where they exist before paying for a year.

What this list shows

Reading the ten pages side by side shows a split. Four services stream games you already own or let you install them on a rented machine (GeForce NOW, Shadow, Loudplay, Parsec in its own way), while the rest sell access to a catalog. Only Parsec lists a free trial, and Loudplay is the only one that sells prepaid hours with no auto-renewal.

Seven of the ten gave no plan amount in readable text, which makes price comparison the weakest part of this category.

The trade-offs are practical. Ownership-based streaming protects your purchases but depends on each store being supported and on the game publisher allowing cloud play. Catalog services spare you the purchase but can remove a title when a license ends.

Console-tied services are strongest if you already pay for the membership. Rented PCs offer the most freedom and the most upkeep. Vendor locations are also spread: Loudplay names Armenia, Amazon Luna was served from a Dutch page, and most others published no country in the text we read.

How to choose

1. Audit your existing library

Open Steam, Epic, GOG and your console accounts and count what you own. If you hold a few hundred paid PC titles, an ownership-based service such as GeForce NOW or Loudplay avoids buying anything twice. If your shelf is thin or you mostly play what is new on a platform, a catalog subscription saves money.

Write the number down before you look at any price page.

2. Test on your worst screen

Pick the weakest device you plan to use, usually a hotel Wi-Fi laptop or an older Smart TV, and run a trial or a short purchase there. Boosteroid and Blacknut list many device types, Loudplay is sold in small prepaid packs, and Parsec has a trial. A service that works on that screen will work on the rest.

3. Compare the real monthly cost

Add the subscription, any store purchases you would still need, and the cost of extra hours or tiers. Loudplay hours expire after 30 days, Antstream mentions annual and lifetime options, and Game Pass and PlayStation Plus bundle other benefits you may or may not use. Choose the plan you would keep for a year, not the cheapest first month.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best cloud gaming service in 2026?

GeForce NOW is the best fit for most players because it streams games you already own from stores such as Steam, Epic and GOG, with Performance and Ultimate tiers. Xbox Cloud Gaming is better if you already pay for Game Pass, and Boosteroid is better if you want to play in a browser on Linux, macOS or iOS.

Do I need to buy games again for cloud gaming?

It depends on the service. GeForce NOW and Loudplay stream games from your existing store libraries, so purchases carry over when the publisher allows it. Blacknut, Antstream and Amazon Luna offer their own catalogs, so you play what they license.

Xbox and PlayStation tie access to their memberships.

Can I play cloud games on an iPhone or Smart TV?

Often yes, but check the service. Boosteroid lists iOS and browser play, and Blacknut lists smartphones, tablets and Smart TV. Antstream names PC, console, mobile and TV.

Loudplay clients exist for Windows, macOS and Android only, so it does not cover iPhone or TV.

How fast does my internet need to be?

Vendors publish few numbers. Loudplay says at least 10 Mbps works and recommends 30 Mbps or higher, preferably over Ethernet or 5 GHz Wi-Fi. Other services did not state a minimum in the text we read, so check the vendor's help pages and your own connection before subscribing.

Is Amazon Luna included with Prime?

The Dutch Luna page we opened says Luna needs no downloads or console and is included with Prime. The library rotates, so a game you like may leave. Availability and the included catalog can differ by country, so confirm on your regional Luna page.

What is the difference between a cloud gaming service and a cloud PC?

A cloud gaming service usually gives you a menu of games and manages the install. A cloud PC such as Shadow is a full Windows machine in the cloud where you install launchers and software yourself, which gives more freedom and more setup. Parsec is different again: a remote desktop and streaming app.

Which services publish their prices?

Loudplay lists three prepaid desktop packages, Parsec shows Pro and Teams amounts, and Amazon Luna says it is included with Prime. GeForce NOW, Shadow, Boosteroid, Blacknut, Antstream and PlayStation Plus showed no plan amounts in the text we could read, so check their checkout pages.
