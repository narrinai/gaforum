Best Playtesting Platforms in 2026

What are playtesting platforms?

Playtesting platforms let a studio get real players to try an unfinished game and report what happened. Some recruit testers and record sessions for analysis, some only deliver builds through a store or beta channel, and general research tools run moderated or unmoderated sessions that a team can adapt to games.

How we ranked these

Showing a build to friends tells you little. Strangers get stuck in places you never expected, and a remote session can show it without a lab.

This ranking covers twelve tools that help a studio run those sessions, from a platform built for player research to free delivery channels and general usability services. We read each vendor homepage and pricing page, compared formats, platforms and published prices, and weighed fit for an indie or mid-size studio.

We did not run the tools. Two crowdtesting vendors, Applause and Testlio, do not describe playtesting on the pages we opened, and the entries say so.

Order reflects how directly each tool supports watching players.

Who finds the players

Some services supply testers and some assume you already have them. PlaytestCloud runs a player panel, Userlytics and dscout offer panels or your own participants, Steam Playtest opens a build to Steam users, and Firebase and TestFlight only deliver builds to people you invite. Recruitment decides how soon a first session can happen, so it carried the most weight.

What you can observe

Seeing a player struggle beats reading a survey. PlaytestCloud lists video, audio and touch recording with transcripts, Lookback offers moderated and unmoderated sessions, and Hotjar replays website visits. Delivery tools offer crash data or annotated screenshots at best.

We ranked tools higher when their pages describe recording or live observation of play.

Platforms the build can reach

A platform that cannot run your build is useless. Steam Playtest serves Steam, TestFlight serves Apple devices, Firebase serves iOS and Android, PlaytestCloud lists PC through browser and Steam plus Android and iOS, and console is not listed anywhere we looked. Check this first, because a tool that cannot install your build cannot test it.

Cost and commitment

Budgets for playtests are small. Steam Playtest and Firebase App Distribution are free, Lookback publishes yearly plans from $299, and PlaytestCloud offers a two-player trial. UserTesting, dscout, Applause and Testlio sell through quotes or demos.

Cost visibility mattered, since a studio cannot plan a test round without a price.

    PlaytestCloud

    Player insights platform for recruiting and analyzing playtests

    Why #1: Built for games: recruits a player panel, records sessions and analyzes them, with a free trial of two players.

    Best for
    Mobile and PC studios needing real players
    Pricing
    No plan prices or per-tester rates are shown; a free trial lets you watch two players for up to 30 minutes, and the page mentions special pricing for small studios.
    Free option
    Free trial
    Company
    PlaytestCloud GmbH
    Platforms
    PC (browser and Steam) and mobile on Android and iOS; console is not explicitly listed
    Key features
    AI analysis, Video, audio and touch recording, Transcripts and surveys, Player panel, Moderated and longitudinal playtests

    PlaytestCloud is the one tool here that describes itself around games. It recruits players, records video, audio and touch input, adds transcripts and surveys, and offers single-session, multi-session, longitudinal and moderated formats.

    The page claims results in about 48 hours and names Bethesda, Zynga, Supercell and Rovio among customers. PC (browser and Steam) and Android and iOS are covered.

    A trial that lets you watch two players for 30 minutes is a cheap way to judge quality before paying.

    Where it falls short

    No plan prices or per-tester rates are published on the page we opened, and console is not listed. The 48-hour turnaround is a vendor claim we could not verify.

    Skip it if

    Console-first teams, or studios that only want to hand out builds to friends, should look at Steam Playtest or TestFlight instead.

  2. 2

    Steam Playtest

    Free Steamworks feature for running a playtest on Steam

    Why #2: A no-cost way to put a build in front of real Steam users, with limited or open signup and key controls.

    Best for
    PC games already in Steamworks
    Pricing
    Valve says Steam Playtest is completely free for customers and developers; charging for access is prohibited and keys may not be sold.
    Free option
    Free
    Company
    Valve
    Platforms
    Steam; the page does not explicitly name PC, and mentions Steam desktop and mobile clients for notifications
    Key features
    Limited and open signup, Steam keys for playtest access, Friend invites, Playable or not playable status, Store-page signup controls

    Steam Playtest gives developers a free route to gather playtest data from the platform where a PC game will launch. Signup can be limited or open, keys control access, and friend invites let a player bring one to three friends if they have been friends for at least 30 days.

    Developers can request more than 50,000 keys, though Valve reviews that case by case, and release state override keys cap at 2,500 per app. It costs nothing, which earns second place behind a dedicated research service.

    Where it falls short

    It supplies testers and a delivery channel, not recordings, surveys or analysis: you must collect feedback yourself. Valve can reject large key requests, and you need a Steamworks account.

    Skip it if

    Teams that want session recordings and structured feedback should use PlaytestCloud, and mobile-only games cannot use Steam at all.

    • partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest

What this list shows

Counting the twelve entries, two are free to use (Steam Playtest, Firebase App Distribution), and itch.io is free for visitors. Only Lookback publishes plan prices in dollars on the pages we opened. Four vendors, UserTesting, dscout, Applause and Testlio, sell by quote or demo, and Userlytics lists plan names without figures.

Only PlaytestCloud and Steam Playtest present themselves around games; the rest are general research or testing tools.

The trade-off is depth against price. A free channel such as Steam Playtest or Firebase gets builds to people but leaves the observing to you. A panel service such as PlaytestCloud adds recruitment and recordings at a cost the vendor does not publish.

General research platforms are flexible and often well documented, yet they assume websites and apps, so game footage, controllers and long sessions may need workarounds. Many studios combine a free delivery channel with a paid round of observed sessions before launch.

How to choose

1. Name the question you need answered

Decide whether you want to know if players understand the tutorial, if the loop is fun after an hour, or if the build crashes. Comprehension and fun call for observed sessions, so shortlist PlaytestCloud, Lookback or Userlytics. Crashes and install problems call for delivery tools such as TestFlight or Firebase, which are cheaper.

2. Match the tool to your build platform

List where the build runs: Steam, iOS, Android, browser or console. Use Steam Playtest for Steam, TestFlight for Apple, Firebase for Android and iOS, and confirm console options with a vendor because none of the pages we opened describe them. Ask each shortlisted vendor to confirm the platform in writing before paying.

3. Run a small pilot before a big order

Start with PlaytestCloud's free trial of two players, a Lookback trial of 5 sessions or a Steam Playtest with limited signup. Check whether the feedback changes a decision you were unsure about. Only then buy a larger panel or a yearly plan, and keep five to eight players per round so results are readable.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best playtesting platform in 2026?

PlaytestCloud is our first pick because it is built around games: it recruits players, records video, audio and touch, and offers a free trial. Steam Playtest is better for free tests on Steam, and Lookback is better when you recruit players yourself and want published prices.

Is Steam Playtest really free?

Yes, Valve says it is completely free for customers and developers. Charging for access is prohibited, and keys cannot be sold or bundled. Valve may reject requests for more than 50,000 keys, and release state override keys are limited to 2,500 per app.

How many players do I need for a playtest?

For observed sessions, five to eight players per round usually surface the main usability issues, and further rounds after fixes help more than one large round. PlaytestCloud's trial lets you watch two players for up to 30 minutes, which is enough to judge the format.

Can I playtest a console game remotely?

Console playtests depend on platform holder programs and hardware, and none of the pages we opened describe console support. PlaytestCloud lists PC and mobile and says console is not explicitly listed, so ask the vendor.

What is the cheapest way to get feedback on a mobile build?

Firebase App Distribution is a no cost tool for sending iOS and Android builds to testers, and TestFlight supports up to 10,000 external testers on Apple devices. Both deliver builds but do not recruit players or record sessions.

Should I use a general user testing tool for a game?

It can work for menus, onboarding and store pages, since Lookback, Userlytics and dscout run moderated or unmoderated sessions. Their opened pages do not mention games, so test whether screen capture handles your build before committing to a plan.

What is the difference between playtesting and QA testing?

Playtesting asks players whether the game is understandable and fun; QA testing looks for defects. Applause and Testlio focus on defects and device coverage, while PlaytestCloud and Steam Playtest centre on player behavior. Most studios need both before launch.
