What are mobile game ad networks and mediation platforms?

A mobile game ad network buys and sells advertising inside apps, while a mediation platform sits above several networks and runs an auction so each ad slot goes to the highest bidder. Rewarded video, where players choose to watch an ad for an in-game reward, is the format most mobile games build around.

How we ranked these

Choosing an ad stack for a free-to-play game means choosing both a mediation layer and the networks behind it. We read each vendor's product page, documentation and legal footers, and compared what they name: formats, platforms, bidding support, ownership and whether fees are shown.

We did not run integrations or measure fill rates, so no revenue claim here is ours, and every figure is the vendor's. Mediation platforms rank first for a small or mid-size studio because they make switching networks cheap, followed by networks with rewarded and playable formats, then offerwalls, marketing platforms and acquisition-first tools.

AppLovin MAX is missing: the address we opened returned a general AppLovin advertising page that did not describe MAX, so we could not verify it.

Mediation, bidding and network coverage

Find out whether the product runs an auction or only supplies demand. Unity LevelPlay and AdMob describe mediation, AdMob adds bidding across networks, Unity Ads and Meta Audience Network document bidding, and Pangle is a network. A network gives you one buyer, mediation lets many compete for each impression, so most games need both.

Rewarded video and playable formats

Check which formats each vendor lists. Pangle names rewarded video, interstitial, native, banner, playable and a beta app open format. Mintegral offers tools to build playables.

Tapjoy and Digital Turbine list offerwalls, a different rewarded model where players complete tasks. Rewarded video suits games with currencies, while offerwalls fit particular economies.

Ownership and where the product is going

Several names changed hands. The page says LoopMe acquired Chartboost in 2024, Tapjoy now lives on unity.com, liftoff.io redirects to liftoff.ai, and Mintegral names Mobvista as its parent. Unity owns LevelPlay and Unity Ads, and hosts Tapjoy.

Ownership affects contracts, support and whether you depend on one company for both mediation and demand.

Visible terms and pricing

No vendor here prints a revenue share. AdMob shows an earnings calculator with a no-guarantee disclaimer, and Moloco says pricing is performance-based. Everything else asks you to sign up or talk to sales.

Ask for payment terms, minimum payout, data rights and exit conditions in writing before building around any single partner.

What this list shows

Across the twelve tools ranked, none states a fee or revenue share in the pages we opened, and only Moloco describes its pricing basis. Two belong to Unity Technologies (LevelPlay and Unity Ads) and Tapjoy is now hosted on unity.com, while one is run by Google, one sits under LoopMe and one names Mobvista as parent. Pangle is tied to TikTok for Business.

Only AdMob and Digital Turbine publish adoption figures (1 million+ apps; 82K apps), and both are vendor claims. Meta's page documents Android, iOS and Unity setup, which are the platforms it names.

The practical trade-off is concentration versus control. Mediation reduces dependence on one buyer, but the largest mediation layers are owned by companies that also run ad networks, so a studio should check how bids are treated. Marketing-first platforms such as Moloco and Liftoff sell growth as much as monetization, and InMobi's homepage barely mentions games.

For a new release, a free mediation console plus two or three rewarded sources is a manageable starting set.

How to choose

1. Start with the mediation layer

Pick Unity LevelPlay if you build in Unity and want Editor-level integration, or AdMob if you already use Google's tools and want analytics and bidding in one console. Create a test account, integrate one rewarded placement in a development build, and note how long that takes.

2. Add two or three demand sources

Choose networks that document the formats you use. For rewarded video and playables, Pangle and Mintegral are on this list; for bidding documentation, Meta and Unity Ads. Keep the number small, so you can see which source performs and keep your SDK size and crash surface down.

3. Settle terms before scaling spend

Ask each vendor for payout terms, revenue share, data rights and how bids are treated in mediation. Where the page states no price, get the answer in writing. Only after that should you grow acquisition with a platform such as Liftoff, Moloco or Digital Turbine.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best mobile game ad network and mediation platform in 2026?

Unity LevelPlay is the best fit for Unity studios because it integrates from the Editor and is built for mediation. Google AdMob is better for teams that want broad adoption, bidding and analytics in one console. Pangle is a good extra source when rewarded video and playable formats matter.

What is the difference between an ad network and mediation?

A network supplies ads and pays you for impressions or actions, while mediation runs an auction across several networks and picks the highest bid for each slot. Pangle is a network, Unity LevelPlay is mediation, and AdMob offers mediation together with its own ad demand.

Is AppLovin MAX included in this ranking?

No. The address we opened for AppLovin MAX redirected to a general AppLovin advertising page that did not describe MAX, its pricing or its platforms, so we could not verify it. We list only tools whose product pages we could read.

Which formats work best for games?

Rewarded video is the format most mobile games centre on, and Pangle lists it alongside interstitial, native, banner and playable ads. Offerwalls from Tapjoy and Digital Turbine suit some game economies. Vendors give no performance figures we can compare, so test formats in your own game.

Do any of these tools publish their revenue share?

None of the twelve states a revenue share on the pages we opened. AdMob shows an earnings calculator with a no-guarantee disclaimer, and Moloco says pricing is performance-based. Ask for terms in writing, including payment schedule and minimum payout.

Does Meta Audience Network work in Unity?

Its documentation lists setup for Android, iOS and Unity, and describes App Bidding as an open auction with several demand sources. The page states no pricing, and it is documentation for integrators rather than a product overview.

Can one company own both mediation and the network?

Yes. Unity owns LevelPlay and Unity Ads and hosts Tapjoy on its site, and Google runs AdMob with its own demand. That can simplify contracts, but you should ask how bids from rival networks are treated in the auction.
