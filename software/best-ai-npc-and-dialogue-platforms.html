What are AI NPC and dialogue platforms?

An AI NPC and dialogue platform lets a non-player character speak, answer players and remember context, using language models, speech synthesis and animation, or classic branching scripts. The category covers conversational engines such as Convai, authoring languages such as ink and Yarn Spinner, voice services, and tools that generate surrounding assets.

How we ranked these

Developers asking for an AI NPC tool usually mean one of three different things: a character that improvises, a writer's tool that structures planned scenes, or the art and voice that surround both. This ranking keeps those jobs in one list and orders them by how directly each gets a talking character into a shipped game.

We read vendor homepages, documentation, pricing pages and license terms, then compared published prices, supported engines and stated limits. Nothing here comes from running the products ourselves, and vendor claims are marked as such.

Conversational platforms sit at the top because they deliver the improvising character most readers want. Authoring tools follow, since many games are better off with written lines.

Asset generators close the list because they supply content around the character instead of the conversation itself.

Does the character actually converse?

Separate live conversation from authored branches. Convai and NVIDIA ACE generate responses with models, while ink, Yarn Spinner and articy:draft X play lines a writer composed. Pixel Crushers sits between them, adding a model addon to a scripted base.

Decide whether players will ask open questions or choose from options, because that single answer removes half the list. Asset generators such as Scenario, Layer and Ludo.ai do not converse at all.

Engine plugins and platforms

Check whether your engine has first-party support. Convai lists Unity and Unreal plugins plus web plugins; Yarn Spinner covers Unity, Godot and Unreal; ink offers Unity natively and Unreal through a community container; Dialogue System for Unity is Unity only; articy:draft X itself runs on Windows and macOS. A tool without a plugin for your engine costs integration hours that never show on a price sheet.

How the bill grows with players

Per-turn and per-character billing scales with player counts, while a license does not. Convai draws credits on every turn and session. Inworld quotes USD 25 per 1M characters for TTS-2.

ElevenLabs sells monthly credit tiers. ink costs nothing under MIT. NVIDIA ACE shows no price at all.

Estimate lines spoken per session before you pick a model-backed option.

Voice, latency and where inference runs

Spoken NPCs add speech-to-text, a language model and text-to-speech to each exchange, and every hop adds delay. NVIDIA ACE can run models on the player's GPU, which removes network round trips but limits hardware. Cloud services such as Inworld and ElevenLabs keep the player's machine free of that load.

Read the supported language list for voices and check whether a commercial license is included.

    Convai

    Talking AI characters with Unity and Unreal plugins

    Why #1: Closest to a finished NPC product here: characters, engine plugins and a credit meter in one place, with a free tier to start on.

    Best for
    Voiced NPCs in 3D worlds
    Pricing
    Billing is credit-based: every conversation turn, connected session and processed file draws from the plan's credit balance, and the pricing page mentions free-tier access for developers.
    Free option
    Free
    Company
    Convai
    Platforms
    Unity SDK, Unreal Engine plugin, web plugins
    Key features
    AI characters, Convai Playground, Avatar Studio, Unity SDK, Unreal Engine plugin, Core API

    Convai is built around the character itself rather than the model underneath. You define who an NPC is in its Playground, drop the Unity or Unreal plugin into a project, and the character talks.

    Documentation also covers web plugins and a modding framework, which helps if a small team wants one vendor for prototype and shipping build. Because billing is metered per conversation turn, you can estimate cost from expected play sessions before committing.

    Where it falls short

    Plan amounts sit behind a JavaScript pricing page that we could not read, so cost per player cannot be checked here. Credits draw down on every turn and session, which makes budgeting harder for games with long, chatty play.

    Skip it if

    Teams that want fully scripted, offline dialogue should look at Yarn Spinner or ink, and those who need on-device inference should read the NVIDIA ACE entry.

    Speech, language and animation models for in-game characters

    Why #2: The deepest technical stack of the group, spanning speech, language and face animation, though it asks you to assemble the parts yourself.

    Best for
    Technical teams with GPU budget
    Pricing
    No price is shown on the ACE for Games page, which offers Get Started and Notify Me buttons instead.
    Free option
    Unknown
    Company
    NVIDIA
    Platforms
    Cloud and on-device; Unreal Engine 5 for Audio2Face-3D
    Key features
    Small speech, language and animation models for games, Cloud and on-device inference, NVIDIA In-Game Inferencing (NVIGI) plugins, Agentic loop and RAG guides for custom engines, Audio2Face-3D in Unreal Engine 5

    ACE bundles small speech, language and animation models that can run in the cloud or on the player's own GPU, plus In-Game Inferencing plugins that schedule model work next to rendering. The page names Total War: PHARAOH, PUBG Co-Player Characters and inZOI as partner examples, and offers agentic loop and retrieval guides for custom engines.

    A studio with graphics engineers gets the most control over latency and running cost here.

    Where it falls short

    This is a toolkit, not a character editor, so design, memory and dialogue tooling are yours to build. There is no price on the page, and on-device models only help players whose hardware can carry the load.

    Skip it if

    Small teams without engine programmers will move faster with Convai or Inworld, where the plumbing is already assembled.

    Free option

    Key features

    ink treats text as the main material and slips logic in as markup, which keeps long branching scripts readable. inkle built it as middleware for its own releases and says millions of words have been written in it. What this list shows

Of the eleven tools ranked, five publish price figures we could read (Inworld, ElevenLabs, Scenario, Ludo.ai and the free ink), while NVIDIA ACE, Pixel Crushers, Layer and articy:draft X leave amounts out of the pages we opened. Convai's plans exist but render through JavaScript. Three are free or open source in some form: ink under MIT, Yarn Spinner with a free tier, and articy:draft X with a free version.

Only articy (Germany) and Layer (founded in San Francisco) state a location on the pages we read.

The list also shows how loose the label is. Only four entries produce live, model-driven speech; four more author conversations; three generate assets. Inworld now describes itself as voice and inference for consumer applications, so its place reflects what its pages say today.

The practical trade-off is control versus improvisation: authored dialogue is predictable and cheap, generated dialogue is flexible but metered and harder to moderate. Charisma.ai was left out because its web address now opens a film production studio page. Ready Player Me could not be opened.

How to choose

1. Write down what players may say

List five example exchanges you want in the game. If every one fits a fixed choice, an authoring tool such as Yarn Spinner or ink is enough and costs nothing in usage fees. If players will type or speak free text, you need a model-backed option like Convai or NVIDIA ACE, and you should add moderation to your plan.

2. Match the tool to your engine

Open the plugin list for your engine first. Unity teams can use almost anything here; Unreal teams should check Convai, NVIDIA ACE, Yarn Spinner and ink; Godot teams have Yarn Spinner. Install the free or trial version, and see how long it takes to get one character talking in a blank scene.

3. Price one hour of play

Multiply spoken lines per hour by your expected players, then apply each vendor's unit price: characters for speech, tokens for language, credits for turns. Compare that figure to a flat license. Where the vendor shows no price, ask for a written quote before building on the service.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI NPC and dialogue platform in 2026?

Convai is the best fit for most teams, because it packages characters, voices and Unity and Unreal plugins with a free tier. NVIDIA ACE is better when you have graphics engineers and want models on the player's GPU. Inworld is the better choice when you only need a voice layer with clearly published unit prices.

Can an NPC use a language model without a cloud service?

Yes, in some setups. NVIDIA ACE describes cloud and on-device models, and the Pixel Crushers OpenAI addon says it works with local models run through Ollama, such as Llama 3.2 and 3.3 and Gemma. Local inference depends on the player's hardware and adds memory use.

Is branching dialogue still worth using next to AI?

Yes. Yarn Spinner and ink give writers exact control over plot-critical scenes, and ink is free under the MIT license. Many studios keep story beats authored and add model-driven chatter for minor characters, which Dialogue System for Unity supports through its addon.

Which tools here work in Godot?

Yarn Spinner is the one entry with Godot support in both C# and GDScript. Convai lists Unity, Unreal and web plugins instead. For other options, check that a vendor documents a Godot integration before committing, and consider ink's plain JSON export.

How much do AI voices for game characters cost?

Published figures vary. Inworld lists TTS-2 at USD 25 per 1M characters. ElevenLabs runs from a free 10k-credit tier through $22 Creator and $99 Pro plans up to Enterprise quotes.

Convai bills credits per turn, and NVIDIA ACE shows no price.

Do asset generators like Scenario or Layer make NPCs talk?

No. Scenario, Layer and Ludo.ai generate art, 3D, music and sound effects, and some list voice cloning, but they do not run a conversation. Use them to produce portraits and props, then connect a conversational tool for speech.

What should I check before shipping AI dialogue to players?

Confirm the commercial license, data handling terms and moderation controls. Inworld states GDPR and SOC 2 Type II on its plans. Read each vendor's terms on generated content, and plan fallbacks for when a service is slow or offline.
