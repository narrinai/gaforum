Parsec Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

Parsec is a remote desktop and streaming app for connecting to work or games from anywhere; its pricing page shows a Pro plan at $9.99 or $8.33 and a Teams plan at $35 or $30, depending on billing. Alternatives matter when latency behavior, licensing or the use case differs: some tools are open source, some come from a store you already use, and some are built for IT support instead of games.

This page sorts ten of them by what they do best, using only vendor pages we opened. See the remote play and game streaming software ranking too.

Why this order: we opened every vendor's own pages on the review date and placed the products by fit, not by popularity or payment. When to replace Parsec

  • Parsec is a subscription product with a Teams plan, while Moonlight and Sunshine are described as open source.
  • Steam players may prefer Steam Remote Play since it lives inside the store they already use.
  • Parsec is aimed at low-latency remote desktop, whereas AnyDesk, TeamViewer and Splashtop target support and IT teams.
  • Those who want to rent hardware instead of using their own PC can look at GeForce NOW or Shadow.
  • A free Google service, Chrome Remote Desktop, covers basic access to your own computer.

Our picks

  1. 1

    Moonlight

    Open source game streaming client

    Free option
    Open source

    Moonlight is an open source game streaming client that plays your PC games remotely, up to 4K with HDR and up to 120 FPS with its PC client. It runs on many clients, from Windows, macOS and Linux to iOS, Android and Xbox.

    It is an open source implementation of NVIDIA's GameStream protocol, so you also need a host on the PC. That setup is more manual than Parsec.

    Checked on moonlight-stream.org

    Visit Moonlight

  2. 2

    Sunshine

    Self-hosted host for Moonlight

    Free option
    Open source

    Sunshine is the host half: a self-hosted game stream host for Moonlight, licensed under GPL-3.0. Pair it with Moonlight and you have a stack you control.

    It is for people comfortable with self-hosting. The pages we read did not list supported platforms.

    Checked on github.com

    Visit Sunshine

  3. 3

    Steam Link

    Streaming Steam games

    Free option
    Unknown

    Steam Remote Play is Valve's way to stream your Steam games to other devices, linked to the Steam mobile app. It fits Steam-only households, with no separate account.

    It is not a general remote desktop.

    Checked on store.steampowered.com

    Visit Steam Link

  4. 4

    GeForce NOW

    Rented RTX servers

    Free option
    Unknown

    GeForce NOW streams games from connected stores on NVIDIA servers, so you do not need a strong home PC. Ultimate offers 50-Series servers with up to 5K 120 resolution.

    It is the pick when hardware at home is the limit. You can only play titles available through its connected stores.

    Checked on nvidia.com

    Visit GeForce NOW

  5. 5

    AnyDesk

    Remote support with licences

    Free option
    Unknown

    AnyDesk is remote desktop software installed as a client to control other computers, with licences from Solo to Ultimate. It leans toward support and administration.

    Choose it for office use rather than gaming.

    Checked on support.anydesk.com

    Visit AnyDesk

  6. 6

    Splashtop

    Business remote access

    Free option
    Free trial

    Splashtop offers remote access for individuals and small teams, remote support for IT teams and enterprise access with SSO. It publishes clear plan prices and a free trial.

    It is a fit for managed business access. Gaming is not its stated focus.

    Checked on splashtop.com

    Visit Splashtop

  7. 7

    TeamViewer

    Enterprise remote workplace

    Free option
    Free trial

    TeamViewer describes itself as a digital workplace platform with products including TeamViewer Remote, Tensor, DEX, Frontline and ONE. It suits large organizations that need support and device management.

    Individuals will find it broad compared with Parsec.

    Checked on teamviewer.com

    Visit TeamViewer

  8. 8

    Jump Desktop

    Remote desktop for Apple users

    Free option
    Unknown

    Jump Desktop describes itself as fast, secure remote desktop for any computer, with separate paths for individuals and teams, a Teams security paper and enterprise licensing. It is a neutral alternative if you want a simple connection tool.

    Verify the current prices on its site.

    Checked on jumpdesktop.com

    Visit Jump Desktop

  9. 9

    Chrome Remote Desktop

    Free basic access

    Free option
    Unknown

    Chrome Remote Desktop is Google's service for reaching your home or work computer or sharing your screen, from a phone, tablet or another computer. It suits occasional access.

    It does not advertise game streaming features.

    Checked on remotedesktop.google.com

    Visit Chrome Remote Desktop

  10. 10

    Shadow

    A cloud Windows PC

    Free option
    Unknown

    Shadow gives you a full Windows PC in the cloud that you can launch in a browser or on devices. That replaces the need to host your own machine.

    It differs from Parsec in that you rent the computer instead of connecting to yours.

    Checked on shadow.tech

    Visit Shadow

Making the call

Begin with where the games run. On your own PC, compare Parsec with Moonlight plus Sunshine, or with Steam Remote Play if you only play Steam titles.

If your home hardware is weak, rent instead with GeForce NOW or Shadow. If the need is office access or IT support, look at Splashtop, AnyDesk or TeamViewer.

The open source route has no license fee but costs setup time, so give it an afternoon before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

Which Parsec alternative is open source?

Moonlight is an open source client, and Sunshine is a self-hosted host for it under GPL-3.0. Together they cover the same job as Parsec without a subscription.

What is the simplest option for Steam games?

Steam Remote Play is built into the Steam ecosystem and links to the Steam mobile app. It does not cover non-Steam desktop use.

What if my PC is not powerful enough?

GeForce NOW streams from NVIDIA servers and Shadow rents a full Windows PC. Both move the hardware into the cloud.

Which tool publishes clear prices?

Splashtop lists plans from $6 per month billed annually, and Parsec shows $9.99 or $8.33 for Pro. Many others kept prices out of the text we could read.

Is there a free option for basic remote access?

Chrome Remote Desktop lets you reach your computer remotely and share your screen. It does not advertise gaming features.
