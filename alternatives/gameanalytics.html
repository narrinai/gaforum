GameAnalytics Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

GameAnalytics is a game analytics platform built around AnalyticsIQ, PipelineIQ and MarketIQ, with SDKs and integrations for mobile, Roblox, VR/AR and PC/console titles. Studios that outgrow its dashboards usually want finer segmentation: cutting players by behavior, cohort and funnel step, then acting on the result.

This page lists ten tools that cover that ground, some as general product analytics, some as live-game backends, one as market data. Each pick says what you gain and what you give up compared with GameAnalytics.

How this list is ordered: by our editorial judgment of fit for the reader named above, based on vendor documentation, pricing and license pages opened on the review date. Why teams look beyond GameAnalytics

  • Segmentation depth: teams that want to slice cohorts by arbitrary event properties often move to a general product analytics tool built around that workflow.
  • Pricing visibility: the opened GameAnalytics pricing page showed an Overview plan and a Startup program for VC-backed studios, but no readable figures, which makes budgeting hard.
  • Backend overlap: studios that also need cloud save, economy or remote config sometimes prefer analytics bundled with a live-game backend.
  • Experimentation: running A/B tests and feature flags next to the analytics, in one product, is a common reason to switch.
  • Self-hosting and data ownership: some teams want to keep event data in their own warehouse or run the stack themselves.
  • Marketing data: studios focused on user acquisition may want attribution and cost aggregation rather than in-game metrics alone.

The alternatives

  1. 1

    Mixpanel

    Best for: Event funnels and cohorts

    Pricing
    Free plan up to 1M events per month; Growth starts at $0 with volume discounts; Enterprise by sales.
    Free option
    Free plan

    Mixpanel is a product analytics tool with session replay, experiments and feature flags, so it covers the segmentation angle directly. The free tier is free forever with unlimited seats and 1M events per month, which is enough to prototype a game's event schema.

    You gain flexible cohort and funnel analysis; you give up the game-specific benchmarks and the game-focused framing that GameAnalytics ships with. Expect to design your own event taxonomy.

    Sources checked
    • Mixpanel's free plan is free forever and includes up to 1M events per month and 10K session replays per month. mixpanel.com/pricing
    • Mixpanel describes itself as a product intelligence platform combining analytics, session replay, experiments, feature flags and AI insights. mixpanel.com/home

  2. 2

    Amplitude

    Best for: Behavioral analytics at scale

    Pricing
    Free plan with 2M events per month; Growth from $0; Enterprise by sales.
    Free option
    Free plan

    Amplitude pairs product analytics with session replay, experimentation and feature management. Its free plan allows 2M events a month, double Mixpanel's allowance, and startups under $10M in funding with fewer than 20 employees get a free year of Growth.

    The trade is that it is a general product tool, so game concepts such as sessions per level or soft-currency sinks need to be modeled by your team rather than arriving as defaults.

    Sources checked
    • Amplitude's free plan includes 2M events per month forever. amplitude.com/pricing
    • Startups with under $10M in funding and fewer than 20 employees receive one free year on Amplitude's Growth plan. amplitude.com/pricing

    Visit AmplitudeSee its ranking entry

    PostHog

    Best for: One stack for events and replays

    Pricing
    Free monthly allowances on every product, then usage-based pricing.
    Free option
    Free plan

    PostHog ingests analytics, errors, replays and business data in one place, and its pricing page gives every product a monthly free allowance before usage-based billing starts. That suits small teams that want replays and analytics without separate contracts.

    You gain breadth and a single bill; you give up the mobile-game focus. Check how its SDKs fit your engine before committing, because the opened pages did not list game engines.

    Sources checked
    • PostHog's pricing page says it has generous monthly free tiers on every product, with usage-based pricing after that. posthog.com/pricing
    • PostHog describes itself as a product context layer that ingests analytics, errors, replays and business data for teams and agents. posthog.com

    You gain tight engine integration and fewer vendors; you give up neutrality, since the stack is aimed at Unity developers. The page names Subway Surfers and MARVEL SNAP as live game case studies.

    Firebase

    Best for: Mobile apps on Google Cloud

    Pricing
    $300 in credit on creating a Billing Account; no plan figures on the homepage.
    Free option
    Free trial

    Firebase is a Google-backed set of services whose SDKs cover Apple, Android, Web, Flutter, Unity and C++. Teams already shipping a mobile game on Google Cloud can add analytics without a new vendor.

    You gain engine coverage and Google infrastructure; you give up game-specific reporting and need to check current plan limits yourself, since the opened homepage showed only the $300 credit offer and no Spark or Blaze figures.

    Sources checked
    • The homepage lists SDK platforms including Apple, Android, Web, Flutter, Unity and C++. firebase.google.com
    • The homepage offers $300 in credit when you create a Billing Account, with no Spark or Blaze plan figures shown. firebase.google.com

    Game Refinery

    Best for: Mobile market and feature research

    Pricing
    Access is offered for free; trial by contact; no paid prices shown.
    Free option
    Free

    Game Refinery is a different kind of alternative: it is a mobile game intelligence dataset covering live events, gameplay mechanics, player motivations and monetization strategies, plus a Live Events Tracker. It tells you what competitors do rather than what your players do.

    Use it beside an in-game analytics tool, not instead of one. The site describes the company as a Liftoff company.

    Sources checked
    • GameRefinery describes itself as the market's most comprehensive dataset for mobile games, covering live events, gameplay mechanics, player motivations and monetization strategies. www.gamerefinery.com
    • The GameRefinery homepage offers access for free, and a contact option for a trial. www.gamerefinery.com

How to pick between them

Start with the question you cannot answer today. If it is about behavior inside the game, shortlist Mixpanel, Amplitude or PostHog and compare their free event allowances (1M, 2M and usage-based) against your daily active players.

If it is about acquisition, Tenjin is the narrower fit. If you need the backend that acts on the segments, look at PlayFab, Metaplay or Unity Gaming Services.

Game Refinery and Solsten answer market and motivation questions, so treat them as add-ons. See the full game analytics ranking for the rest.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free tool with more segmentation than GameAnalytics?

Mixpanel, Amplitude and PostHog all list free usage on their pricing pages: 1M events per month for Mixpanel, 2M for Amplitude and monthly allowances on every PostHog product. Whether that is enough depends on your player count and how many events each session fires, so estimate that before you migrate.

Can I keep GameAnalytics and add another tool?

Yes. Many studios run an in-game analytics tool next to an attribution or market tool, since Tenjin and Game Refinery answer acquisition and competitor questions that event analytics does not. Keep one source of truth for core retention numbers to avoid conflicting dashboards.

Which alternative suits a Unity mobile game?

Unity Gaming Services lists Analytics, A/B testing and Cloud Save on one page, and Firebase lists Unity among its SDK platforms. Both reduce integration work. Compare their pricing yourself, as neither opened page showed plan figures.

Do these tools publish prices?

Some do. Tenjin states plans from $200 per month, and Mixpanel and Amplitude publish free tiers. Solsten, Game Refinery's paid options and Metaplay's Pro tier require a conversation.

Treat unpublished prices as a sales process and ask for a quote early.

What about a game on Roblox or console?

GameAnalytics lists Roblox, VR/AR and PC/console among its supported areas. Before leaving, check each candidate's SDK list against your platform, because the general tools here describe web and mobile more clearly than console.
