itch.io Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

itch.io is an online marketplace and community where creators publish indie games, game assets and tools, and where players browse by tag, find free games and follow game jams. The site also offers a desktop app for installing and managing purchases.

Developers who outgrow it, or who want a different audience, usually look for a second storefront, not a replacement. This page sorts eight options by what they are for: PC storefronts, bundles, browser portals and platform stores, using only what each vendor says on pages we opened.

About the order: it reflects our own reading of each vendor's documentation, prices and license terms, weighed for the reader this page is written for. Reasons to leave itch.io

  • Players who live inside one launcher often look for games in that launcher first, so a PC storefront with its own wishlist and discovery queue reaches a different crowd than a tag-browse marketplace.
  • A studio with a browser game may want a portal that handles traffic, QA and monetization, which itch.io's open upload model does not promise.
  • Teams that sell a finished game for a fixed price sometimes prefer a curated store, where the vendor decides what appears and when.
  • Bundle-based discovery, where a game is sold alongside others on a pay-what-you-want basis, is a different sales mechanic from a standing store page.
  • Console and device ecosystems have their own storefronts, and a studio that wants a Fire tablet or Xbox presence needs to be listed there.

The options, in order

  1. 1

    Steam

    PC storefront with community tools

    Free option
    Unknown

    Steam is Valve's digital game store, and its menus tie the store to discovery queues, wishlists, Discussions, Workshop, Market and Broadcasts. Next to itch.io's tag browsing, that is a more built-in audience loop for a PC release.

    What you give up is the open, publish-anything feel; the pages we read say nothing about submission requirements, so check Valve's developer documentation for fees and review steps.

    Checked on store.steampowered.com

    Visit Steam

  2. 2

    GOG

    DRM-free PC releases

    Free option
    Unknown

    GOG sells classic and new PC games for Windows, Mac and Linux, DRM-free, which suits itch.io creators who already believe in releasing without copy protection. The shopper base is oriented to owning a game outright.

    The pages we read do not describe how a small developer gets listed, so contact GOG before planning a launch around it.

    Checked on gog.com

    Visit GOG

  3. 3

    Humble Bundle

    Pay-what-you-want bundles

    Free option
    None

    Humble Bundle sells limited-time collections of games, books and software where buyers pick the price and where part of the money goes to charity. The about page says more than 47,000,000 bundles were sold.

    A bundle trades per-copy revenue for reach, which is a sales mechanic itch.io also supports through its own bundles, but with a much larger shopper pool on the Humble side.

    Checked on humblebundle.com

    Visit Humble Bundle

  4. 4

    CrazyGames

    Publishing HTML5 browser games

    Free option
    Unknown

    CrazyGames' developer portal lets creators publish, manage and monetize browser games, and it names Unity WebGL, Phaser, Construct, Cocos and Godot as supported engines. It claims a reach of 20,000,000 players.

    If your itch.io page already offers a browser build, this is a second home where the game is monetized, though the revenue terms need a direct read.

    Checked on developer.crazygames.com

    Visit CrazyGames

  5. 5

    Poki

    Web games with playtesting support

    Free option
    Free

    Poki describes itself as a web gaming platform with 100 million monthly players, and its developer page lists free playtesting, in-house QA, player acquisition, brand protection and monetization. Developers apply to be showcased.

    For a small team, the QA and feedback services are the notable difference from a self-serve upload page.

    Checked on developers.poki.com

    Visit Poki

  6. 6

    GameDistribution

    Distributing HTML5 games to websites

    Free option
    Unknown

    GameDistribution offers HTML5 games for websites and messengers, plus a white label gaming solution and direct game integration. Its about page gives a Schiphol-Rijk, Netherlands address and an Azerion copyright line.

    The model is syndication: your game appears on other publishers' sites, which differs from a storefront where players come to you.

    Checked on gamedistribution.com

    Visit GameDistribution

  7. 7

    Amazon Appstore

    Fire TV and Fire tablet reach

    Free option
    Free

    Amazon's developer portal says developers can test and publish apps and games for Fire OS, Android, iOS, PC, Mac and cross platform, and that apps reach customers in over 236 countries and territories. It suits a mobile or living-room game that itch.io's desktop-leaning audience will never see, with the trade that discovery depends on Amazon devices.

    Checked on developer.amazon.com

    Visit Amazon Appstore

  8. 8

    Xbox Store

    Console and Xbox on PC audience

    Free option
    Unknown

    The Xbox site sells console and PC games, Game Pass and accessories, and lists Xbox Cloud Gaming, Xbox Play Anywhere and free-to-play games among its categories. For a studio ready for platform certification it opens an audience itch.io cannot.

    The pages we read do not describe the publishing program, so read Microsoft's partner documentation first.

    Checked on xbox.com

    Visit Xbox Store

Choosing among these

Start from where your players are and what you built. A downloadable PC game with a loyal following goes to Steam or GOG, and the choice between them is mostly about DRM and audience.

A browser build belongs on CrazyGames, Poki or GameDistribution, each of which handles distribution differently. Use Humble Bundle when reach matters more than per-copy income.

Reserve Amazon Appstore and the Xbox Store for projects that already target those devices. Keep your itch.io page live as the home for devlogs and jam entries, because none of these options replace its community.

Frequently asked questions

Can I stay on itch.io and also sell on Steam?

The pages we read do not state any exclusivity rule for itch.io, which describes itself as a way to find and share indie games for free. Check each storefront's current terms before signing a distribution agreement, since exclusivity clauses vary.

Which alternative is DRM-free like itch.io?

GOG says it offers classic and new games for Windows, Mac and Linux DRM-free. The other options in this list did not state a DRM position on the pages we opened, so ask each one directly if copy protection matters to you.

Where do I put a browser game?

CrazyGames lists HTML5 and Unity WebGL publishing, Poki runs a developer program for web games, and GameDistribution syndicates HTML5 games to websites. All three describe monetization, but none showed a revenue share figure on the pages we read.

Does Humble Bundle accept small indie games?

The pages we opened explain how bundles work for buyers and do not describe how a developer gets included. Humble Bundle's about page says it has sold over 47,000,000 bundles, so reach is large; ask the vendor about submission.

Is any of these free to join?

Amazon says registering a developer account is free, and Poki Playtesting is free for developers. For the others the opened pages did not state sign-up fees. Confirm costs and revenue splits before you build a release plan around them.
