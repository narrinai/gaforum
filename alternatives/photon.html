Photon Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

Photon is a multiplayer development platform from Photon Engine with networking engines, voice and chat services and cloud hosting, and its homepage claims over 1.4 billion monthly players in the Photon Cloud. It does not publish prices on that page, only a Try Photon free sign-up and a mention of cost-effective plans.

Teams swap it for a self-hosted library, a different hosting model or a full backend. Here are nine alternatives and what each vendor says about cost, hosting and engine support.

How this list is ordered: by our editorial judgment of fit for the reader named above, based on vendor documentation, pricing and license pages opened on the review date. Why teams look beyond Photon

  • You want a networking library that carries no CCU cap or paywall, which FishNet states in its documentation.
  • Hosting should be yours: Colyseus deploys to AWS, Google Cloud, DigitalOcean, Fly.io, Railway or bare metal.
  • You want prices you can read before you integrate, as Colyseus Cloud (from $15 a month) does.
  • The project uses Unity and you prefer a first-party option, which Netcode for GameObjects is.
  • You need accounts, storage and matchmaking in the same product, as Nakama and Unity's live services offer.
  • A reactive database-backed approach suits your design better, which SpacetimeDB describes.

The alternatives

  1. 1

    Mirror

    Open-source Unity networking

    Free option
    Open source

    Mirror is an open source networking library for Unity. Its homepage links to a Unity Asset Store download, to documentation on GitBook and to the source on GitHub, but names no license or price.

    Compared with Photon, you host the servers yourself, so you gain control and lose the managed cloud. It is a natural first look for Unity teams that already run their own infrastructure.

    Checked on mirror-networking.com

    Visit Mirror

  2. 2

    Netcode for GameObjects

    First-party Unity co-op

    Free option
    Unknown

    Netcode for GameObjects is Unity's own netcode library, which the multiplayer page aims at casual co-op games and pairs with a Distributed Authority mode. It sits within Unity's multiplayer services, and the page links to a View pricing page without figures.

    Pick it if you want one vendor for engine and networking. It does not help if your game is not built on Unity.

    Checked on unity.com

    Visit Netcode for GameObjects

  3. 3

    Fish-Networking

    Free server-authoritative Unity netcode

    Free option
    Free

    FishNet is described in its documentation as an original free and versatile networking solution for Unity, server-authoritative by design, with the source on GitHub under FirstGearGames. The page states there are no CCU caps and no CCU paywalls, which speaks directly to cost at scale.

    You host the servers, so budget for your own infrastructure and operations.

    Checked on fish-networking.gitbook.io

    Visit Fish-Networking

  4. 4

    Normcore

    Unity multiplayer with private hosting

    Free option
    Free plan

    Normcore is a multiplayer networking solution for Unity from Normal VR LLC of Brooklyn, with Normcore Public and Normcore Private lines. The homepage says you can start free and pay for what you use, with no hidden fees or CCU caps, but it does not state plan prices and shows a Contact Sales button.

    It also lists Private Cloud, On-Premises Hosting and Enterprise Support, useful when a customer demands data on its own servers.

    Checked on normcore.io

    Visit Normcore

  5. 5

    Colyseus

    Node.js rooms and matchmaking

    Free option
    Open source

    Colyseus is an open-source real-time multiplayer framework for Node.js with built-in matchmaking, state sync and client SDKs. The homepage says it is MIT licensed and free forever, even for commercial games, and that Colyseus Cloud starts at $15 a month.

    The server can run on AWS, Google Cloud, DigitalOcean, Fly.io, Railway or bare metal. A good match for web and browser games, and for teams with JavaScript skills.

    Checked on colyseus.io

    Visit Colyseus

  6. 6

    coherence

    Unity netcode plus hosting

    Free option
    Free plan

    coherence describes itself as the simplest way to build and host multiplayer games with Unity, which makes it the closest structural match to Photon: SDK plus hosting. The homepage has a Start for free button but shows no plan prices, and the answer to its cost FAQ is not visible.

    Customer logos include Devolver Digital and LEGO. Ask for a quote based on your peak players before you commit.

    Checked on coherence.io

    Visit coherence

  7. 7

    SpacetimeDB

    Reactive database as backend

    Free option
    Unknown

    SpacetimeDB, from Clockwork Labs, is a reactive backend platform that combines a database, server logic, auth, deployment and real-time sync. It replaces the idea of a relay with one where clients subscribe to data.

    That is a bigger shift than swapping one netcode library for another, so it suits new projects more than rewrites. Pricing sits on a separate page.

    Checked on spacetimedb.com

    Visit SpacetimeDB

  8. 8

    Nakama

    Open-source backend with realtime

    Free option
    Open source

    Nakama is described as a popular scalable open source game backend for building social multiplayer games, from Heroic Labs. The homepage offers a Nakama OSS download and lists Electronic Arts, Paradox Interactive, Remedy, Zynga, Halfbrick, Bandai Namco and Mattel as trusted by.

    It claims 2m+ peak CCU across its users. Choose it when you want accounts, matchmaking and realtime from one self-hostable server.

    Checked on heroiclabs.com

    Visit Nakama

  9. 9

    Unity Gaming Services

    Unity's live-service tools

    Free option
    Unknown

    Unity's live services are tools for post-launch insights and live operations, listed on the page as achievements, player sign-in, leaderboards, IAP, Cloud Code, Cloud Save, Authentication, Analytics, A/B testing, Diagnostics, Cloud Content Delivery, Game Overrides and Push Notifications. The page does not use the name Unity Gaming Services.

    It is a services bundle, so pair it with a netcode library rather than treating it as one.

    Checked on unity.com

    Visit Unity Gaming Services

How to pick between them

Split the choice in two. First, where will the servers run?

Managed cloud keeps you near Photon's model, so compare coherence and Normcore; self-hosting points to Mirror, FishNet, Colyseus or Nakama. Second, what do you need beyond netcode?

If it is accounts and storage, a backend such as Nakama or Unity's services belongs on the list. Ask each vendor for a quote based on your expected peak players, since several pages omit prices.

Frequently asked questions

Does Photon publish its pricing?

Its homepage does not. It shows a Try Photon free sign-up and mentions cost-effective pricing plans without figures, so you need the vendor's pricing pages or a quote. That is the main practical reason some teams look at libraries whose cost is stated plainly.

Which alternative has no player cap?

FishNet states no CCU caps and no CCU paywalls, and Normcore says no CCU caps and no hidden fees. Both are for Unity. A cap-free library still carries hosting costs, since you pay for the servers that run the game.

Is there a non-Unity option?

Colyseus targets Node.js and offers client SDKs, and Nakama and SpacetimeDB are backends rather than Unity plugins. Check each vendor's SDK list for your engine before you plan a migration, because this page only reports what the homepages state.

Can I keep voice and chat when leaving Photon?

Photon bundles voice and chat services with its networking engines. None of the alternatives here is described as a direct voice product, so plan a separate vendor if voice matters. Unity's live services list authentication and cloud tools but no voice product on the page we opened.

What is the cheapest published entry price here?

Colyseus Cloud starts at $15 a month per its homepage, and the Colyseus framework itself is free under MIT. Most other vendors on this list either are free libraries or do not publish plan prices on the page we opened.
