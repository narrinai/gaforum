PlayFab Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

PlayFab is a set of backend services for live games, part of Microsoft's gaming ecosystem, with a free-to-start tier, pay-as-you-go pricing as games scale and a Development Mode capped at 1,000 lifetime player account creations per title and 10 titles per account. Studios look elsewhere to avoid a Microsoft dependency, to self-host, or to get a different pricing shape.

The nine alternatives below were read from their own pages on 18 November 2026, and where a page omits a price, we say so.

Ordering: editorial, based on what each vendor publishes about pricing, licensing and features as of the date above. What pushes teams away from PlayFab

  • You want the backend source: Nakama offers an open-source download and Metaplay ships as full source code.
  • The cap on Development Mode (1,000 lifetime player accounts per title) gets in the way of a long soft launch.
  • Pricing should follow a metric you understand, such as AccelByte's peak concurrent players.
  • You want to avoid dependence on Microsoft's gaming ecosystem and its cloud.
  • A general-purpose backend such as Supabase (Postgres) or Firebase suits a game that is closer to an app.
  • A publishing-focused platform, like Pragma, fits a studio planning many titles.

What to use instead

  1. 1

    Nakama

    Self-hostable social and multiplayer backend

    Free option
    Open source

    Nakama, from Heroic Labs, is a scalable open source game backend for social multiplayer games. You can download Nakama OSS from the homepage, which names no license and no price.

    Customers listed include Electronic Arts, Paradox Interactive, Remedy, Zynga, Halfbrick, Bandai Namco and Mattel. You gain control over where it runs; you take on operations, which a managed service like PlayFab handles for you.

    Checked on heroiclabs.com

    Visit Nakama

  2. 2

    AccelByte

    Studios that want usage-based pricing

    Free option
    Free trial

    AccelByte is a multiplatform game backend with modular online services. The homepage says the first 30 CCU are free during development, there is no monthly platform fee on Public Cloud after the trial, and pricing scales with peak concurrent players.

    Its estimate of about $350 per month for 50,000 monthly players is labeled as an estimate. It lists 15+ platform integrations, including PlayStation, Xbox and Switch.

    Checked on accelbyte.io

    Visit AccelByte

  3. 3

    Pragma

    Publishing-scale backend and live ops

    Free option
    Unknown

    Pragma presents itself as a modern publishing tools platform for game studios, built around a Backend Engine and live operations tools. The homepage calls the Backend Engine the game industry's leading backend, a claim, and says more than 50% of top PC and console studios use it.

    It states no prices and no free tier, so expect a sales conversation. It targets larger studios rather than first-time indies.

    Checked on pragma.gg

    Visit Pragma

  4. 4

    Beamable

    Game server and live ops with a trial

    Free option
    Free trial

    Beamable is a game server and LiveOps platform for Web2 and Web3 games that replaces custom backend work with hosted services. The homepage describes trial usage of up to 1,000,000 free API calls and a LaunchPad program worth $2,500 in credits, with no plan prices shown.

    It is a fit when your team lacks backend engineers and wants hosted services from day one.

    Checked on beamable.com

    Visit Beamable

  5. 5

    LootLocker

    Small teams who want a free start

    Free option
    Free plan

    LootLocker is a player platform that helps publishers own their player relationships, collected content and live systems, organized into Connect, Engage and Reward. The homepage says Free to start and claims to power 10,000+ live games from publishers including Team17, Astragon Entertainment and Fast Travel Games.

    Prices are on a separate page. It is a gentle route for small teams leaving a capped dev tier.

    Checked on lootlocker.com

    Visit LootLocker

  6. 6

    Unity Gaming Services

    Unity projects wanting one vendor

    Free option
    Unknown

    Unity's live services cover player sign-in, leaderboards, Cloud Code, Cloud Save, Authentication, Analytics, A/B testing, Diagnostics and Cloud Content Delivery, among others, according to the page. It cites Subway Surfers and MARVEL SNAP as live-game case studies.

    The page does not use the name Unity Gaming Services. It makes sense mostly when the game already runs on Unity.

    Checked on unity.com

    Visit Unity Gaming Services

  7. 7

    Metaplay

    Source-available live-service backend

    Free option
    Free plan

    Metaplay is a complete backend platform for building and operating live service games. The homepage lists a Free tier for building and running a game locally, a Pro tier for cloud deployment with no price shown and an Enterprise tier for self-hosting.

    It says the platform ships as full source code and is production-proven by Supercell, Metacore, Trailmix and Lessmore, a vendor claim. Good for mobile live-service teams.

    Checked on metaplay.io

    Visit Metaplay

  8. 8

    Firebase

    App-style backend from Google

    Free option
    Free trial

    Firebase is a platform of services for building and running intelligent apps, backed by Google and powered by Google Cloud infrastructure. The homepage lists SDKs for Apple, Android, Web, Flutter, Unity and C++, and offers $300 in credit for new Billing Accounts.

    It is not game-specific, so you assemble leaderboards and economy logic yourself. A cheaper fit for simple games, and it moves you from Microsoft's cloud to Google's.

    Checked on firebase.google.com

    Visit Firebase

  9. 9

    Supabase

    Postgres backend for lighter games

    Free option
    Free plan

    Supabase is an open source backend platform built on Postgres. The homepage says all core tools are open source and self-hostable, that Auth offers 20+ social sign-in providers and that Storage is S3-compatible.

    A Free plan exists, with free projects pausing after 7 days of low activity, which matters for a live game. Like Firebase, it is a general backend rather than a game-specific one.

    Checked on supabase.com

    Visit Supabase

Which one fits

Decide how much of the live-game stack you want to own. For source access and self-hosting, shortlist Nakama and Metaplay.

For game-specific services run for you, compare AccelByte, Beamable and LootLocker, and ask each for a figure at your expected peak players. If your game is simple and relational, Supabase or Firebase can cover accounts and storage at lower complexity.

Plan a migration path for player data before you pick, since moving identities is the hard part.

Frequently asked questions

What limit applies to PlayFab's free evaluation mode?

Microsoft's page says Development Mode is limited to 1,000 lifetime player account creations per title and 10 titles per account. Beyond that you move to the free-to-start tier with pay-as-you-go pricing, for which the page shows no dollar figures.

Which alternative publishes a sample monthly cost?

AccelByte gives an estimate of about $350 per month for 50,000 monthly players, labeled as an estimate, and says pricing scales with peak concurrent players. Most other backends on this list do not publish plan figures on the pages we opened.

Can I run a replacement on my own servers?

Nakama offers an open-source download, Metaplay ships as full source code with a self-hosting Enterprise tier, and Supabase says its core tools are self-hostable. Self-hosting trades subscription fees for operations work, so account for on-call time.

Do any of these avoid Microsoft's cloud?

Firebase runs on Google Cloud, Supabase is built on Postgres and can be self-hosted, and Nakama can be downloaded and run where you choose. Your game's own servers and platform logins may still involve other clouds, so check the full chain.

Is a general backend enough for a live game?

For simple loops, Firebase or Supabase can handle accounts and storage. Game-specific features such as matchmaking, economy and live ops are what Nakama, AccelByte, Beamable and Metaplay describe on their pages, so weigh how many of those you need.
