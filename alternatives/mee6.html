MEE6 Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

MEE6 is a Discord bot that describes itself as a way to manage a server with leveling, moderation, and Twitch, YouTube and Reddit notifications. Many server owners eventually compare it with other bots, usually because they want to know which features sit behind a premium plan and which bots cover the same jobs for less.

This page lists ten Discord bots with different strengths, from a completely free bot to open source software you can host yourself, so you can split MEE6's duties across the tools that fit your community. See the Discord moderation and community bot ranking for the wider field.

About the order: it reflects our own reading of each vendor's documentation, prices and license terms, weighed for the reader this page is written for. Reasons to leave MEE6

  • MEE6 bundles leveling, moderation and social notifications in one bot, and some servers only need one of those jobs done well.
  • The MEE6 premium page renders its text with JavaScript, so a quick read of what the paid tier adds is harder than with some rivals.
  • Several alternatives publish a plain statement about cost: Sapphire says it is completely free and YAGPDB says it is open source.
  • Servers with a heavy ticket workload or a security focus can pick a bot built around that single job, such as Ticket Tool or Wick.
  • Communities that want control over where the bot runs can look at YAGPDB, which the vendor says can be hosted yourself.

The options, in order

  1. 1

    Dyno

    Web-configured moderation and auto roles

    Free option
    Unknown

    Dyno is the closest swap if you mostly use MEE6 for moderation and role automation. It is described as modular, with a web dashboard, anti-spam, auto roles and custom commands.

    You gain a more moderation-centered feature set and the ability to switch modules on or off. You give up the Twitch, YouTube and Reddit notification focus that MEE6 puts in its own description, so check how you handle announcements before moving.

    Carl-bot

    Reaction roles and logging

    Free option
    Unknown

    Carl-bot is the usual stop for servers that want members to pick their own roles. Its site lists reaction roles, automod, logging and custom commands in a fully customizable, modular bot.

    You gain role menus and detailed logs in the same place as automod. What you may miss is a leveling system, which is not named in the Carl-bot description we read, so confirm that before dropping MEE6 levels.

    Visit Carl-bot

  3. 3

    Sapphire

    A free bot with slash commands

    Free option
    Free

    Sapphire says it is completely free, which makes it the simplest answer for servers that want to avoid a subscription. It lists custom slash commands, buttons, select menus, auto moderation and reaction roles, plus scheduled and triggered messages.

    You gain a no-cost option with a lot of automation. The trade is that a free bot gives you no premium tier to lean on, and you should read its own limits before you migrate a large server.

    Arcane

    Leveling-focused community bots

    Free option
    Unknown

    Arcane is the nearest match for MEE6's leveling side. Its site lists leveling, moderation, logging, custom commands and member count channels.

    You gain a familiar leveling-plus-moderation pairing and counters for channels. You give up any social-feed notifications, which the Arcane description does not mention.

    Because we could not read its premium page, check what is paywalled in the dashboard before you commit your members' level data to it.

  5. 5

    YAGPDB

    Open source and self-hosting

    Free option
    Open source

    YAGPDB stands for Yet Another General Purpose Discord Bot, and its site says it is open source and can be hosted yourself for more customization. You gain control over where it runs and room to customize behavior.

    You give up the point-and-click simplicity of a hosted bot, since self-hosting means you maintain the setup. It suits servers with someone technical on the moderation team who wants to avoid depending on one vendor.

    Ticket Tool

    Support tickets

    Free option
    Unknown

    Ticket Tool does one job: support tickets. It calls itself an advanced, highly customizable ticket bot for handling community support.

    Use it when your server runs a help desk, a store or an appeals channel and tickets are the main workload. You gain depth in ticket handling.

    You give up everything else MEE6 does, so it works as an addition next to a moderation bot rather than a full replacement.

    Zeppelin

    Moderation with a dashboard

    Free option
    Unknown

    Zeppelin's site title calls it a moderation bot for Discord, and that is all we could confirm, because its about page did not render a feature list for us. It makes sense to test it when moderation is your only need and you want a dedicated bot rather than a bundle.

    You gain a narrower tool. The risk is not knowing its exact features in advance, so read its documentation before moving any rules over.

    Tatsu

    Fun community engagement

    Free option
    Unknown

    Tatsu positions itself as the way to build a fun, inviting community server, and says more than 1,350,000 Discord servers use it. That is a vendor claim, but it signals a large user base for a bot focused on engagement rather than enforcement.

    You gain a lighter, community-first feel. You give up moderation depth, so it pairs well with a stricter bot such as Wick or Dyno handling rules while Tatsu handles the social side.

  9. 9

    Statbot

    Server statistics and counters

    Free option
    Unknown

    Statbot is a server stats bot with customizable channel counters, automatic role rewards and personalized dashboards. Pick it if the reason you opened this page is to see how members behave over time, not to moderate them.

    You gain analytics and role rewards driven by activity. You give up general moderation and social alerts, which makes it an add-on to a main bot, not a standalone replacement for MEE6.

Choosing among these

Start by writing down what MEE6 does for your server today: leveling, rules enforcement, announcements, or all three. If moderation is the core, compare Dyno, Wick and Zeppelin.

If members earn levels, look at Arcane and Tatsu. If you want zero subscription cost, test Sapphire, and if you want control of hosting, test YAGPDB.

Treat Ticket Tool and Statbot as specialists you add alongside a main bot. Move one module at a time and keep MEE6 running until the replacement behaves the way your moderators expect.

Frequently asked questions

What does MEE6 actually do?

MEE6 describes itself as a Discord bot for managing a server with leveling, moderation, and Twitch, YouTube and Reddit notifications. That combination is why it often ends up as a default bot in new servers. Its premium page exists but its text is rendered by JavaScript, so we could not read the prices.

Is there a completely free MEE6 alternative?

Sapphire's site says the bot is completely free. YAGPDB is open source and can be self-hosted, although its site also mentions a Premium subscription. Free to use still means you should check which limits apply to your server size before migrating.

Which bot is best for support tickets?

Ticket Tool is built around that single job and describes itself as a highly customizable ticket bot for community support. It does not replace a moderation bot, so most servers run it beside another bot such as Dyno or Carl-bot.

Can I run two or three bots at once?

Yes, and many servers do. A common split is one bot for moderation, one for engagement and one specialist for tickets or stats. Watch for overlapping commands and duplicate welcome or logging messages, and disable the modules you no longer need in the older bot.

How do I move leveling data away from MEE6?

None of the pages we opened describe an import tool, so assume levels do not transfer automatically. Announce the change to members, decide whether to start fresh or grant starter roles, and confirm the new bot's leveling behavior in its dashboard first.
