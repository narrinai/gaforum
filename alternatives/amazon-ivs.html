Amazon IVS Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

Amazon IVS is a hosted live-streaming service from Amazon Web Services that delivers video worldwide with low delay so developers can build interactive live experiences, and it is billed pay-as-you-go with per-hour rates. Reasons to look past it include AWS lock-in, a different billing model or a need for more than one-way video.

The ten picks below range from video APIs to real-time communications SDKs, each described from its own vendor pages.

About the order: it reflects our own reading of each vendor's documentation, prices and license terms, weighed for the reader this page is written for. Reasons to leave Amazon IVS

  • IVS prices are per hour of channel input and output, with a free usage tier limited to the first 12 months; teams with steady volume may want per-minute or flat monthly pricing instead.
  • Interactive products with many people on camera need real-time rooms, which LiveKit, 100ms, Agora and Vonage are built around.
  • Teams that avoid single-cloud dependence may prefer a vendor that is not part of a hyperscaler.
  • A content business may want hosting, analytics and on-demand together, as Mux and api.video package them.
  • Some regions or buyers favor European providers; api.video and Ceeblue list European addresses or entities.

The options, in order

  1. 1

    Mux

    Live and on-demand video API

    Free option
    Free

    Mux covers streaming, analytics, moderation and video transformation behind one developer API, and it says no credit card is needed to start. Per-minute pricing is simple to model against IVS's hourly rates.

    You gain analytics in the same product and a billing model that fits spiky events. You lose the tight integration with other AWS services if your stack already lives there.

    api.video

    Simple hosting and live from France

    Free option
    Free

    api.video offers hosting, management and delivery of live and on-demand video through an API, with a registered address in Roubaix, France. A free sandbox exists but caps videos at 30 seconds with a watermark.

    It suits product teams that want to add video quickly and prefer a European vendor. Check live latency in your own test, because the pages do not state a figure.

    Dolby OptiView

    Sports and ad-supported streams

    Free option
    Unknown

    Dolby OptiView is a live sports platform for service providers with streaming, Sports Intelligence clipping, OptiView Ads for dynamic ad insertion and a player. It makes sense for an esports league or broadcaster that wants clips and ads built in.

    It is a sales-led platform for operators, so a small indie app will find it a heavier engagement than a self-serve API.

    Checked on optiview.dolby.com

    Visit Dolby OptiView

  4. 4

    LiveKit

    Open source real-time video and voice

    Free option
    Free

    LiveKit is an open source framework and developer platform for voice, video and AI agents. It is the choice when your product needs participants talking back, such as co-streaming or live Q&A, rather than one-way broadcast.

    The open source base also eases moving to your own infrastructure later. Expect to design more of the experience yourself than with a turnkey broadcast service.

    Checked on livekit.com

    Visit LiveKit

  5. 5

    Cloudflare Stream

    Video on an existing Cloudflare stack

    Free option
    Unknown

    Cloudflare Stream stores, encodes and delivers live and on-demand video, with RTMP and SRT ingest and HLS and DASH output. Teams already routing traffic through Cloudflare may find billing and delivery familiar.

    It is a broadcast pipeline, not a real-time room product, so interactive formats need another tool alongside it.

    Checked on cloudflare.com

    Visit Cloudflare Stream

    100ms

    Audio and video rooms in apps

    Free option
    Free trial

    100ms is a live video infrastructure provider with SDKs and APIs for audio and video in apps. It addresses the room-based use case that a one-to-many broadcast service does not, such as coaching sessions or watch parties.

    Start with the free trial and measure join times on your target devices, since the homepage gives no latency numbers.

    Ceeblue

    Near real-time delivery with European roots

    Free option
    Unknown

    Ceeblue's Media Fabric platform transcodes and delivers streams in near real time, aiming for sub-second latency, and the company is Ceeblue B.V. Published starting prices in euros are rare in this category and help with budgeting. Starting prices are not total cost, so ask how much a given viewer load adds above those entry fees.

    Agora

    Real-time engagement at scale

    Free option
    Free

    Agora offers low-latency APIs for voice, video, chat and conversational AI, with video calling and interactive live streaming among its products. It is geared toward apps where audiences interact in real time, with many participants.

    The free tier is a quick way to prototype, but you must request paid rates from the vendor before planning a launch.

    Wowza

    Server software with a cloud option

    Free option
    Free trial

    Wowza Streaming Engine is video server software that can run on-prem, in the cloud, at the edge or air-gapped, and Flowplayer OVP is its managed platform. It gives more control over where video runs than a hosted service does.

    The price of that control is operations work and a quote-based purchase.

    Vonage

    Video inside a wider communications stack

    Free option
    Unknown

    Vonage offers voice, messaging, video and data capabilities across communications APIs, unified communications and contact centers. It fits a company that wants video next to SMS or voice calls under one supplier.

    For a game or esports stream, a specialist video vendor will usually offer more streaming-specific features.

Choosing among these

Separate broadcast from interaction. If viewers only watch, compare Mux, api.video, Cloudflare Stream and Dolby OptiView on price per viewer hour and latency.

If viewers speak or join the stage, compare LiveKit, 100ms, Agora and Vonage. Run the same two-hour test event on a shortlist of two vendors, record join time and delay, and ask for a quote at your expected peak audience.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main reason to leave Amazon IVS?

Typical reasons are avoiding dependence on one cloud, preferring a different billing model, or needing real-time participants rather than one-way video. IVS is billed per hour with a first-12-months free tier, so teams with steady volume sometimes compare it with per-minute or flat plans.

Which alternatives suit interactive streams with guests?

LiveKit, 100ms, Agora and Vonage focus on real-time audio and video in apps. Broadcast-oriented products such as Mux and Cloudflare Stream deliver to large audiences but are not designed around many speaking participants.

Is there a European option?

api.video lists an address in Roubaix, France, and Ceeblue is a B.V. with euro prices on its homepage. Confirm data residency and contract terms with each vendor, since an address alone does not settle where streams are processed.

Which alternatives list a free start?

Mux and LiveKit say you can start free with no credit card, Agora lists a free tier of 10,000 minutes, and 100ms has a Try for Free button. api.video offers a limited sandbox. Always read the limits, because free tiers differ widely.

Can I run my own servers instead?

Yes, with Wowza Streaming Engine or an open-source server, though those are covered more fully on our Wowza alternatives page. Self-hosting trades a monthly service bill for infrastructure and staff time.
