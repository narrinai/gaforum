Helpshift Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

Helpshift calls itself the AI-native player engagement platform, with in-game support SDKs for mobile, PC and web and console support through QR codes and links; its home page names Zynga, Jam City, Rovio and Gameloft among its case studies. Mobile game teams look past it when price, team size or tooling does not line up.

Here are ten alternatives, from a game-focused rival to general helpdesks that you wire into your game through a widget or API.

Ordering: editorial, based on what each vendor publishes about pricing, licensing and features as of the date above. What pushes teams away from Helpshift

  • Unpublished pricing: Helpshift's opened home page showed no prices, so teams compare against vendors that list figures.
  • Team size: a small studio may not need a platform aimed at large publishers and prefer a helpdesk with a free plan.
  • Channel mix: studios that support players on email, live chat and social may want a general helpdesk with those channels built in.
  • AI costs: some tools charge per resolution or per outcome, which changes the math for high-volume games.
  • Data location: a European studio may prefer a vendor that states it is built in Europe.

What to use instead

  1. 1

    AIHelp

    In-app game support with chatbots

    Free option
    Free trial

    AIHelp is the closest in kind: an in-app and web customer support platform with AI chatbots, in-app messaging, push notifications and web chat, delivered as a mobile SDK. Its home page claims 30K clients and 800M+ monthly active users, which are vendor claims.

    You gain a comparable setup with a free trial; you give up the case-study names Helpshift shows, and you must open the pricing page yourself.

    Zendesk

    Large multi-channel support

    Free option
    Free trial

    Zendesk's home page describes self-learning AI agents and channels including email, messaging, live chat, phone, social media and help centers. That suits a publisher with a large player base and a support team in several time zones.

    You gain channel depth and a startup program; you give up game-specific SDK focus, and the opened page did not list plan prices.

    Freshdesk

    Budget-conscious ticketing

    Free option
    Free trial

    Freshdesk from Freshworks offers omnichannel ticketing and automated workflows with prices posted: $19 and $55 per agent per month billed annually. A studio can see its cost per support agent in advance.

    You gain price transparency; you give up an in-game SDK built for mobile players, so players will reach you through a web form or widget you embed.

    Visit Freshdesk

    Intercom

    Automated resolution with an AI agent

    Free option
    Free trial

    Intercom pairs a helpdesk with its Fin AI Agent and bills $0.99 per outcome on top of $19 per seat per month, with spending limits you can set. High-volume games with repetitive questions about purchases or accounts may like that model.

    You gain automation; you give up flat predictability, since cost rises with resolved conversations. No free plan is mentioned.

    Help Scout

    Free plan

    Help Scout gives a shared inbox, AI assistance, workflow automation and a help docs product, with a free plan for 5 users. A five-person indie studio can run player email support there at no cost.

    You gain a gentle start and a docs site; you give up in-game messaging, and AI resolutions are billed at $0.75 each if you add that feature.

    You gain a CRM view of each customer; you give up game-specific features and published pricing.

    You gain commerce focus; you give up an in-game support flow, and you must go through a demo to learn anything about price.

    Unknown

Which one fits

Check where your players ask for help. If it is inside the game, AIHelp is the like-for-like test, since the others expect players to reach you through a web page or an embedded widget.

If it is email, Help Scout (free for 5 users), Freshdesk ($19 and $55 per agent) and Zoho Desk keep cost visible. If a bot should absorb repetitive tickets, price Intercom's per-outcome fee against your monthly volume.

Crisp is the pick when a European vendor matters. Our player support ranking adds more detail.

Frequently asked questions

Which alternative has an in-game SDK like Helpshift?

AIHelp is the one on this list that describes itself as an in-app and web support platform offered as a mobile SDK. The general helpdesks reach players through email, widgets and web chat, so you would embed a link or web view from the game.

What is the cheapest way to start?

Help Scout offers a free plan for 5 users, 1 Inbox and 1 Docs site, and Zoho Desk lists a Free plan. For paid use, Freshdesk lists $19 and $55 per agent per month billed annually and LiveChat says from $19 per user per month.

How does AI pricing differ between tools?

Intercom charges $0.99 per outcome on top of seat fees, Help Scout lists $0.75 per resolution for its AI add-on, and Freshdesk lists Freddy AI Copilot at $29 per agent per month. Per-resolution models scale with volume, per-agent add-ons scale with headcount.

Is there a European option?

Crisp says on its homepage that it is built from France and made in Europe, and names Crisp IM SAS in its copyright. Whether that satisfies your data rules is for your legal team to confirm.

Do these tools suit console or PC games?

General helpdesks work on any platform because the player contacts you by web or email. Helpshift's own page mentions PC and web SDKs and QR codes for console, so if you rely on that, test how your chosen tool handles the same cases.
