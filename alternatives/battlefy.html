Battlefy Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

Battlefy is an esports tournament platform that describes itself as the simplest way to start, manage and find tournaments. Organizers look at other options when they want different fee structures or a format Battlefy does not suit, such as crowdfunded prizes, school seasons or a venue-based event.

The Battlefy pricing page does not render readable text without JavaScript, so we cannot compare fees directly; this page therefore frames each alternative by what its own pages say about how it works. For the wider set of tools, see the esports tournament management software ranking.

What pushes teams away from Battlefy

  • The Battlefy pricing page loads without readable text, so an organizer cannot compare fees without opening it in a full browser.
  • Prize-pool events can fit better elsewhere: Matcherino describes crowd-funded pools with free organizer tools.
  • Some organizers want matchmaking and gamification around events, which Challengermode lists as core features.
  • School programs may want a seasonal structure with coaching tools, as PlayVS describes.
  • A physical venue needs booking and point of sale, which ggLeap provides beside tournaments.

What to use instead

  1. 1

    Challengermode

    Tournaments with matchmaking

    Free option
    Free

    Challengermode combines tournaments, matchmaking and gamification and says it supports more than 200 games across more than 80 countries. Compared with Battlefy's find-and-start pitch, it leans toward keeping players engaged between events.

    The free start reduces the cost of a test run. What you give up is the single-purpose feel of a tournament-only tool, and its developer pricing figures were not available to us.

  2. 2

    start.gg

    Events inside a shared hub

    Free option
    Unknown

    start.gg bundles tournament creation with rankings, shops and fantasy sections, and links a developer portal. It is an alternative when an organizer wants players to follow an event through a wider community hub.

    The extra surface area means more to learn than a lean tournament page, and fee details need to be confirmed on the vendor site.

    Checked on start.gg

    Visit start.gg

  3. 3

    Toornament

    League and cup structures

    Free option
    Unknown

    Toornament says it has everything needed for competitions and has an organizer plan page aimed at all competition projects. It is a candidate for organizers who want more formal structure than a simple event listing.

    Its content loads with JavaScript, so we could only read the meta description; trial it with a real event before committing a season to it.

    Checked on toornament.com

    Visit Toornament

  4. 4

    FACEIT

    Events inside an established platform

    Free option
    Unknown

    FACEIT publishes an organizer help center that covers setting up tournaments and clubs, and developer documentation for integrations. It suits organizers wanting to sit inside a large competitive platform.

    The main website returned HTTP 403 to our fetcher, so confirm prices and format limits on the vendor side before switching.

    Checked on support.faceit.com

    Visit FACEIT

  5. 5

    Matcherino

    Prize pools funded by fans

    Free option
    Free

    Matcherino pairs brackets, registration, payouts and sponsorships with community-funded prize pools. Where Battlefy's fee structure feels wrong for a prize-driven event, this model puts the money on the audience side.

    It fits community cups better than private leagues, and platform fees were not readable, so ask before you publish a pool total.

    Checked on matcherino.com

    Visit Matcherino

  6. 6

    PlayVS

    Scholastic esports seasons

    Free option
    Unknown

    PlayVS has operated since 2018 and offers programs for high school, middle school and elementary school gaming, with real-time stats and coaching tools. Pick it when a school or district wants a managed season rather than an open event.

    It is not designed for drop-in public cups.

    Checked on playvs.com

    Visit PlayVS

  7. 7

    GGLeap

    Venue-run events

    Free option
    Unknown

    ggLeap, from ggCircuit, is software for gaming centers and LAN cafes, covering PC booking, a player portal, point of sale with Stripe and tournaments. Choose it if the competition is part of running a room.

    It is unnecessary for online-only organizers, and the price is only available through a demo.

    Checked on ggcircuit.com

Which one fits

Work out why you are leaving before you compare features. If the issue is cost, ask each vendor for a written fee sheet, since Battlefy, start.gg and Toornament all keep prices behind JavaScript.

If the issue is format, match it directly: audience-funded prizes to Matcherino, matchmaking to Challengermode, school seasons to PlayVS, venue nights to ggLeap. Run one low-stakes event on the shortlist and have a few players register, because the player-side sign-up flow often decides how many people finish it.

Frequently asked questions

Which Battlefy alternative is free to start?

Challengermode says it is free to start, and Matcherino describes free tools for organizers. Prices for the other options could not be read on the opened pages, so confirm with each vendor.

Why compare fees if the pricing pages cannot be read?

Several tournament sites render their prices with JavaScript, which our fetcher could not run. We recorded that limit instead of guessing. Open each pricing page in a normal browser or ask for a quote, and compare the same event size across vendors.

Is there an alternative for school gaming programs?

PlayVS describes infrastructure for amateur competitive gaming with high school, middle school and elementary school programs, plus stats and coaching tools. It follows a managed season rather than an open event.

What if my tournaments run in a gaming center?

ggLeap covers PC booking, loyalty, point of sale and tournaments for gaming centers and LAN cafes. It is priced by demo request, so ask for a quote based on your number of stations.

Does Battlefy still make sense for open online cups?

Its own description centers on starting, managing and finding tournaments, so open events with public discovery remain its core use. These alternatives matter when your format, fee preference or venue differs.
