Unreal Engine Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

Unreal Engine is a 3D game engine from Epic Games. Its own website blocked our fetch tool on 18 November 2026, so this page makes no claim about its price, royalty or feature list; check Epic's licensing pages directly.

What we can offer is a comparison of nine engines whose vendors state their terms openly, aimed at teams that want a lighter editor or a royalty position they can read in one paragraph before committing to a project.

Why this order: we opened every vendor's own pages on the review date and placed the products by fit, not by popularity or payment. Vendors can pay for more visibility; how that works is explained in our disclosure, linked above every ranking. No commission is earned on clicks or purchases, and we do not work as an affiliate.

When to replace Unreal Engine

  • You want an engine whose license sits on its homepage in plain words, as Stride (MIT), Bevy (MIT or Apache 2.0) and Defold (no royalties, no runtime fee) do.
  • A C# codebase suits your team better, which is what Stride and Flax Engine offer.
  • Your game is smaller in scope and you prefer an engine that is described as lightweight or code-first.
  • You want full engine source under a stated royalty, as CRYENGINE publishes (5%, with the first $5K of annual revenue per project royalty-free).
  • You need mobile or web targets handled by an engine aimed at them, such as Cocos Creator or Defold.
  • You prefer foundation governance of the engine, as with O3DE or Godot.

Our picks

  1. 1

    Unity

    Broad commercial 3D and 2D engine

    Free option
    Unknown

    Unity is the nearest commercial rival to Unreal as a general-purpose engine, described on its homepage as a real-time 3D and 2D game engine for developing, deploying and growing games. It lists desktop, iOS, Android, Switch, PlayStation, Xbox, Meta Quest, web and Apple Vision Pro.

    Unity 6.6 is available and Unity 7 is announced. You gain a wide platform list; you accept pricing that is explained on a separate plans page.

    Checked on unity.com

    Visit Unity

  2. 2

    Godot

    Free open-source 2D and 3D

    Free option
    Open source

    Godot is a free, open-source 2D and 3D engine hosted by the Godot Foundation, with Godot 4.7.2 listed as the latest download. Its homepage makes no pricing statement beyond calling it free and open source, which answers the royalty question in one line.

    It fits studios that want to read and modify the engine, and that can live with a smaller editor than a heavyweight 3D engine.

    Checked on godotengine.org

    Visit Godot

  3. 3

    Stride

    C# 3D engine under MIT

    Free option
    Open source

    Stride is a free, cross-platform C# engine for 2D and 3D games and other interactive content on desktop and VR. The homepage says the editor and runtime source are MIT licensed and that the .NET Foundation supports it.

    Stride 4.4 (October 9, 2026) adds a Vulkan and Direct3D 12 rewrite and Native AOT support. It is a natural step for .NET developers who want source access without a royalty clause.

    Checked on stride3d.net

    Visit Stride

  4. 4

    Flax Engine

    Source access with C++ and C#

    Free option
    Unknown

    Flax Engine advertises full source code and scripting in C++, C# and Visual Scripting, and presents itself as agile. The homepage links to a Licensing page but does not state a license name, royalty terms or prices, so treat the commercial terms as something to confirm.

    It suits teams that want an engine-and-editor package close in spirit to Unreal's source-visible approach.

    Checked on flaxengine.com

    Visit Flax Engine

  5. 5

    O3DE

    Open-source 3D under a foundation

    Free option
    Open source

    O3DE is an open source, community-driven 3D engine supported by the Open 3D Foundation; the latest release named on its homepage is O3DE 26.05. It targets the same large-scale 3D ambitions as Unreal while being governed by a foundation.

    The homepage does not name the software license or any price, so read the repository terms before choosing it.

    Checked on o3de.org

    Visit O3DE

  6. 6

    CryEngine

    High-end visuals with full source

    Free option
    Free

    CRYENGINE is Crytek's game development platform with a real-time editor and full source code. The homepage says it is free to use, with a 5% royalty when you ship and the first $5K of annual revenue per project royalty-free.

    It names PC, Xbox, PlayStation and Oculus as platforms, and showcases Kingdom Come: Deliverance, Prey, Star Citizen and Sniper: Ghost Warrior. Its platform list is shorter than Unity's.

    Checked on cryengine.com

    Visit CryEngine

  7. 7

    Defold

    Lightweight engine, no royalties

    Free option
    Free

    Defold is a free, all-in-one engine for cross-platform 2D and 3D games, source available under what the homepage calls a developer-friendly license. It lists Xbox, PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4, Switch, Steam, iOS, Android and HTML5, and Xbox support comes in Defold 1.14.0, free for approved Xbox developers.

    Choose it for compact games where startup size and zero royalty matter more than photoreal tooling.

    Checked on defold.com

    Visit Defold

  8. 8

    Bevy

    Rust engine, no license fees

    Free option
    Open source

    Bevy is a data-driven engine built in Rust, open source under MIT or Apache 2.0, with no contracts, license fees or sales cuts. Targets are Windows, macOS, Linux, Web, iOS and Android.

    It is a programmer's engine rather than an editor-led one, so artists and designers will miss the all-in-one workflow Unreal is known for. Best for small, code-focused teams.

    Checked on bevy.org

    Visit Bevy

  9. 9

    Cocos Creator

    Mobile-leaning 2D and 3D creation

    Free option
    Unknown

    Cocos Creator is a cross-platform graphics engine and real-time 3D content creation platform. Its homepage claims it hosts over 70% of game content development and lists ARM, Meta, Google and Microsoft as partners, but gives no price or license statement.

    It is worth a look for teams that ship lightweight games to many devices and want a 3D-capable editor without Unreal's footprint.

    Checked on cocos.com

    Visit Cocos Creator

Making the call

Decide whether you are replacing Unreal's scope or only its weight. For similar scope and a visible royalty, look at CRYENGINE or O3DE.

For a lighter engine with a license you can read in one sentence, shortlist Godot, Stride, Defold or Bevy. For commercial support and the widest platform list, price out Unity.

Whatever you choose, confirm Unreal's current terms on Epic's own site, because we could not open them, and put your intended shipping platforms against each vendor's target list.

Frequently asked questions

Why does this page not state Unreal Engine's price?

Epic's website returned an access error to our fetch tool on 18 November 2026, so we could not read its license or pricing pages. Rather than quote a figure from memory, we describe only the engines we could verify. Check Epic's own licensing page for current terms.

Which engine here publishes a royalty rate?

CRYENGINE states a 5% royalty when you ship, with the first $5K of annual revenue per project royalty-free. Defold, Stride, Bevy and Godot describe themselves as free, with Defold stating no royalties and no runtime fee on its homepage.

Is there a 3D engine here that uses C#?

Stride is a C# engine for 2D and 3D games, and Flax Engine lists C++, C# and Visual Scripting. Unity is also widely associated with C#, but its homepage text we opened does not mention a language, so this page says nothing on that point.

Which choice has foundation governance?

Godot is hosted by the Godot Foundation, Stride is supported by the .NET Foundation, and O3DE is supported by the Open 3D Foundation. Foundation backing does not replace reading each engine's license, but it tells you who stewards the project.

What should a small studio test first?

Build a short vertical slice with your real art and your intended platform in two finalists. Measure editor responsiveness, build times and export friction, since those decide daily productivity more than a feature checklist does.
