OBS Studio Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

OBS Studio is free and open source software for video recording and live streaming that runs on Windows 10 and 11, macOS 13.0 or newer, and Linux. Its homepage describes scenes built from multiple sources such as window captures, browser windows, webcams and capture cards.

This page looks at the live streaming side only: tools that replace or sit beside OBS when you want a friendlier interface, a browser-based studio, built-in multistreaming or a heavier production switcher. Our streaming software ranking for gamers covers the same field by use case.

About the order: it reflects our own reading of each vendor's documentation, prices and license terms, weighed for the reader this page is written for. Reasons to leave OBS Studio

  • OBS asks you to build scenes and sources yourself, and some streamers want a studio that is already assembled around alerts and overlays.
  • A browser-based studio such as StreamYard or Lightstream removes the local install and the encoding load on the streaming PC.
  • Multistreaming is built into tools like Meld Studio and Prism Live Studio, where OBS needs extra setup for several destinations.
  • Corporate and event teams may need a production switcher with multi-camera workflows, which is where vMix and Wirecast sit.
  • Mobile contribution from a phone is a separate job that an app like Larix Broadcaster handles directly.

    Meld Studio

    Free studio with built-in multistreaming

    Free option
    Free

    Meld Studio calls itself free streaming and recording software with multistreaming to every platform at once and 4K recording. Its Meld Spark tool covers interactive overlays and alerts.

    You gain multistreaming without extra plumbing and a gallery of free overlays. You give up the huge plugin culture around OBS.

    The paid tiers are $17 per month for Pro, or $200 billed annually, so check what the free level leaves out.

    XSplit Broadcaster

    Windows streaming with a polished UI

    Free option
    Free

    XSplit Broadcaster is live streaming and recording software for games, podcasts and presentations, with scenes, sources and outputs in one workspace. It runs on Windows only.

    You gain a commercial product with a free tier and a Premium plan that adds higher-resolution output and commercial use. You give up Mac and Linux support and the open source license, which matters if you want to inspect or modify the code.

    Prism Live Studio

    Free multistreaming plus a mobile app

    Free option
    Free

    PRISM Live Studio is free software for live streaming and recording with multi-platform streaming and video editing. It runs on Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit) and on Apple Silicon Macs with Monterey 12.3 or higher, and has a mobile app.

    You gain simultaneous streaming and extras like screen sharing and AvatarLive. You give up Linux and Intel Mac support, and Credits are sold only in the mobile app.

    Lightstream

    Cloud streaming without a powerful PC

    Free option
    Free

    Lightstream runs in the browser and needs no downloads, which suits streamers on weaker machines or consoles using cloud capture. The free tier gives 720p, a 10 hour limit per stream and up to 3 scenes with a logo on your stream.

    Premium removes the logo and adds 1080p and 20 scenes. You gain no encoding on your own hardware.

    You give up local control and the deep customization of OBS.

    vMix

    Windows live production switching

    Free option
    Free trial

    vMix is a live streaming and live production video mixer and switcher for Windows PCs. Switch to it when your stream looks more like a TV broadcast than a gaming session.

    You gain a production-oriented tool, and a 60-day trial lets you test it properly. You give up a free price tag, since the homepage shows purchase from $50 per month, and you give up anything other than Windows.

    Wirecast

    Multi-camera shows with remote guests

    Free option
    Free trial

    Wirecast is live video streaming and broadcast software for Mac and Windows with unlimited cameras, remote guests and multistreaming. It fits corporate and event streams where several people join a show.

    You gain both operating systems and a free trial that includes every feature. You give up OBS's zero price, as Studio starts at $34 per month billed yearly.

    It also lacks Linux support on the pages we opened.

    Ecamm Live

    Mac-only live studio

    Free option
    Free trial

    Ecamm Live is a live streaming and video production studio built for Mac, requiring macOS 11.2 or newer. Mac users who find OBS fiddly may prefer its approach.

    You gain a Mac-native studio and a 14-day trial with no credit card. You give up Windows and Linux entirely.

    The two plans are listed at $16 per month, billed as one payment of $192, and $32 per month, billed as one payment of $384.

    StreamYard

    Browser studio for interviews

    Free option
    Free

    StreamYard is a browser-based live streaming and recording studio that streams to Facebook, YouTube and other platforms. It is a fit for talk shows and webinars where guests join from a link.

    You gain no install and a free plan with local recordings of 2 hours per month. You give up scene-level control and capture of games, so it is closer to a meeting-style studio than OBS.

    Checked on streamyard.com

    Larix Broadcaster

    Streaming from a phone

    Free option
    Free

    Larix Broadcaster is a free mobile broadcasting app from Softvelum for remote contribution streaming from Android and iOS devices. It does not replace a desktop studio, but it covers the case where your camera is a phone in the field.

    You gain a free contribution tool. You give up scenes, overlays and any desktop production features, so most people would send its feed into another tool rather than use it alone.

Choosing among these

Decide first where the stream is produced. A weak PC or a console points to Lightstream.

A Mac points to Ecamm Live. Guests joining by link point to StreamYard.

A Windows rig for a multi-camera show points to vMix, and a Mac or Windows one to Wirecast. If you want multistreaming without extra setup, try Meld Studio or Prism Live Studio first because both are free to start.

Keep OBS installed while you trial, since its free license makes it a safe fallback if a trial falls short.

Frequently asked questions

Why would anyone leave a free tool like OBS Studio?

Usually for workflow, not cost. OBS is free and open source, but you assemble scenes yourself. Browser studios, built-in multistreaming and production switchers each save time on a specific job.

Some teams also want a vendor behind the software.

Which OBS alternative is free?

Meld Studio and Prism Live Studio are described as free, XSplit Broadcaster has a free tier at $0, and Lightstream has a free tier with a logo on your stream. Larix Broadcaster is free for mobile. Always read the limits of each free level.

What runs on Mac?

OBS supports macOS 13.0 or newer. Ecamm Live is Mac-only, Wirecast runs on Mac and Windows, and Prism Live Studio supports Apple Silicon Macs. XSplit Broadcaster and vMix are Windows tools.

Is there a browser-based option?

Yes. StreamYard and Lightstream both run in the browser, so nothing needs installing. That helps on locked-down computers, though you give up the local scene control that OBS gives you.

Can I keep using OBS next to these tools?

Often, yes. Some teams use OBS as the encoder and a cloud tool for distribution. Check each vendor's documentation for supported inputs before you rely on that setup for a live event.
