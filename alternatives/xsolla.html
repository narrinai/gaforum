Xsolla Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

Xsolla calls itself a global video game commerce company, and its homepage lists Web Shop, Publishing Suite, Payments and Xsolla Pay. The About page claims 200+ geographies, 1000+ payment methods and 130+ currencies, and the homepage says studios can keep up to 95% of each sale in the Web Shop.

Pricing is not published; the pricing link goes to a contact form. That opacity, plus a narrower need, is why studios shop around for a merchant-of-record or web shop partner.

Eight options follow.

What pushes teams away from Xsolla

  • The pricing URL leads to a contact form, so a studio cannot read a rate card before it books a call.
  • The 95% share on web shop sales is a ceiling stated on the homepage, not a quote, which makes a head-to-head fee comparison hard.
  • A mobile-only publisher may not need a suite that spans publishing, payments and distribution.
  • Some studios want one vendor to be merchant of record for tax, others want to keep an existing payment provider and add a store.
  • One vendor on this list prints transaction rates, so teams that value upfront numbers have an option.

Which one fits

Sort the choice by who carries the tax and the checkout. If you need a vendor to be merchant of record for a mobile live-ops store, compare Appcharge, Stash and Aghanim.

If you sell a PC or community game with a store front, Tebex and FastSpring fit. Pick Coda when published rates matter most, and Nuvei when you only need payment infrastructure.

Ask every vendor for the fee at your expected monthly volume in writing, because most of these pages do not show one.

Frequently asked questions

What does a merchant of record do for a game studio?

A merchant of record is the seller of record for a transaction, so it handles payment, tax and compliance on the studio's behalf. Appcharge, Stash, Aghanim, Paddle and FastSpring each describe a merchant-of-record model on their own pages.

Which Xsolla alternative publishes its fees?

Coda Payments lists standard rates from 1% per transaction, plus 5% and a flat 15%, and offers custom pricing from $100K USD of monthly volume. FastSpring advertises one flat rate but its page text did not show the figure.

Can a small indie use these platforms?

Tebex shows an Open Account button, which suggests self-serve sign-up, and Aghanim describes no upfront costs. Others route you to sales. Confirm minimum volumes or fees before building a store around any of them.

Is Xsolla the only option for direct-to-consumer sales?

No. Appcharge, Stash and Aghanim all describe direct-to-consumer web stores for games, and Tebex covers community-style webstores. They differ in platform focus, mostly mobile, and in how they present pricing.

How should I compare payment-method counts?

Treat them as vendor claims. Xsolla says 1000+, Nuvei over 720, Appcharge 500+, Tebex 130+. Ask which methods are live in your top five player countries, since a headline total tells you little about local coverage.
