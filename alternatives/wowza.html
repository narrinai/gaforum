Wowza Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

Wowza makes video streaming server software, Wowza Streaming Engine, for live and on-demand video that can run on-prem, in the cloud, at the edge or air-gapped, and it also sells the Flowplayer OVP managed platform. Teams look elsewhere when they want an open-source engine, a managed video API or a pay-per-use price.

The ten picks split into self-hosted media servers, cloud encoding services and developer video APIs, each described from its own pages.

How this list is ordered: by our editorial judgment of fit for the reader named above, based on vendor documentation, pricing and license pages opened on the review date. Why teams look beyond Wowza

  • Wowza lists a trial and premium support plans on its pricing page but no price amounts in the pages we opened, so budgeting means a sales conversation.
  • Engineering teams that prefer to read and modify the code can choose open-source servers such as SRS and the community edition of OvenMediaEngine.
  • Teams that do not want to operate servers at all can use video APIs such as Mux or api.video that bill by usage.
  • Projects centered on WebRTC and sub-second latency may want engines built for that, such as Ant Media Server, Red5 or Ceeblue.
  • Companies already inside AWS may prefer to keep encoding and delivery under one cloud bill with MediaLive or IVS.

How to pick between them

Decide who runs the servers. If your team wants to, pick between open source (SRS, OvenMediaEngine, Red5's server) and supported licenses (Ant Media Server, Nimble, Flussonic).

If you do not, choose a managed API (Mux, api.video) or an AWS service. Write down your protocol list, target latency and peak concurrent viewers, then ask each vendor to price that exact load.

The numbers will say more than feature lists.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an open-source alternative to Wowza Streaming Engine?

SRS is an open source realtime video server with RTMP, WebRTC, HLS, SRT and more, and OvenMediaEngine and Red5 both offer open-source or community editions. Open source removes the license fee but moves support and upgrades to your team.

Which alternatives publish prices?

Ant Media Server lists Flex at $0.35 per instance-hour and a $129 monthly plan, Mux lists per-minute rates, api.video lists storage and delivery rates, and Ceeblue gives starting monthly prices for some services. Wowza, Flussonic and Unified Streaming point to sales or trials.

What if I do not want to manage servers?

Use a managed video API such as Mux or api.video, or an AWS service such as MediaLive, which encodes live video as a service. You trade fine control for fewer moving parts and for usage-based billing.

Can I keep Wowza for some streams and move others?

Yes. Many teams move one workload first, such as low-latency interactive streams, to an engine built for WebRTC, and leave on-demand delivery where it is. Treat it as a pilot with a clear success measure, such as glass-to-glass latency or cost per viewer hour.

Does Wowza still offer a free way to try it?

Its pricing page offers a Start a Trial option and lists premium support plans, and its site footer shows copyright through 2026. Check the pricing page for current trial terms before you plan a proof of concept.
