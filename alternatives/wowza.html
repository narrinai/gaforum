Wowza Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

Wowza makes video streaming server software, Wowza Streaming Engine, for live and on-demand video that can run on-prem, in the cloud, at the edge or air-gapped, and it also sells the Flowplayer OVP managed platform. Teams look elsewhere when they want an open-source engine, a managed video API or a pay-per-use price.

The ten picks split into self-hosted media servers, cloud encoding services and developer video APIs, each described from its own pages.

How this list is ordered: by our editorial judgment of fit for the reader named above, based on vendor documentation, pricing and license pages opened on the review date. Why teams look beyond Wowza

  • Wowza lists a trial and premium support plans on its pricing page but no price amounts in the pages we opened, so budgeting means a sales conversation.
  • Engineering teams that prefer to read and modify the code can choose open-source servers such as SRS and the community edition of OvenMediaEngine.
  • Teams that do not want to operate servers at all can use video APIs such as Mux or api.video that bill by usage.
  • Projects centered on WebRTC and sub-second latency may want engines built for that, such as Ant Media Server, Red5 or Ceeblue.
  • Companies already inside AWS may prefer to keep encoding and delivery under one cloud bill with MediaLive or IVS.

The alternatives

  1. 1

    Ant Media Server

    Self-hosted WebRTC streaming

    Free option
    Free trial

    Ant Media Server supports WebRTC, CMAF, HLS, RTMP, RTSP and SRT on your own infrastructure or any cloud. Published prices make it easy to compare with a Wowza quote, including an hourly Flex option for events.

    You gain a clear price and a WebRTC focus. You still run the servers yourself, as with Wowza, so operations work does not disappear.

    Red5

    Low-latency streams and SDKs

    Free option
    Free trial

    Red5 has an open-source media server and the licensed Red5 Pro, Red5 Cloud and SDK products, and its homepage advertises sub-250 millisecond latency. It is a natural comparison for teams who want to start with open source and move to a supported product.

    Compare the subscription tiers against your viewer counts, because they are sized for developers through enterprises.

    Nimble Streamer

    Lightweight, cost-focused servers

    Free option
    Unknown

    Nimble Streamer from Softvelum is a light-weight software media server for live and VOD that lists SRT, RTMP, WebRTC with WHIP ingest, NDI, SDI and Apple Low Latency HLS. Live transcoding is a premium add-on.

    It suits teams that want a small footprint on modest hardware. Contact the vendor for current license costs, since we could not confirm them.

    Checked on softvelum.com

  4. 4

    SRS

    Free open-source realtime server

    Free option
    Open source

    SRS, the Simple Realtime Server, is an open source video server supporting RTMP, WebRTC, HLS, HTTP-FLV, SRT and MPEG-DASH, with a Docker image and Kubernetes deployment. You pay no license fee, which matters for projects where per-instance fees would dominate.

    The trade is support: you rely on the community and your own engineers rather than a vendor contract.

    OvenMediaEngine

    Open source plus an enterprise edition

    Free option
    Unknown

    OvenMediaEngine comes from OvenMedia Labs in Seoul, formerly AirenSoft, and is offered as a community edition, an Enterprise edition and a hosted OvenMedia Cloud. The tiers let a team begin with the source edition and add vendor support later.

    Ask the vendor what separates community from Enterprise before you plan on it for a production launch.

    Flussonic

    IPTV, OTT and surveillance streaming

    Free option
    Unknown

    Flussonic builds streaming software for media server, transcoding, DVR, IPTV and OTT delivery, and video surveillance, with Flussonic Media Server as the base product. It fits operators with TV-style channels and recording needs.

    If your work is interactive game or event streaming, its center of gravity is elsewhere, so test latency requirements early.

    Unified Streaming

    Packaging for live and VOD

    Free option
    Unknown

    Unified Streaming sells software for live streaming, VOD packaging, live-to-VOD and audio streaming, and lists provenance through C2PA under its Trusted Media offering. It is a packager and origin layer, which makes it a component in a larger chain, not a full replacement for an engine that also ingests and transcodes.

    Teams with existing encoders and CDNs benefit most.

    AWS Elemental MediaLive

    Encoding inside AWS

    Free option
    Unknown

    AWS Elemental MediaLive is a live video encoding service for 24/7 linear channels and live events at scale. You hand over encoder operations to AWS, which helps a team with no appetite for servers.

    It handles encoding, so you add packaging, delivery and players around it. Cost modeling requires the AWS pricing pages rather than a single published rate.

    Mux

    Developer video API with usage pricing

    Free

    Mux is a video API platform that covers streaming, analytics, moderation and search, and says it is free to start with no credit card. Public per-minute rates let you model a month of live events precisely, a contrast with Wowza's quote-based route.

    The compromise is control: you consume an API rather than tuning your own servers.

How to pick between them

Decide who runs the servers. If your team wants to, pick between open source (SRS, OvenMediaEngine, Red5's server) and supported licenses (Ant Media Server, Nimble, Flussonic).

If you do not, choose a managed API (Mux, api.video) or an AWS service. Write down your protocol list, target latency and peak concurrent viewers, then ask each vendor to price that exact load.

The numbers will say more than feature lists.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an open-source alternative to Wowza Streaming Engine?

SRS is an open source realtime video server with RTMP, WebRTC, HLS, SRT and more, and OvenMediaEngine and Red5 both offer open-source or community editions. Open source removes the license fee but moves support and upgrades to your team.

Which alternatives publish prices?

Ant Media Server lists Flex at $0.35 per instance-hour and a $129 monthly plan, Mux lists per-minute rates, api.video lists storage and delivery rates, and Ceeblue gives starting monthly prices for some services. Wowza, Flussonic and Unified Streaming point to sales or trials.

What if I do not want to manage servers?

Use a managed video API such as Mux or api.video, or an AWS service such as MediaLive, which encodes live video as a service. You trade fine control for fewer moving parts and for usage-based billing.

Can I keep Wowza for some streams and move others?

Yes. Many teams move one workload first, such as low-latency interactive streams, to an engine built for WebRTC, and leave on-demand delivery where it is. Treat it as a pilot with a clear success measure, such as glass-to-glass latency or cost per viewer hour.

Does Wowza still offer a free way to try it?

Its pricing page offers a Start a Trial option and lists premium support plans, and its site footer shows copyright through 2026. Check the pricing page for current trial terms before you plan a proof of concept.
