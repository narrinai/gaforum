GameMaker Alternatives in 2026

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology

GameMaker is a 2D game engine that its vendor calls The Friendly Indie Game Engine, with a free tier, a Professional tier for commercial release and an Enterprise tier that adds console release. Developers who outgrow it usually hit one of three walls: how licenses are tiered, how the project is scripted, or where the game can ship.

The nine tools below answer those walls differently, and every claim comes from the vendor's own pages as read on 18 November 2026.

Why this order: we opened every vendor's own pages on the review date and placed the products by fit, not by popularity or payment. When to replace GameMaker

  • Commercial release needs Professional and console release needs Enterprise, and the homepage shows no prices for either, which makes budgeting harder.
  • You want an open-source or MIT-licensed engine, such as Godot, Solar2D or LOVE, so the tool cannot change terms under you.
  • You prefer a different language: Lua powers Solar2D and LOVE, and Defold and Godot bring their own scripting.
  • A no-code editor suits your team better, as GDevelop and Stencyl are built around.
  • You want the same project to grow into 3D, which Godot, Defold and Unity cover.
  • Your target is the browser or a phone, where a lighter framework or a mobile-leaning engine may fit more directly.

Our picks

  1. 1

    Godot

    Free open-source 2D with a 3D path

    Free option
    Open source

    Godot is a free, open-source 2D and 3D engine. For a GameMaker developer, the draw is a license question with a one-line answer: the homepage says free and open source and states no other pricing.

    The creators are Juan Linietsky and Ariel Manzur, and the Godot Foundation hosts the project. You gain 3D headroom and no tiers; you lose GameMaker's narrow 2D focus and its listed Enterprise console route.

    Checked on godotengine.org

    Visit Godot

  2. 2

    GDevelop

    Event-based, no-code 2D and 3D

    Free option
    Open source

    GDevelop calls itself a free, open-source, no-code engine for 2D and 3D games. Where GameMaker has tiers tied to release rights, GDevelop lists two paid plans, Silver and Gold, without figures in the page text.

    Exports include web, Android, Steam and iOS in one click. Good for creators who want to ship fast without writing code, less so for programmers who want full control of the engine.

    Checked on gdevelop.io

    Visit GDevelop

  3. 3

    Defold

    Cross-platform engine with no royalties

    Free option
    Free

    Defold is a free, all-in-one engine for cross-platform 2D and 3D games. The homepage says free forever, source available, no royalties and no runtime fee.

    Its target list includes Xbox, PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4, Switch, Steam, iOS, Android and HTML5, with Xbox support free for approved developers from Defold 1.14.0. A direct answer to GameMaker's console-tier question, though it asks you to learn a different workflow.

    Checked on defold.com

    Visit Defold

  4. 4

    Unity

    Commercial engine, 2D and 3D

    Free option
    Unknown

    Unity is a real-time 3D and 2D engine for developing, deploying and growing games. The homepage lists desktop, iOS, Android, Nintendo Switch, PlayStation, Xbox, Meta Quest, web and Apple Vision Pro.

    Compared with GameMaker, you gain a much wider platform list and an engine built for 3D as well. You give up simplicity, and you must read the separate plans page to learn what a paid plan costs.

    Checked on unity.com

    Visit Unity

  5. 5

    Cocos Creator

    2D and 3D with a mobile ecosystem

    Free option
    Unknown

    Cocos Creator is a cross-platform graphics engine and real-time 3D content creation platform for 2D and 3D games. The homepage claims it hosts over 70% of game content development and names ARM, Meta, Google and Microsoft among partners.

    It gives no price or license statement, so confirm terms before switching. It suits teams building for many devices who want a single editor.

    Checked on cocos.com

    Visit Cocos Creator

  6. 6

    Solar2D

    Lua-based open-source 2D

    Free option
    Open source

    Solar2D is a Lua-based, open source 2D engine focused on fast iteration, forked from the Corona SDK. The homepage states an MIT license with no hidden fees, charges or royalties.

    Build targets include iOS, tvOS, Android, Android TV, macOS, Windows, Linux and HTML5. A fit for developers who like a small scripting language and a quick edit-and-run loop; it has no console targets listed.

    Checked on solar2d.com

    Visit Solar2D

  7. 7

    LOVE

    Code-only 2D framework in Lua

    Free option
    Open source

    LOVE (LÖVE) is a free, open-source framework for making 2D games in Lua, licensed zlib/libpng. The homepage lists version 11.5 and builds for Windows, macOS, Linux, Android (APK) and iOS (source and libraries).

    It is a framework with no editor, so you trade GameMaker's visual room and object tools for code. Best for programmers who want the least between them and the game loop.

    Checked on love2d.org

    Visit LOVE

  8. 8

    Stencyl

    No-code creation for mobile, web, desktop

    Free option
    Unknown

    Stencyl is a no-code game creation tool for mobile, web and desktop. Its homepage lists iOS, Android, Windows, Mac, Linux and HTML5 export targets and claims 500,000 developers have adopted it.

    It links to a pricing section without stating any figures. A reasonable match for makers who like GameMaker's approachable style and want block-based logic instead of scripts.

    Checked on stencyl.com

    Visit Stencyl

  9. 9

    Buildbox

    Games without scripting

    Free option
    Unknown

    Buildbox describes itself on its games page as software that lets anyone create games regardless of technical skill, with no scripting, programming or software design experience. The page shows a Get Buildbox link but no price or free trial.

    It is the lightest-touch option here, and the one with the least public detail, so confirm terms and export targets before relying on it.

    Checked on buildbox.com

    Visit Buildbox

Making the call

Sort by what failed you in GameMaker. If it was cost predictability, look at Godot, Solar2D or LOVE, which publish open licenses.

If it was console release, read Defold's target list and Unity's. If it was scripting, choose by language: Lua for Solar2D or LOVE, no-code for GDevelop, Stencyl or Buildbox.

Then rebuild one small level, a menu and a save screen in the finalist to see how long the move really takes.

Frequently asked questions

Which GameMaker rival has no license tiers at all?

Godot calls itself free and open source, Solar2D and LOVE state permissive licenses, and Defold says free forever with no royalties or runtime fee. None of these ties release rights to a paid tier, according to their own homepages.

Do any of these export to consoles?

Defold lists Xbox, PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4 and Switch. Unity lists Switch, PlayStation and Xbox. GameMaker itself reserves console release for Enterprise.

Console programs have their own platform approvals, so treat an engine's target list as a start, not a guarantee.

I like GameMaker's ease. Which pick feels closest?

Stencyl and GDevelop aim at approachable, no-code creation, and GDevelop adds an open-source engine. Godot is friendlier than most code-heavy tools, though it uses its own concepts. Try a one-hour prototype in each rather than judging from screenshots.

Is Lua a good reason to move?

Solar2D and LOVE are both Lua-based, which suits people who prefer a compact scripting language. LOVE is a code-only framework, while Solar2D describes fast iteration. The deciding factor is usually your existing code and whether you want an editor.

Which options let me add 3D later?

Godot, Defold, GDevelop and Unity are described as handling 3D alongside 2D. Solar2D, LOVE and Stencyl are presented as 2D tools on their homepages, so they suit games that will stay flat.
