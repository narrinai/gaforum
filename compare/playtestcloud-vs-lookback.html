PlaytestCloud vs Lookback: Which Fits Your Team in 2026?

By GA Forum editorial team, edited by Sebastiaan SmitsLast reviewed November 18, 2026Methodology
vs

PlaytestCloud is a player insights platform built around recruiting, recording and analyzing playtests, while Lookback is an AI-assisted user research platform for moderated and unmoderated interviews and usability testing. One comes from games and supplies the players; the other is general research software where you bring your own participants or buy panel access.

This page compares methods, pricing visibility, platforms and trial terms from the vendors' own pages.

The short answer

Choose PlaytestCloud if you want a games-specific service that finds testers and returns recorded sessions with analysis, and you are fine requesting a price. Choose Lookback if you want published yearly plans, run interviews with your own audience and need research tooling that is not limited to games.

Tiny teams can try both, since each offers a free trial.

Pick PlaytestCloud if

Pick PlaytestCloud if you need players recruited for you, test on PC or mobile, and want recordings with transcripts, surveys and AI analysis handled in one service.

Pick Lookback if

Pick Lookback if you want a fixed annual price, plan to talk to your own players or customers live, and value a general research workflow across products.

Different starting points

PlaytestCloud calls itself a Player Insights Platform for recruiting, recording and analyzing playtests. The home page lists video, audio and touch recording, transcripts and surveys, and a Player Panel, so the service covers the steps from finding testers to reviewing footage.

It names customers and partners including Bethesda, Zynga, Supercell, Rovio, PlayStation, Kwalee and ZeptoLab, which are vendor claims about its audience.

Lookback describes itself as the AI-powered user research platform for interviews, usability testing and analysis. It is not game-specific.

You schedule or send sessions, watch them live or later, and use its tools to pull out findings. That makes it a fit for testing a launcher, a store page or a companion app along with a game.

Who supplies the testers

The biggest practical difference is participants. PlaytestCloud runs a Player Panel and says it can recruit, record and analyze playtests in about 48 hours, which removes a recruiting job that small studios often underestimate.

Lookback's plans include one panel participant on Freelance, and the page treats extra sessions as purchasable packages, from $29.90 per session on Freelance. Its moderated research lets you interview people you already have, such as beta signups or Discord members.

Decide whether recruiting is the part of testing you want to hand off.

Pricing you can see and pricing you cannot

Lookback publishes its prices. Freelance is $299 per year with 10 sessions and one panel participant.

Team is $1,782 per year with 100 sessions and 10 collaborators. Insights Hub is $4,122 per year with 300 sessions and 30 collaborators.

Enterprise is priced on request. Dividing the listed plans gives about $29.90 per session on Freelance, about $17.82 on Team and about $13.74 on Insights Hub.

PlaytestCloud shows no plan prices or per-tester rates on the home page, only that special pricing exists for small studios. A recruited-player service has costs per tester, so the figure will vary with audience and length.

Ask for a quote with your target platform and session count.

Trying before buying

Both offer a trial, in different currencies. PlaytestCloud's trial lets you watch two players play your game for up to 30 minutes.

That is a proof of output quality: you see what a recorded session looks like for your own build.

Lookback's trial gives 5 sessions free with 60 days of access. That is longer and gives you time to run moderated interviews and see how the analysis tools handle several sessions.

Plan the first test before starting either clock.

Where each belongs in a testing stack

On the playtesting platforms shortlist, PlaytestCloud sits at the games-first end and Lookback at the general-research end. PlaytestCloud's list of platforms covers PC through browser and Steam, plus Android and iOS; console is not explicitly listed, which matters if you are targeting those devices.

A reasonable pattern for a studio is to use a recruited-player service for early external feedback and a research tool for conversations with its own community. Whether you need both depends on how often you test, and annual cost against sessions should settle it.

Frequently asked questions

Does PlaytestCloud publish its prices?

No plan prices or per-tester rates appear on its home page. The page mentions special pricing for small studios and a free trial, so a quote is the way to get numbers.

How much is Lookback per year?

Lookback lists Freelance at $299 per year, Team at $1,782 per year and Insights Hub at $4,122 per year. Enterprise is priced on request. Extra session packages start from $29.90 per session on Freelance.

Which one finds players for me?

PlaytestCloud runs a Player Panel and says it recruits, records and analyzes playtests in about 48 hours. Lookback includes panel participants on its plans, but most of its use is interviewing people you bring.

What do the free trials include?

PlaytestCloud lets you watch two players play your game for up to 30 minutes. Lookback gives 5 sessions free for 60 days of access.

Can PlaytestCloud test console games?

Its home page lists PC through browser and Steam, plus Android and iOS. Console is not explicitly listed, so ask the vendor before planning a console test.
